## The problem this area exists to solve — **and WHY, in the owner's own words**

The platform issues the cleaner's invoice **for** the cleaner: on the document the cleaner is the
supplier (*Dodavatel*) and Cleansia the customer (*Odběratel*). That is self-billing, and self-billing
rests on the supplier having agreed to it **in advance**. T-0522 shipped the document. Nothing shipped
the agreement.

So today the platform generates documents whose legal basis is a contract clause the owner is *adding*,
with **no per-cleaner record that anyone agreed to anything**.

> **`Q-PAYOUT-02`, answered 2026-08-07 — a cleaner is an OSVČ and the platform self-bills. The owner's
> reason is OPERATIONAL, and it is the thing this topic could not previously say:**
>
> *"This is what I meant by self bill, instead of them making invoices on their own and sending them to
> us one by one, we have all of the invoices generated from their completed orders in place for all of
> the cleaners and pay for them in 1 day. So there is a need to have an agreement of self bill/paying."*

**Read the chain in that sentence, because the whole design inherits it.** The platform does not
self-bill because self-billing is tidier. It self-bills because **the payout is a single batch, on a
single day, for every cleaner at once** — and a batch cannot wait on N suppliers each raising and
sending their own invoice. In the EU a customer may issue the supplier's invoice **only where the
supplier agreed in advance**. So the agreement is **the precondition of the payout shape the business
runs on**, not compliance decoration attached to it. Three rules below stop being assertions and become
consequences:

- **the record is per-supplier, versioned and timestamped** — the batch pays *each* cleaner, so the
  authority to invoice *for* them is held per cleaner; a single contract clause (A6) cannot say which
  wording *this* supplier agreed to, when, and from where;
- **the gate is at approval, never at issuance** (rule 4, A5) — blocking issuance would break the
  one-day batch, which is the very thing the agreement exists to enable, in order to punish a cleaner
  for a gap the platform created;
- **coverage is derived, never stamped** (rule 5) — the batch has two writers today and will acquire
  more.

> ✅ **All three round-2 openings are closed, and the block got smaller.** **(1)** The forbidden
> lookup index is replaced by `(EmployeeId, Kind, RecordedAt DESC, Id DESC)` — reader-consumed indexes
> do not lead with `TenantId`. **(2)** `Sequence` is **dropped**; the tiebreak is the ULID primary key,
> and with it go the raw-SQL append, the `42P08` trap, the auto-commit, the arbiter index and the
> lost-append bug. **(3)** `OccurredAt` has its invariant, in two halves, enforced in the factories —
> self-service `== RecordedAt`, paper `<= RecordedAt` — plus a validator bound which **rev 4 changed**:
> the refusal is a **platform constant**, and `OccurredAt < employee.CreatedOn` is a **warning, not a
> refusal**, because that combination is the paper channel's *main line* (an operator countersigns on the
> spot; the cleaner registers later that week). **Net vs rev 2: one column, one unique index and one
> lookup index fewer.**
>
> ⚠️ **`TenantId` is stamped by the REPOSITORY, and `Append` is its SOLE WRITER.**
> `CleansiaDbContext.CommitAsync`'s stamping loop is `ChangeTracker.Entries<Auditable>()` (`:74`) with
> the `ITenantEntity` stamp **nested inside it** (`:89-92`), and this archetype is
> `BaseEntity + ITenantEntity`, **so it is never stamped by the pipeline** — the shipped archetype's own
> writer says exactly this and compensates (`DbContextAuditWriter.cs:10-12`, `:18`). Rev 2's D10 said the
> statement *"rides the filter"*; that is **false for the write side** and is disavowed. Unstated, every
> statement lands `TenantId = NULL`, becomes invisible to its own tenanted reader, and the mandatory gate
> refuses a cleaner who just accepted.
>
> **The third panel confirmed the finding and broke its REPAIR, and rev 4 fixed the repair.** *"The
> repository stamps it"* was a **convention on a method nobody was obliged to call**: `Add`
> (`IRepository.cs:37`) and `AddRange` (`:39`) sit on the same object, neither stamps, and the original
> check #7 was a happy-path round trip through `Append` that **stays green the day someone calls `Add`**.
> So: **`Add`/`AddRange` are FORBIDDEN for this entity** on the shipped §B10 allowlist model
> (`consistency.md:105-115`), and check #7 gains a **static sole-writer sweep** — the half that can go
> red. **The general defect is ROUTED, not absorbed** (ticket 14): `CommitAsync` fails to stamp *every*
> `BaseEntity + ITenantEntity` adopter, and the two stamps do not even agree —
> `string.IsNullOrEmpty(...)` at `CleansiaDbContext.cs:89` vs `??=` at `DbContextAuditWriter.cs:18`, so
> **an empty-string `TenantId` is replaced by one and survives the other.**

> ⚠️ **The index's COLUMN ORDER stands; its stated MECHANISM is withdrawn (round 3, ADR-0041 §X.2).**
> `(EmployeeId, Kind, RecordedAt DESC, Id DESC)` is the only order optimal in **both** tenancy modes and
> both challengers agree on it **from opposite mechanisms**. What is deleted is *"a btree cannot put an
> OR'd leading column into an `Index Cond`, and everything behind it becomes unreachable"* — that
> reproduces only under `plan_cache_mode = force_generic_plan`, and **production gets custom plans**
> (`Max Auto Prepare` is absent from `src/`; `DbContextBindingExtensions.cs:35`/`:68` build a plain
> `NpgsqlDataSource`, so statements are unnamed and re-planned with their values). **What a
> `TenantId`-leading reader index actually loses is the btree PATHKEY** — every `ORDER BY … LIMIT 1`
> behind it becomes a sort. The rule is therefore a **preference with a stated bet**, and it bites only
> on **ordered** reads.

### The composite-FK discriminator chain: flagged by the author, **built and measured by a specialist, and it works**

Rev 2 declined to assert EF feasibility and flagged it for the `db` lane instead. The round-2 challenger
built all three entities on EF Core 10 + Npgsql 10 and read the emitted DDL: both alternate keys and both
composite FKs emit, `citext` emits with the extension, `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` emits, and the mixed-nullability
FK models as an **optional relationship with `Kind` staying `NOT NULL`** — so D1.3's `MATCH SIMPLE` note is
right and the paper channel's NULL text id satisfies the constraint vacuously. **Record this as the
hedging discipline working**, on the one limb an architect had no business asserting. Four costs, all
acceptable, three of which become ADR text: two EF-generated FK-backing indexes the naming check cannot
reach; **63-byte identifier truncation** (PostgreSQL emits a `NOTICE`, not an error, and EF truncates
*differently*, so one index has three candidate names and a hand-copied `DROP INDEX` fails) — which makes
the naming check load-bearing, not cosmetic; **the statement must carry no navigation** to
`AgreementVersionText` (EF fixup nulls all FK properties on severance and `Kind` is a non-nullable enum),
which contradicts the CRC card and fixes it; and it is **first-of-class** — the schema today has **one**
`HasAlternateKey` (`LanguageEntityConfiguration.cs:18`) and **zero** composite FKs. That is a first *use*
of an ordinary construct, now built and run, so ADR-0033 does not fire and no catalog edit is created.

**Two questions, two orders — this is the thing rev 1 conflated:**

| Question | Who asks | Order | Whose artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supersession** — *what is the state now?* | the approval gate, the status endpoint | `RecordedAt DESC, Id DESC` | **ADR-0041** |
| **Coverage at T** — *was an agreement in force at T?* | the derivation, the report | `OccurredAt <= T`, then `OccurredAt DESC, RecordedAt DESC, Id DESC` | ADR-0041 fixes the **definition**; the **SEVERED** decision fixes the shape, the anchor and the plan check |

Using `OccurredAt` for supersession is a live defect: a paper signature **dated June**, filed in August
after a July revocation, would silently *resurrect* the agreement. Using `RecordedAt` for coverage is the
mirror defect: it would refuse to let a genuine, correctly-dated paper signature cover the invoice it
actually covered — which is the very repair `Q-SELFBILL-02` may require.

### The five rules that carry the design

1. **It is not a `ConsentType`.** A commercial contract term filed into the GDPR consent register would
   be withdrawable by its subject with a one-field POST on four hosts — and the existing table has no
   version and overwrites its own evidence on re-grant.
2. **The version is server-decided; the client echoes the exact served text row.** A mismatch is
   **rejected** (`agreement.version_stale`), never silently upgraded. An un-updated app must not be able
   to record agreement to text it did not show. The refusal is **legible**: both keys live under
   `api.agreement.*` **and** in `PARTNER_SURFACE_ERROR_KEYS`, because the parity spec only checks
   *array → backend*, so a key missing from the array renders as the generic bug message and the spec
   stays green.
3. **The text is server-owned and provenance-gated — and renderable ≠ demandable.** No `BusinessSupplied`+
   text for a country ⇒ nothing rendered, nothing demanded. A reviewed body in **some** language makes it
   *renderable*; only a reviewed body in the **caller's** language makes it *demandable*. Otherwise the
   expected launch case is a Ukrainian-speaking cleaner hard-blocked on affirming a Czech text — a
   SHA-256 proving *which bytes were displayed*, over bytes the signer cannot read.

   > **3a (rev 4) — AND THE GAP THAT FOLLOWS IS COUNTED, NOT HIDDEN.** Rule 3 is right and it has a
   > price: on a Czech-first launch, every cleaner whose language has no reviewed body is approved,
   > works, and is self-billed **with no statement** — and that population **grows with hiring**. The
   > owner ruled `Q-SELFBILL-06` **(b)**: open on Czech, **with visibility**. So the platform carries
   > **one `SELECT` over config** naming the uncovered `(serviced country × active language)` pairs plus
   > the count of approved cleaners in them, on the admin surface; and **one partner-home card** that
   > prompts each affected cleaner the day their text lands, off the supersession read the gate already
   > issues. **Neither blocks anybody**, and neither is rev 2's deleted interstitial — that counted a
   > historical backlog which does not exist; this measures a forward population the platform creates one
   > approval at a time. **Visibility is not consent**, and the only complete fix is the four
   > translations — option (a), which the owner declined for launch timing. That is a recorded,
   > deliberate, owner-taken risk with a named remedy.
4. **"Mandatory" binds at `ApproveEmployee`, and nowhere else.** Not `IsProfileComplete()`, not the job
   board, not any issuance path. It governs only the `Pending → Approved` edge, so **no already-approved
   cleaner is ever re-gated and deploy-day lockouts are zero**; it is host-agnostic, so it covers web and
   mobile with one rule; its jurisdiction term is `command.WorkCountryId`, a **scalar on the command**;
   and its release valve is the operator-recorded paper signature. Its cost is back-office friction, and
   that is named.
5. **Coverage of an issued invoice is DERIVED from the log, never stamped on the invoice.** A future
   third invoice writer is covered on the day it is written, with no reviewer and no compile break —
   which is the one axis rev 1 **empirically failed**. The **positive** specification of that query is
   severed; the **negative** (`EmployeeInvoice` gains no column) is ADR-0041's and is settled.

**And two rules about the running system rather than the design (rev 3's findings, rev 4's wording):**

6. **A reader-consumed index that serves an ORDERED read should not lead with `TenantId` — because
   `TenantId` is nullable and `null` is production**, not because we are multi-tenant. **The mechanism is
   the lost btree PATHKEY**, not an unreachable `Index Cond`: a leading `IS NULL` nulltest is indexable
   but yields no ordering, so every `ORDER BY … LIMIT 1` behind it becomes a sort. It is therefore a
   **preference with a stated bet** — we take the order that is optimal in *both* tenancy modes — and it
   does not reach unordered aggregates. *(The same "index's job" test `consistency.md` already uses for
   `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` decides column order; nobody had noticed it extends.)*
7. **A `BaseEntity + ITenantEntity` entity is not tenant-stamped by the commit pipeline; its repository
   must stamp it — and the stamping method must be its SOLE writer.** `CommitAsync`'s loop is over
   `Auditable`. This is a **property of the shipped archetype**, documented on `DbContextAuditWriter`,
   and it applies to every future adopter — not just to this one. **The obligation is not discharged by
   naming a stamping method**, because the inherited `Add`/`AddRange` sit beside it and do not stamp;
   it is discharged by an **allowlist plus a static check**. *(Routed for the general case as ticket
   14 — the two shipped stamps do not even agree on empty string.)*

**And one rule about how this topic verifies itself, which cost three panels to learn:**

8. **A check that cannot distinguish this design from the alternative the decision REJECTED is not a
   check.** Ask what each check does on the rejected alternative. On this topic, **four times**, the
   answer was *"it stays green"* — the plan check on a `TenantId`-leading index, the tenant round trip on
   an `Add`, the append-only check on an `ExecuteUpdateAsync`, the no-silent-success check on an
   idempotent no-op. **The question is cheap, it is mechanical, and it is the one this topic kept
   skipping.**

## "Append-only" — what is real, what is not, and what the real thing costs

The word is enforced by **nothing** today. The honest v1 property is:

> **Append-only by construction at the entity** — `sealed`, no public setters, two named factories and no
> other constructor — **with exactly one named mutator** (the GDPR redaction of `IpAddress`,
> `DeviceLabel`, `ContractReference`) — **on a repository base that still exposes `Remove` and
> `Deactivate`, on a database that enforces nothing, **and against a data-access layer on which a
> set-based `UPDATE` or `DELETE` is one call from any holder of the repository — against which the
> entity-level property is *vacuous*, because no entity is materialized.** *(The final clause is rev 3's,
> and it is the one rev 2 was missing.)*

Three checks make it checkable rather than documentary: a **reflection test** (sealed; no public setter
outside the forced/redaction allowlist; exactly two factories), a **property check on the emitted SQL**
(*no production code issues a DELETE or an UPDATE against this table other than the named redaction* —
**not** a list of four method names, which is what let the non-materializing path through), and a
**grep** (no read predicate references `IsActive` — an `IsActive` on an immutable row means nothing and
must never be read). **Rev 3 adds four more**, of which two pin properties nothing pinned before: the
tenant is actually stamped, and the append cannot return success without a row.

> ⚠️ **Round 2: the property is narrower than that, and the three checks name the WRONG BYPASS.** They
> enumerate the **entity-materializing** destructive methods and miss the **non-materializing** ones,
> which are precisely the ones an entity-level property cannot see. `IRepository` hands out **four**
> queryable-returning members — `GetFiltered` (`:33`), `GetAll` (`:35`), `GetQueryable` (`:49`),
> `GetQueryableIgnoringTenant` (`:59`) — and on any of them `ExecuteUpdateAsync` / `ExecuteDeleteAsync`
> is one call away. **This codebase uses them at 14 production sites in 7 files** (13 `ExecuteUpdateAsync`
> — `UserRepository.cs:164/186/206/222`, `RefreshTokenRepository.cs:102`, `PromoCodeRepository.cs:43/61`,
> `UserNotificationRepository.cs:45`, `MembershipBenefitUsageRepository.cs:159`,
> `DataRetentionBackgroundService.cs:79/85/129`, `DeactivateAdminUser.cs:71` — plus `ExecuteDeleteAsync`
> at `RefreshTokenRepository.cs:175`). **`ExecuteUpdateAsync` never materializes an entity, so the
> reflection test is VACUOUS against it**: it can set `Action`, `OccurredAt`, `BodyHash`, anything. And
> the design supplies the template a future author will copy — the GDPR redaction is a repository method
> that mutates existing statement rows, and `DataRetentionBackgroundService` is exactly how bulk
> redaction is written here today.
>
> **So the claim narrows again, and its tier is named:** append-only is a **reviewer-enforced convention
> at `T3-HUMAN`** — `T2-ADVISORY` if mechanized as a `check-consistency.mjs` rule, because that tool is in
> **zero** `.github/` workflows and can never set an exit code (`consistency.md` says so itself). It is
> **not a property of the system.** The check must state the property — *no production code issues a
> DELETE or an UPDATE against this table other than the named redaction* — rather than a method list.
>
> **This is derivation's conceded weakest joint, and the ruling is that it may still ship first.** Under
> stamping, an FK `Restrict` is a *database* enforcement of the anchor for referenced rows; derivation
> moves that anchor off the design and routes its replacement to a future ADR. That is acceptable for v1
> — the table is empty until `Q-SELFBILL-01` is answered, and the threat is an insider with `psql`, the
> same model `BodyHash` already answers with detection rather than prevention — **but it is a
> conditionality the design must state, and the dismissal *"a partial anchor is worthless"* is a
> non-sequitur that must be retracted.**

**Narrowing the repository interface is NOT available.** Every repository interface in this codebase
derives from `IRepository<T, string>` — including `IAdminActionAuditRepository`, the append-only audit
log a challenger held up as precedent. There is no precedent; creating one is a new canonical archetype
that puts existing code in violation, which **routes to its own ADR** under ADR-0033 and cannot ride in
as an ADR-0041 amendment.

**The two real mechanisms, priced and routed rather than hand-waved:** a `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE`
trigger (one `migrationBuilder.Sql` block; raw SQL in a migration is a shipped construct — but it is a
first-of-class mechanism and must be decided **across every append-only table at once**), or column-level
`REVOKE UPDATE/DELETE` from the application role (cheaper — **but it buys nothing if the app connects as
the table owner, which is unknown and is a factual question for the `db`/infra lane, not an assertion**).
**Neither in v1; the trigger ADR is routed as a ticket.** *This is the weakest joint in the design and
rev 2 says so.*

## Traps this area has already stepped on once

- **A gate that trips on everyone.** ADR-0034 §F2/§D7 caught a completeness term that would have 403'd
  every cleaner off the whole partner surface. This design adds a term **100%** of the population fails.
  Rule 4 is not a preference; it is the same defect refusing to recur — and rev 2 strengthens it by moving
  the gate to an edge that **no already-approved cleaner ever crosses again**.
- **A control wired to the writer that does not write.** Rev 1 put the stamp in `GenerateInvoice`'s
  handler; the monthly close builds the invoice **inline** and never calls it. Every ordinary invoice
  would have been unstamped forever, the report would have returned 100% of rows, and **the ADR's own
  verification step passed green on that implementation.** The compounding trap: `CreateFromOrderPays`
  **delegates to `Create`**, which is separately public — so "add a required parameter to the one factory
  I found" leaves a second construction path open.
- ~~**A tenant-less sweep reading a tenant-scoped row.**~~ ❌ **Written from a false premise; DELETED.**
  The sweep sets the override before issuing, and the queue path does the same. Prescribing
  `…IgnoringTenantAsync` there would **introduce** a cross-tenant read of a legal record on the money
  path. Recorded rather than quietly removed, because an accepted decision asserting that correct shipped
  code is broken teaches the next reader to "fix" it. **What survives: the pinning test seeds a non-null
  `TenantId`** — the test that would have caught the real trap below.
- **The real tenancy trap: a NULL-tenant config row is invisible to a tenanted caller, and the design
  reads that invisibility as consent-not-required.** A **query-filter** defect, not a unique-index one;
  invisible in single-tenant mode; fails in the **unsafe** direction. The fix is that the two config
  tables are not tenant-scoped at all — matching `CountryInvoiceConfig`, this design's own cited
  precedent.
- **An ordering column an operator can type.** The paper-signature channel supplies the *legal* date, so
  using it as the supersession key lets a backdated filing resurrect a revoked agreement. Two questions,
  two orders. **Round 2 closed the direction that was tested and found the mirror open:** the *coverage*
  axis still reads that operator-typed date, with **no stated bound**, on an **append-only, `init`-only**
  row — so one mistyped year retroactively covers every invoice a cleaner ever received, and the design
  **forbids the only mechanical correction** (appending a `Revoked` would assert an act the supplier
  never performed). The paper channel is also the only one with no `BodyHash`, so the tamper-detection
  row cannot see it.
- **⚠️ A `BaseEntity + ITenantEntity` row is NOT tenant-stamped by the commit pipeline.** *(Rev 3, and
  no panel has seen it.)* `CleansiaDbContext.CommitAsync`'s stamping loop is
  `ChangeTracker.Entries<Auditable>()` (`:74`); the stamp is at `:89-92`. The `AdminActionAudit`
  archetype — the one this design adopts precisely because it is a real append-only tenant-scoped log —
  is **not `Auditable`**, so its writer compensates by hand and **says so in a doc-comment**:
  `DbContextAuditWriter.cs:10-12` (*"…`CommitAsync` does not stamp its `TenantId` (its loop is over
  `Auditable` only); this writer stamps it here"*), `:18`
  (`entry.TenantId ??= tenantProvider.GetCurrentTenantId();`). **Adopt the archetype and you adopt the
  obligation.** Missed, every row lands `TenantId = NULL` and becomes **invisible to its own tenanted
  reader** — the same query-filter defect as the config-table trap above, relocated onto the legal
  record, where its symptom is *the mandatory gate refusing a cleaner who just complied*. **The
  compensation lives in a different project from the entity, which is how it stayed invisible across
  three panels.** The pin is a **real-PostgreSQL round trip under a non-null tenant** — a `tenantId:
  null` fixture passes while production is broken.
  > ⚠️ **And the OBVIOUS REPAIR is not enough — this is the trap's second half, found in round 3.**
  > *"The repository stamps it"* is a **convention on a method nobody is obliged to call**: the inherited
  > `Add` (`IRepository.cs:37`) and `AddRange` (`:39`) sit on the **same object**, neither stamps, and a
  > happy-path round trip through the stamping method **stays green the day someone calls `Add`**. So the
  > repair is an **allowlist plus a static sole-writer check** (`consistency.md` §B10 idiom,
  > `:105-115`), not a doc-comment. **Narrowing the interface is not available** — that is a new
  > canonical archetype and routes to its own ADR under ADR-0033.
  > ⚠️ **The two shipped stamps do not agree, and nobody has decided which is right:** the pipeline
  > stamps when `string.IsNullOrEmpty(tenantEntity.TenantId)` (`CleansiaDbContext.cs:89`); the
  > archetype's writer uses `entry.TenantId ??= …` (`DbContextAuditWriter.cs:18`). **An empty-string
  > `TenantId` is replaced by one and survives the other.** Routed to the `db`/infra lane as ADR-0041
  > ticket 14, decided **across every future adopter** rather than per repository.
- **A tenant-scoped index is not one thing — and the trap has a SECOND layer: the first stated mechanism
  for it was wrong.** An index consumed by the **writer** may lead with `TenantId` — uniqueness is
  evaluated by the index itself, so a tenant-scoped unique arbiter with `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` is correct.
  An index consumed by the **reader**, **serving an ordered read**, should not. `consistency.md` already
  decides `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` by *"the index's JOB"*; **the same job test decides column order**, and
  nobody had noticed it extends.
  > ❌ **The mechanism round 2 measured and round 3 falsified — recorded because getting this wrong twice
  > is the actual lesson.** *"A btree cannot put an OR'd leading column into an `Index Cond`, so every
  > column behind it becomes unreachable"* reproduces **only under `plan_cache_mode = force_generic_plan`**.
  > Production gets **custom** plans: `Max Auto Prepare` appears **nowhere** in `src/`, and
  > `DbContextBindingExtensions.cs:35`/`:68` build a plain `NpgsqlDataSource` with no preparation
  > settings, so statements are unnamed and re-planned with their values — the filter's booleans fold and
  > the leading `IS NULL` stays indexable.
  > ✅ **What is actually lost is the btree PATHKEY:** a leading `IS NULL` yields no *ordering*, so every
  > `ORDER BY … LIMIT 1` behind it becomes a sort of the whole matched group. **The rule survives as a
  > preference with a stated bet** — the equality-leading order is optimal in *both* tenancy modes — and
  > **its scope narrows with its mechanism: it does not reach unordered aggregates.**
  > ⚠️ **The shipped schema already does this — in ONE place, not three.** Corrected in rev 4:
  > `IX_AdminActionAudits_TenantId_OccurredOn` (`AdminActionAuditConfiguration.cs:65-67`, declared
  > `.IsDescending(false, true)`) **is** in violation, because it exists to serve an ordered read.
  > `IX_MembershipBenefitUsages_Quota` (`MembershipBenefitUsageEntityConfiguration.cs:70-71`) is **not** —
  > its own comment at `:68-69` says it *"serves the remaining-count read"*, and a `COUNT` has no
  > `ORDER BY`, so there is no pathkey to lose. `AuditableEntityConfiguration.Configure`
  > (`EntityConfiguration.cs:31`) is **not** — nothing is behind the leading column.
  > **This is not called a bug**: it is a bet on a multi-tenant future that has not arrived, correct the
  > moment `TenantId` is non-null. **But one instance is enough to fire ADR-0033 routing test 1 (it puts
  > existing code in violation), so the catalog version needs an Architect-ratified edit plus a
  > canonicalization decision — it may not be written inline, and it may not be written as a bare
  > prohibition.** Filed for the catalog lane as ADR-0041 ticket 13, **with the corrected evidence and the
  > plan mode named** — *a law whose stated reason a `db` agent can falsify in ten minutes is how a
  > confidently-wrong comment stops the next reviewer from checking.*
- **The append that refused a true statement.** *(Round 2, measured. **Fixed in rev 3 by removing the
  column, not the mechanism.**)* `MAX+1` + `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING RETURNING` returned **zero rows**,
  raised nothing, and the `Revoked` was **not in the log** — falsifying the design's own sentence *"the
  append never refuses a true statement."* The precedent it copied
  (`MembershipBenefitUsageRepository.TryReserveSlotAsync`) has **no retry** (`:109-111`), and there zero
  rows legitimately means *"the quota is exhausted"*; here it has exactly one cause, a lost race.
  **The general lesson: importing a concurrency shape without importing the semantics of its failure
  return is worse than not importing it. `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` + a zero-row return mapped to caller
  success is the forbidden form.** The design's own hedge said *"I would rather be attacked for
  over-machinery than for a probability argument"* — and the over-machinery is what lost the data.
  **Rev 3's resolution is the generalizable part: `Sequence` is dropped, so the append is an ordinary
  `Add` riding the unit of work, and the forbidden shape is not merely unused but *unreachable*.** Only
  the **two-column total order** transferred from `OrderStatusTrack`; the column did not, because its
  enabling condition (*"the order is the consistency boundary"*, `Order.cs:447-448` assigns the ordinal
  in memory inside the loaded aggregate) is absent here. **A ULID primary key was already a total order.**
- **`TenantId` is nullable and `null` is production, so a tenanted test and an untenanted test exercise
  DIFFERENT CODE.** Four facets now, on three artifacts, and neither test is the other's superset:
  **visibility** (a NULL-tenant config row is invisible to a tenanted caller — fires only when tenanted,
  covered by no rule), **enforcement** (a `(TenantId, …)` UNIQUE index enforces nothing at NULL — fires
  only untenanted), **orderability** (the lost pathkey above — untenanted only), and **parameter typing**
  (`42P08` on a bare untyped `@tenantId`, which `MembershipBenefitUsageRepository.cs:95-100` records in a
  comment as *"why the promo path shipped this bug past a tenanted test run"*). **Routed to the catalog
  lane as a missing rule** — it needs a named enforcer that can fail a build, and it must be filed under
  *"`TenantId` is nullable"*, not under *"multi-tenancy"*, or nobody reads it while single-tenant.
- **A mandatory gate that passes on invalid input.** The approval gate's jurisdiction term is
  `command.WorkCountryId`, and FluentValidation's class-level cascade is `Continue`, so the new rule runs
  regardless of the country chain's verdict (`ApproveEmployee.cs:56-62`). A blank or unserviced country
  resolves to *"no version for this country"*, which the fail-open rule reports as *"the gate does not
  fire"* — so **the mandatory gate silently passes on exactly the inputs that are invalid.** That is the
  safety-valve-as-disguise defect recurring. **Rev 3's containment is at the resolver's boundary:** it
  returns **three** values — `Renderable` · `NoVersionForCountry` · `NotAJurisdiction` — and the third is
  an error, never a fail-open. *(The validator's chain ordering is the backend lane's; the resolver not
  being the thing that swallows it is the ADR's.)*
- **A navigation-walking erasure.** `GdprDeletionService` loads `Employee → Address` and nothing else,
  and there is **no lazy loading** — so a clear reaching through a navigation is a silent no-op that
  returns success. Redaction is an **id-keyed** repository call.
- **A jurisdiction term that depends on query shape.** `Employee.Address` is a navigation and the include
  lists disagree. Rev 2 dissolves it: the resolver takes a **country id parameter**, and both callers
  already hold a scalar.
- **`ContractStatus` is a false friend.** The admin approval state wearing a legal name. Note rev 2's gate
  sits *inside* `ApproveEmployee` — a precondition on the **transition**, which is the opposite of reading
  the state as evidence.
- **A fixture that supplies an input production never produces.** T-0522 hit it twice on this document.
  The erasure test and the tenancy test both fail that way by default — hand-populated navigations and
  null-tenant fixtures pass green while production is broken.
- **An error key under `errors.*`.** On partner web the shared interceptor reads `api.*` only, and the
  parity spec asserts *array → backend*, never *backend → array* — so a new key that never enters
  `PARTNER_SURFACE_ERROR_KEYS` renders as *"An error occurred. Please try again."* with the spec green.

## Where the seams are

- **Per country** — one `AgreementVersion` row. Adding SK, DE or PL costs **zero migrations and zero
  code**; no handler branches on a country code (ADR-0017).
- **Per agreement kind** — one enum member, propagated down the chain and pinned by composite FKs. The
  signed B2B contract, a DPA, a code of conduct are the same shape. *(Deliberate contrast with ADR-0034
  D1: there cardinality was pinned at one; here many statements per (employee, kind) **is** the feature.)*
- **Per audience** — self-service endpoints on **partner web + partner mobile**; the record-on-behalf
  command on **admin**. Customer hosts untouched. No host learns about another.
- **Per client** — only the **body** comes from the server; heading, checkbox label, "read full text",
  the fallback-language notice and the error strings stay in the client i18n catalogs where the
  `ConsentCatalog` guards live.
- **Per invoice writer** — **zero.** Nothing in this design touches `EmployeeInvoice`,
  `GenerateInvoice` or `PayPeriodBackgroundService`, and a future third writer inherits coverage for
  free. That is the seam the derivation buys, and it is the one rev 1 broke.
