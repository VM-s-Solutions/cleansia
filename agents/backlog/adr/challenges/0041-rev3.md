## CH-R3-1 — §Owner rulings 1's *"the cohort cannot form"* is falsified by rev 3's own D4.3 and its own check #12. The deletion removed the instrument along with a **different** subject. **BLOCKING**

**The deletion.** §Owner rulings 1 deletes F3, F4, D5's already-approved row, the interstitial, D6.4's
keep-issuing default and the report row **`cleaners with no current acceptance`**, on one structural
reason, stated twice:

> *"With D5's gate at `ApproveEmployee` **from day one**, no cleaner reaches work in a jurisdiction with
> demandable text without a statement — so the cohort **cannot form**."* (§Owner rulings 1)

> *"The gate IS the control. Every cleaner approved for a jurisdiction with demandable text has a
> statement, because `ApproveEmployee` refuses otherwise. The residue — a jurisdiction opened before
> its text lands — is closed by **D4.5's launch precondition**."* (D5 §P22)

**The refutation is three sections away, in rev 3's own text.** D4.3 — sustained from CH-L4, correct,
and one of the better decisions in this ADR — makes *demandable* a property of the **caller's
language**, not of the jurisdiction:

> **Demandable**: the **caller's own language** has a `BusinessSupplied`+ text for that version. Only
> then may D5's gate fire. … A cleaner without a reviewed body in their language gets the **prompt**,
> never the block. (D4.3)

And rev 3's own **check #12** pins the consequence as an acceptance criterion:

> A test with a version whose only `BusinessSupplied` text is `cs` asserts, for a `uk` caller:
> `renderable: true`, `demandable: false`, and that **`ApproveEmployee` succeeds** for that cleaner.

And **D4.7** names it and calls it acceptable:

> Until then a version ships with the languages the owner supplied and the rest fall back **visibly** —
> and, per D4.3, **unenforcedly**.

**So the population is:** *every cleaner whose preferred language has no `BusinessSupplied`+ text for
their work country, in a jurisdiction that fully satisfies D4.5's launch precondition.* They are
approved, they work, they accrue pay, they are issued self-billed invoices, and **they have no
statement**. `ApproveEmployee` does not refuse them — check #12 asserts that it must not.

**This is not F3′ and must not be filed under it.** F3′ is *"a country we chose to open early"*, and
rev 3 closes it with D4.5 — *"a jurisdiction is not opened to cleaner registration before it has a
`BusinessSupplied`+ text"*, **singular**, i.e. *renderable*. D4.5 is satisfied by **one** reviewed
language. The residue I am naming survives D4.5 completely: the country is open, the text exists, the
precondition is met, and the cleaner still lands un-stated.

**Its size, on the owner's own expected launch case.** D4.3's worked example is not hypothetical — it
is the launch case the ADR itself predicts: *"the expected launch case — the owner delivers reviewed
**Czech** first"*. This platform ships **five** partner locales (`cs`, `sk`, `uk`, `ru`, `en`) precisely
because its cleaners are not all Czech readers; `consistency.md` §E8 and the `.xcstrings`/i18n parity
machinery exist for that reason. So on day one the un-stated cohort is *"every non-Czech-reading
cleaner"*, and it **grows with hiring**, exactly as deleted-F3 described — the growth mechanism the
owner ruling was read as removing.

**Why the deletion is the harm, not the population.** Rev 3 is right that an empty database plus a
day-one gate removes the *historical* cohort. What it removed *with* it was the only instrument that
could see this one:

| Deleted in rev 3 | Would it have covered the language residue? |
|---|---|
| report row `cleaners with no current acceptance` (D6.3) | **Yes, directly** — it counts cleaners whose latest statement is absent, whatever the reason |
| the prompt / interstitial on the partner home surface (D5) | **Yes** — it is the surface that asks a cleaner who was never asked |
| D6.4's keep-issuing default | **Yes** — it governed *"a supplier with no self-billing agreement of any kind"*, which is exactly this supplier |
| `Q-SELFBILL-02`'s count | **Yes** — it is the number that would have made the residue visible to the owner |

**And rev 3's surviving surface has no row for them.** The D5 table's one surviving prompt row is *"the
cleaner whose accepted text is superseded"* — explicitly *"a cleaner who **did** comply"*. There is no
gate, no prompt, no count and no report row for a cleaner who was never asked because the platform had
no text in their language. They are invisible by construction, and D4.4's fail-open safety valve
reports the invisibility as *"nothing is demandable"* — which is round 1's CH-S4 pattern for the third
time in this document: **the safety valve disguising the failure.**

**What I am NOT arguing.** I am not arguing for hard-blocking a Ukrainian-speaking cleaner on Czech
text — D4.3 is right and I could not break it (§Found sound). I am not arguing to restore F3/F4; the
owner ruling is correct about the historical cohort. I am arguing that **the ruling's stated structural
reason does not reach the population D4.3 creates**, and that rev 3 deleted the compensating control on
that reason.

**What I want changed** (a property, not a repair — three shapes are available and the choice is the
author's):

- **either** D4.5's launch precondition is stated per **language** — *a jurisdiction is not opened to
  cleaner registration before it has a `BusinessSupplied`+ text in every locale the partner clients
  ship* — which closes it by sequencing at the cost the owner already pays for i18n parity, and keeps
  D4.3 untouched (it is a precondition on **opening a country**, not a block on a cleaner);
- **or** the report row / the prompt returns for **this** population with its subject restated (it is
  not *"people we failed to ask"* either — it is *"people we could not lawfully ask in a language they
  read"*);
- **or** the ADR states plainly, in §Owner rulings 1 and in D5, that *the cohort **can** form, by design,
  wherever a locale lacks reviewed text; that this is accepted; and that nothing in the platform counts
  it* — which is the honest minimum and is a materially different decision from the one on the page.

Whichever is chosen, **§Owner rulings 1's sentence must stop claiming the cohort cannot form**, and the
`Q-SELFBILL-02` narrowing that drops the count must be re-examined, because the count now has a live
subject that is not the F3′ residue.

*Blocking?* **Yes.** It is a deletion executed on a false premise, it removes the only visibility into a
population the design creates on purpose, and — per §W.12's own rule — the sentence asserting the
property has no check while the check that exists (#12) asserts the opposite.

## CH-R3-2 — D1.9's stated mechanism is **false as EF, Npgsql and PostgreSQL actually execute it**. Measured. The conclusion survives; the reason, the magnitude and ticket 13's evidence do not. **BLOCKING**

**The claim.** D1.9, stated as the rule and inherited from §W.2:

> every read of an `ITenantEntity` carries `CleansiaDbContext.cs:229-264`'s three-armed `OR`, **a btree
> cannot put an OR'd predicate on its leading column into an `Index Cond`**, PostgreSQL 16 has no skip
> scan, **and every column behind the leading one becomes unreachable with it**. So a reader-consumed
> index leads with the query's sargable **equality** terms…

Round 2 measured a demotion and the lead generalised it. **Nobody asked what plan Npgsql actually
gets** — the round-2 challenger said so itself: *"the plan **depends on whether EF's two filter
booleans arrive as literals or parameters**, and EF emits parameters. Nobody has pinned which plan
production gets."* Its inference from *"EF emits parameters"* to *"the OR is not folded"* skips a step,
and the step is decisive.

**Measured — rev 2's forbidden index, `(TenantId, EmployeeId, Kind, RecordedAt DESC, Id DESC)`, sole
index on the table, EF's captured supersession statement with its own bound parameters, single-tenant
(`TenantId` NULL — production today):**

```
-- captured statement (EF Core 10.0.0):
WHERE (@ef_filter__p3 OR (@ef_filter__p1 AND e."TenantId" IS NULL) OR e."TenantId" IS NULL)
  AND e."EmployeeId" = '…' AND e."Kind" = 1
ORDER BY e."RecordedAt" DESC, e."Id" DESC LIMIT LEAST(@p, 1)
-- params: @ef_filter__p3=False, @ef_filter__p1=True, @p=1

Sort  (Sort Key: e."RecordedAt" DESC, e."Id" DESC)   Sort Method: top-N heapsort
  ->  Bitmap Index Scan on "IX_EAS_Supersession_TenantLeading"
        Index Cond: ((e."TenantId" IS NULL) AND ((e."EmployeeId")::text = '…') AND (e."Kind" = 1))
```

**All three terms are in the `Index Cond`. The leading column is not demoted and nothing behind it is
unreachable.** With `enable_bitmapscan=off, enable_seqscan=off` the same three-term `Index Cond`
appears under an ordinary `Index Scan`. And **tenanted**:

```
Index Scan using "IX_EAS_Supersession_TenantLeading"
  Index Cond: ((e."TenantId" = 'T1') AND (e."EmployeeId" = '…') AND (e."Kind" = 1))     -- and NO Sort
```

**The mechanism is constant folding, and it is a property of the plan mode.** PostgreSQL plans the
*unnamed* extended-protocol statement with the bound parameter values substituted (a **custom plan**),
so `@ef_filter__p3=false` and `@ef_filter__p1=true` fold and the three-armed `OR` collapses to the
single arm `TenantId IS NULL` — which is an **indexable btree qual** (nulls are stored in btree
indexes). I pinned the boundary condition rather than assume it:

```
SET plan_cache_mode = force_generic_plan;  PREPARE … EXECUTE p1(false, true);
->  Parallel Seq Scan on "EmployeeAgreementStatements"
      Filter: (($1 OR ($2 AND ("TenantId" IS NULL)) OR ("TenantId" IS NULL)) AND …)
      Rows Removed by Filter: 66665
```

So the demotion round 2 measured is a **generic-plan** phenomenon. **Production does not get generic
plans:** `Max Auto Prepare` appears **nowhere** in `src/` (`grep` over the whole tree returns zero
hits), `DbContextBindingExtensions.cs:68` builds a plain `NpgsqlDataSource` with no preparation
settings, and Npgsql's default is auto-prepare **disabled** — so every statement is unnamed and
re-planned with its values.

**What a `TenantId`-leading reader index actually costs, measured on the coverage predicate** (the one
round 2 measured), same data, same seed, EF's captured statement:

| Coverage index | `Index Cond` | Extra node | Buffers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `(EmployeeId, Kind, OccurredAt DESC, RecordedAt DESC, Id DESC)` | `EmployeeId`, `Kind`, `OccurredAt` — 3 terms | — | **373 459** |
| `(TenantId, EmployeeId, Kind, OccurredAt DESC, RecordedAt DESC, Id DESC)` | `TenantId IS NULL`, `EmployeeId`, `Kind`, `OccurredAt` — **4 terms** | **`Sort` (top-N heapsort) inside each SubPlan** | **656 839** |

**The cost is 1.76×, and its cause is the lost *pathkey*, not the lost `Index Cond`.** A leading
`IS NULL` nulltest is indexable but does not yield btree ordering, so every `ORDER BY … LIMIT 1` behind
it becomes a sort of the whole matched group instead of a one-row ordered probe. In **tenanted** mode
the leading term is an equality, pathkeys survive, and the `TenantId`-leading index is **strictly
better** than rev 3's (measured above: ordered `Index Scan`, no `Sort`, no residual filter).

**Three consequences, and I am deliberate about which I am and am not claiming.**

1. **D1.9's conclusion survives and I am not asking for the schema block to change.** Leading with
   `EmployeeId` is the right call: it is the only column order that is optimal in *both* tenancy modes,
   and rev 3's index measured clean in both (§Found sound). What must change is the **sentence**, which
   asserts a mechanism that does not occur, on the exact joint the ADR is being ratified on. Under
   §W.12's own rule — *"a sentence that asserts a property of the running system is either accompanied
   by the check that fails when it stops being true, or it is deleted"* — this is the **fourth**
   instance of the pattern, and the first one rev 3 authored.
2. **The magnitude claim inverts the argument's weight.** *"Unseekable by construction"* justifies a
   prohibition; *"1.76× and a top-N sort in single-tenant mode, strictly better once a tenant exists"*
   justifies a **preference with a stated bet**. That is still a decision, and it is a smaller one.
3. **Ticket 13's routing evidence is wrong for at least one of its three rows.** D1.9 hands the catalog
   lane *"the rule as a bare law would put shipped indexes in violation"*:
   - `IX_MembershipBenefitUsages_Quota` (`:70-71`) serves a **`COUNT`** — *"the remaining-count read"*,
     per its own comment — with **no `ORDER BY`**. It loses no pathkey, seeks on all four terms, and is
     **not in violation under the corrected mechanism**. Rev 3's *"today they cost storage and write
     amplification and buy nothing"* is wrong about it in the favourable direction: it works.
   - `IX_AdminActionAudits_TenantId_OccurredOn` (`:65-67`) **is** the affected shape — leading
     `TenantId` with an `OccurredOn` ordering behind it — so it does lose its pathkey in single-tenant
     mode. Test 1 still fires, on **one** row rather than three.
   - `EntityConfiguration.cs:31`'s single-column `TenantId` index has nothing behind it and is
     unaffected either way; it is dead weight for an independent reason.

   The corrected evidence should travel to ticket 13 **with the plan mode named**, or the catalog lane
   will write a law whose stated reason a `db` agent can falsify in ten minutes — which is how a
   confidently-wrong comment stops the next reviewer checking (`consistency.md`
   §*"Tenant-scoped unique indexes"*, last paragraph).

*Blocking?* **Yes**, on the sentence and on the routed evidence, **not** on the index. It is
pre-ratification because acceptance freezes the rule as stated, and D1.9 is stated **as the rule**.

## CH-R3-3 — Check #21, the check that *"makes D1.9 a decision rather than a preference"*, **passes green on the forbidden index**. Measured. **BLOCKING**

**The check.** Check #21 asserts that the `Index Cond:` of the node naming
`IX_EmployeeAgreementStatements_Supersession` **carries `EmployeeId` and `Kind`**, over the captured
statement the approval gate emits. P7 required exactly this and forbade discharging the choice *"by an
index declaration"*.

**Measured, both index shapes, same captured statement, same parameters:**

| Index behind the name | `Index Cond` | Check #21 verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `(EmployeeId, Kind, RecordedAt DESC, Id DESC)` — rev 3's | `EmployeeId`, `Kind` | **passes** |
| `(TenantId, EmployeeId, Kind, RecordedAt DESC, Id DESC)` — **the forbidden shape** | `TenantId IS NULL`, **`EmployeeId`, `Kind`** | **passes** |

An `Index Cond` that carries `TenantId IS NULL AND EmployeeId = … AND Kind = 1` **contains `EmployeeId`
and `Kind`**. A future author who re-orders the index to lead with `TenantId` — the thing D1.9 exists to
prevent, and the thing rev 2 actually did — keeps the `HasDatabaseName`, keeps the check green, and
introduces the `Sort`. **The only thing that would fail is the index's name**, which is P7's forbidden
discharge wearing an `EXPLAIN`.

This is the same vacuity class the ADR has now diagnosed twice in others' work: check #2 is *"vacuous
against a non-materializing `UPDATE`"* (D11/P14, correct), check #5 *"asserts classification… and would
pass green at 90 seconds"* (§W.8, correct). Check #21 is rev 3's own, is listed first among *"the four
checks a challenger should test for vacuity"*, and is the third.

**What I want changed** (a property): the assertion is the one that **changes between the two shapes**.
Two are available and both are measurable on the captured statement:

- **no `Sort` node above the index scan** — i.e. *the ordering is served by the index*, which is the
  property that is actually lost; **and/or**
- **the `Index Cond` of that node contains no `TenantId` term** — the direct negation of the rule.

Assert the selectivity too, per T-0540: my seed is 200 000 statements over 50 000 employees, which
makes a seq scan the wrong plan; on rev 3's stated seed (*"thousands of rows across many employees"*)
that is satisfied, and the ADR should say the number as an assertion rather than as prose.

*Blocking?* **Yes.** D1.9's own text says check #21 *"is what makes D1.9 a decision rather than a
preference"*. Measured, it does not.

## CH-R3-4 — **"109×" is a seeding artefact**; the measured work ratio on a production-shaped seed is **1.94× in buffers** — and the one-scan-per-row shape is expressible **inside EF's expression tree**, so *"derive, but not through the expression tree"* answers a question that does not arise. **BLOCKING (on the sentence, not on D6)**

**Credit and scope first.** CH-S2-1's **mechanism** reproduces exactly and independently: written as
LINQ, EF Core 10 emits D6.1's correlated top-1 lookup **twice per invoice row**, because `covered` is a
two-part boolean. My captured statement is character-for-character the shape round 2 published, on my
own model:

```
SELECT e."Id", ( SELECT e0."Action" … ORDER BY … LIMIT 1) = 1
             AND ( SELECT e0."Action" … the same predicate again … LIMIT 1) IS NOT NULL AS "Covered"
```
```
Seq Scan on "EmployeeInvoices"   SubPlan 1 (48 000 loops) + SubPlan 2 (45 180 loops), no Memoize
```

That finding is **sustained**, and the round-2 challenger deserves the credit for it. What does not
survive is the number rev 3 carried into the record.

**Measured, same container, same data, same index, three runs each. `Buffers: shared hit` is invariant
across runs and is the metric; wall clock is the range.**

| Shape | Scan nodes / row | Buffers | Wall clock |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EF naive** (D6.1 written as LINQ) | **2** | **373 459** | 1.49 – 2.94 s |
| Hand-written `LEFT JOIN LATERAL` | 1 | 192 739 | 0.48 – 0.87 s |
| **EF `SelectMany` + `Take(1).DefaultIfEmpty()`** | **1** | **192 739** | 0.63 – 0.87 s |

**Work ratio: 1.94×, not 109×.** My seed is the one T-0540 and §W.10 P3 demand — **48 000 invoices with
distinct `GeneratedAt`**, so `Memoize`'s hit rate is 0 % **for both shapes**. That is exactly the point:
round 2's 109× came from comparing an un-memoizable `SubPlan` against a `Memoize`d nested loop that its
own seed had flattered (75 % hits on 12 distinct keys), and it **disclosed the artefact honestly** —
*"my 827 ms is optimistic, by a lot… the hit rate is 0 %"*. Remove the artefact from the **baseline**
and the ratio collapses to the mechanism's own prediction: two subqueries do twice the inner work.
Round 2's own diagnosis said so — *"The index is not the problem… the problem is the outer loop
cardinality **× 2**"* — and the × 2 is what I measure.

**Rev 3 does not inherit it loosely — it inherits it precisely, three times, and that is the problem.**
D6.2 test 5's retraction states **"90 411 ms vs 827 ms … (109×)"**; the severed spec §S2 restates it as
the measurement *"that forced this"*; the living doc restates it again. R.3 hedge 1 is careful about
attribution (*"where I cite a ratio I attribute it"*) and the round-2 lead ruled correctly that a lead
rules *"on the obligation it invokes, not on the digit"*. **But the digit then became load-bearing
prose in three artifacts**, and the ADR's own §W.12 rule is about sentences, not about who first said
them.

**And the second half is the constructive one.** Rev 3 writes:

## CH-R3-6 — P8's stated property is falsified by **D2's own idempotency short-circuit**, and *"unreachable"* is conditional on a property with no check. **BLOCKING (narrow)**

**The claim.** D1.5, promoted from prose to an obligation at the lead's instruction:

> **P8, as a property of this design:** … ***No code path can respond to a write by returning success
> without a row.*** Check #20 discharges it.

**D2, 170 lines earlier, is such a code path:**

> **Idempotency (S7).** Accepting when the latest statement … is already an `Accepted` of **the same
> `AgreementVersionTextId`** is a **no-op returning success** — not a new row.

A client POSTs an accept; the server writes nothing; the caller sees success. That is the sentence's
literal negation. Rev 3 is *aware* of the short-circuit — D1.5 says *"Idempotency (D2) is still a
read-then-write short-circuit with no index behind it, deliberately"* — and then states the universal
anyway. **Check #20 cannot catch it:** part (a) greps *the statement repository* for `ON CONFLICT` /
`SqlQueryRaw` / `ExecuteUpdate` / `ExecuteDelete`, and the short-circuit lives in the handler or the
authority service; part (b) tests two concurrent appends of *different* actions.

**And the precedent it says it copies does the opposite.** `ConsentService.TryGrantAsync` returns
**`false`** when the consent is already granted (`:27-30`) — a distinguishable *"nothing happened"* the
caller can act on. D2 says the no-op is *"copied from `ConsentService`'s already-granted short-circuit,
deliberately"* and inverts the return to success. That may be the right product answer (a double-click
should not 409), but it is a **different** answer from the precedent, and it is the answer that makes
P8's universal false.

**Now the word the brief asked me to test: *unreachable*.** D1.4:

> `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` + a zero-row return mapped to caller success — the named forbidden shape — is
> not merely avoided, it is **unreachable from this design**, and check #20 is what keeps it that way.

**Tested, and it is *nearly* right — for a reason the ADR does not state, and which nothing checks.**
`ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` without a conflict target is legal PostgreSQL and swallows a conflict on *any*
constraint, including the primary key. On this table the only constraint is the PK, whose value is a
fresh `Ulid.NewUlid()` (`BaseEntity.cs:5`), so a conflict effectively never fires and a zero-row return
effectively cannot occur. **So the reachability of the forbidden shape is a function of D1.5's
"no uniqueness AT ALL", and of nothing else.** Add one unique index later — and the obvious candidate
is an *idempotency arbiter* on `(TenantId, EmployeeId, Kind, AgreementVersionTextId)`, which is exactly
what a future author reaching to make D2's read-then-write short-circuit race-safe would write — and
the forbidden shape is reachable again, in the same edit.

**Nothing checks it.** Check #18 asserts that `NullsNotDistinctIndexModelTests`'s theory list *"gains no
row"*, which keys on **columns of `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` indexes** — a nulls-*distinct* unique index adds
no row and passes. There is no check that `EmployeeAgreementStatements` has **no unique index**.
*(Independently: that theory list is already incomplete — five configurations ship `.AreNullsDistinct(false)`
(`FiscalCounter :28`, `PromoCodeRedemption :71`, **`LiveActivityToken :28`**, `MembershipBenefitUsage :64`,
`EmployeePayoutDetails :81`) and the list at `:53-57` carries four; `LiveActivityToken` is missing. So
check #18's *"a reviewer who finds a new entry in that list is looking at a design that is not this
one"* leans on a list that already fails to enumerate a shipped instance. Routed, not this ADR's.)*

**What I want changed** (properties): P8's sentence is stated about **the append** — *no **append** path
returns success without a row* — and D2's deliberate no-op is named in the same breath as the one
sanctioned exception, with its return semantics compared to `ConsentService`'s; and D1.5's *"no
uniqueness at all"* gains the check that makes *"unreachable"* true, which is a **model assertion that
the entity type declares zero unique indexes** (the same reflection surface check #2 and check #18
already use), not a grep.

*Blocking?* **Yes**, narrowly — it is the ADR's own flagship property, stated as satisfied *"by
construction, which is stronger than satisfied by mechanism"*, and both halves of that claim are
conditional on things the document does not check.

## CH-R3-9 — D1.3.1's `OccurredAt >= employee.CreatedOn` floor: **the case rev 3 could not construct is the paper channel's ordinary one**

R.3 hedge 2 asks for this explicitly:

> ⚠️ **D1.3.1's lower bound (`OccurredAt >= employee.CreatedOn`) is mine and is untested by anyone.** …
> If a cleaner can somehow hold a signed contract predating their platform registration, it is **wrong**
> … *I did not escalate it because I could not construct the case.*

The `Employee` row is created by **self-registration**: `RegisterEmployee.cs:79-90` calls
`Employee.CreateWithUser(userEntity)`, and `Employee : Auditable` so `CreatedOn` is stamped then
(`Auditable.cs:9`, re-stamped by `CommitAsync:84-87` when `CreatedBy` is empty). So the floor is
*"the moment this person made an account"*.

Now read D7's own justification for the channel that carries the operator-typed date:

> the owner's *"it for sure will be written in the contract that user signs"* means a **countersigned
> paper agreement is a real, expected instrument** that the platform must be able to record truthfully
> rather than ignore.

A countersigned B2B contract is an **offline** instrument with its own signature date, and there is no
mechanism in this design that couples that date to the account's creation. The ordinary sequences are:

- the operator meets the cleaner, countersigns the framework contract on the spot, and the cleaner
  installs the app and registers **later that week** — `OccurredAt` (the true signature date) is
  earlier than `CreatedOn`, and the validator **refuses the true record**;
- a cleaner registers, is rejected or lapses, the account is erased or a second account is created, and
  the contract on file predates the surviving `Employee` row;
- a legal entity (`EmployeeEntityType.LegalEntity` is a shipped case, `Employee.cs:150-166`) signs the
  contract in the company's name and the individual account is created afterwards.

None of these is exotic; the first is the *expected* operating mode of an operator release valve. And
the consequence is asymmetric with the defect the bound was chosen to catch: a mistyped year produces a
**false record that cannot be corrected**; the bound produces a **refusal to file a true one**, which is
recoverable only by typing a date the operator knows to be wrong. On an append-only legal log that is
the worse trade in the direction the ADR cares about.

**What I want changed:** either the floor becomes a platform constant (the ADR's own alternative — *"the
bound should become a platform constant instead"*), or it moves to a **warning** on the admin surface
rather than a validator refusal, or `Q-SELFBILL-05` gains the limb *"may a recorded contract predate the
cleaner's registration?"*. The hedge asked for a constructed case; this is one, and it is the channel's
main line.

*Blocking?* **No** — it is a validator rule, not a column, and it is answerable at ticket 3. Filed
because rev 3 asked.

## CH-R3-10 — The gate's read is **not** the read the index is shaped for, and the ADR never writes the gate's query down

D1.4 defines supersession as *"the latest statement for **(EmployeeId, Kind)**"* and the index is
`(EmployeeId, Kind, RecordedAt DESC, Id DESC)`. D5's ruled edge defines the gate's question as
**country-qualified**:

> The gate asks *"is the latest statement an `Accepted` of a text belonging to **the version applicable
> to `command.WorkCountryId`**?"*

Those are two different queries, and the difference is not cosmetic: the first is answered from the
statement table alone; the second needs `AgreementVersionTextId → AgreementVersionText → AgreementVersion.CountryId`.
Implemented as *"take the latest statement, then check its country"* it is one PK join and the index
serves it. Implemented as *"take the latest statement **whose version's country is X**"* it is a
join-then-limit and the index serves it only as an ordered driver.

Two consequences, both small, both cheap now:

1. **§W.6's own rule applies to this index too.** *"An index that serves a query must be specified in
   the artifact that fixes the query, or it is specified against a query nobody has written."* Rev 3
   severed the coverage index on that rule and kept the supersession index — while D1.4's table
   specifies a `(EmployeeId, Kind)` order and D5 specifies a country-qualified question, and neither
   section writes the gate's actual predicate down. Check #21 then pins *"the statement the production
   entry point actually emits"* — i.e. whatever ticket 2 happens to write.
2. **The two readings differ observably.** Under *"latest statement, then check its country"*, a cleaner
   who accepted country A's text and later accepted country B's has a latest statement for B, so an
   approval for A is refused even though the A acceptance was never revoked. Today that is
   unreachable through `ApproveEmployee` (its second chain refuses an already-`Approved` cleaner,
   `:28-34`), but it is reachable on the **status endpoint** and the supersession card, which show one
   answer for a cleaner who holds two true, un-revoked acceptances.

**What I want changed:** D1.4's table gains the gate's predicate as written, or D5 states which of the
two readings is the decision. One sentence.

*Blocking?* **No.**
