> ## AC1 — the ruling, in one sentence a test can check
>
> **At the moment the customer opens the preferred-cleaner picker for a slot they have already chosen,
> every cleaner who holds a live-commitment assignment overlapping that slot is rendered greyed and
> unselectable; and if one is somehow selected anyway, the order is still created, the preference is
> still stored, and NO hold and NO targeted push are produced for them.**

> ## AC2 — the property that makes it honest
>
> **The picker's answer and the hold resolver's answer come out of the same repository call with the
> same window.** Not "the same rule" — the *same method*. If the picker can say *available* and the
> resolver can then say *busy* for a reason of its own, the feature has already failed, and no amount
> of shared documentation prevents it.

## Context

### What the owner overturned, and why the original reasoning was not wrong

ADR-0036 D5.1 lists the cleaner's **time conflict** under *"Deliberately NOT checked (dynamic) — the
hold is created and simply expires"*, and A6 rejects the check outright:

> *"Both are genuinely dynamic (`TakeOrder.cs:125-161`): the limit resets weekly and the conflict
> depends on orders taken after creation. A creation-time check is wrong in both directions… Cost of
> being wrong is capped at 10% of the fill window (Invariant H), which is cheaper than a check that is
> confidently wrong."*

That argument is internally sound and its premise is stated plainly: **a hold is cheap, and a busy
cleaner simply declines.** The owner's requirement replaces the premise. It is not "check the conflict
because holds are expensive" — it is **"never present a choice we cannot honour."** The cost ADR-0036
priced was *latency*; the cost the owner is pricing is *a promise made to a customer's face*. Those are
different currencies and the second one is not bounded by Invariant H, because Invariant H bounds what
the marketplace loses, not what the customer was told.

**Both directions of A6's "wrong in both directions" survive this ADR, and only one of them was ever
avoidable:**

| Direction | ADR-0036's fear | This ADR |
|---|---|---|
| Free at creation → **busy** when they open the app | the hold expires unused | **Unchanged and accepted.** Not knowable at creation; bounded by Invariant H exactly as ADR-0036 says. |
| **Busy** at creation → free when they open the app | we suppressed a hold that would have worked | **Accepted, deliberately.** The window we withhold on is the *customer's own appointment slot*; a cleaner becomes free for it only by a cancellation. Rare, and cheap: no hold means the order goes to the open board **immediately**, which is the better failure. |
| **Busy at creation, still busy at take** | *(not considered)* | **This is the case the owner is closing.** ADR-0036 spent up to 12 h of first-seat exclusivity on a cleaner `TakeOrder.cs:59` was always going to refuse. **Pure delay with a zero success probability** — the exact failure Invariant H exists to bound, arriving in the one form we could have predicted and chose not to. |

### A6's other half is NOT overturned, and the reason is in two method signatures, not in taste

ADR-0036 A6 bundles the **weekly order limit** with the time conflict. The owner asked about *"an order
assigned to him on this date and time"*. Only one of the two is that.

| | `HasOverlappingOrderAsync` (`OrderRepository.cs:272-292`) | `GetEmployeeOrderCountThisWeekAsync` (`:247-258`) |
|---|---|---|
| Parameterised by | the **booking's** instant (`cleaningDateTime`, `estimatedTimeMinutes`) | **`DateTime.UtcNow.Date`** (`:249-252`) |
| At creation, for a booking 10 days out, it answers | *"is this cleaner busy at the customer's appointment?"* — **the question asked** | *"how many jobs does this cleaner have in the week we are in right now?"* — **a week that does not contain the booking** |

**A creation-time weekly-cap check is not a wrong answer; it is an answer to a different question.**
A6's weekly-cap half therefore stands, unchanged, on evidence rather than on judgement. Anyone who
later "completes the job" by adding the cap check to the resolver is reintroducing a defect this
section exists to prevent.

### The tool that already exists, and its two verified defects

`OrderRepository.HasOverlappingOrderAsync(employeeId, cleaningDateTime, estimatedTimeMinutes, ct)`
(`:272-292`) already answers the singular form of the owner's question, with the right status set
(`SlotBlockingStatuses`, `:263-270`) and the right NULL-`CurrentStatus` fail-closed fallback
(`:285-290`, the sanctioned exception in `patterns-backend.md` B7). It has two defects, **both
re-verified by reading for this ADR**:

**Defect 1 — it is tenant-scoped while one of its two callers is tenant-ignoring.**
`GetDbSet()` is `Context.Set<TEntity>()` (`BaseRepository.cs:158`), so the global filter applies
(`CleansiaDbContext.ApplyTenantQueryFilters:201-269`: `providerNull || (currentTenantId == null &&
e.TenantId == null) || e.TenantId == currentTenantId`). `NewJobsDigestService` selects its cleaners
and its orders with `GetQueryableIgnoringTenant()` (`:63`, `:98`) because the timer has no tenant
claim — and then calls `HasOverlappingOrderAsync` at `:137`. Under a tenant, `currentTenantId` is
`null` and the rows are not, so **every branch is false, the query returns no rows, and every cleaner
reports as free**. The digest would start advertising double-booked jobs. **The same method is
`TakeOrder`'s time-conflict write gate** (`TakeOrder.cs:59`), where the tenant-scoped read is
*correct* — the caller is a request path with a claim.

> **The defect is not "it is tenant-scoped." It is that ONE method serves TWO callers with OPPOSITE
> tenancy requirements and silently picks one of them.** That is the shape, and it generalizes.

**T-0529 found this, fixed its own instance, walked the sweep (AC5), confirmed this one, and asked the
PM to file it** (`T-0529…md:180-184`: *"`HasOverlappingOrderAsync` needs its own ticket — it does not
have one"*). It still does not have one. T-0401 is `done` and only ever touched the status set.
**§D10 files it.**

**Defect 2 — the scan has no lower bound and evaluates an interval cast per row.** The predicate
(`:282-284`) is:

```
o.AssignedEmployees.Any(e => e.EmployeeId == employeeId)
&& o.CleaningDateTime < newEnd
&& o.CleaningDateTime.AddMinutes(o.EstimatedTime) > newStart     -- computed per row, not sargable
```

The second term is the only range bound and it is an **upper** one. For a booking three days out the
scan is *everything the cleaner was ever assigned to up to that instant*, with the interval
computation evaluated per row. `SlotBlockingStatuses` prunes terminal rows — but the platform demonstrably
carries stale non-terminal rows: T-0401's own source is *"the test account's stale in-progress order
(whose completion is photo-gated and can't be finished)"*.

**Calling that once per candidate cleaner as the customer opens the picker is N of those per render.**
The picker's set is `Take(20)` (`GetMyServingCleaners.cs:50`). The naive implementation is therefore
**up to 20 unbounded history scans on the customer's booking hot path**, and — under a tenant — **20
scans that all return `false`.** Both halves are why this is an ADR and not a ticket.

### D1 — The ruling: **the picker never offers what the platform cannot give**

At the moment of choosing, a cleaner who holds a live-commitment assignment overlapping the slot the
customer has chosen is **shown, marked unavailable, and not selectable**.

**Shown, not hidden.** Hiding is the cheaper implementation and the worse product: the customer's
favourite silently disappears from a list they have seen before, and the platform has manufactured a
mystery to avoid writing a sentence. The owner's word is *"mark"*.

**Marked, not merely disabled.** ADR-0036's whole copy debt came from surfaces that changed behaviour
without changing text. A greyed row with no line is that failure again.

**Unselectable, not selectable-with-a-warning.** If it can still be selected, D2's ruling has to be
enforced twice (client and server) and the customer has been allowed to make a choice we have already
told them we cannot honour. One rule, one place.

### D2 — A cleaner we **already know** is busy gets **no hold** — and **no targeted push either**

This is the consequence the owner's requirement forces, and it is ruled here rather than left to the
implementer.

`IPreferredCleanerHoldResolver` (ADR-0036 D5.1) gains **one check and one reason**:

```csharp
public enum HoldDeclineReason
{
    None = 0, NoPreference = 1, NoMembership = 2, ShortLeadTime = 3,
    CleanerNotApproved = 4, CleanerCountryMismatch = 5, CleanerMutedNewJobs = 6,
    CleanerNotFound = 7, CleanerUnreachableForPush = 8,
    /// ADR-0039. The cleaner already holds a live-commitment assignment overlapping
    /// [cleaningUtc, cleaningUtc + estimatedMinutes). Notify: NO. Hold: NO.
    CleanerBusyAtCleaningTime = 9,
}
```

**Its row in ADR-0036 D4.1's outcome table — and it sits with the "no signal" cases, not with
`ShortLeadTime`:**

| Resolver outcome | Notify? | Hold? |
|---|---|---|
| `CleanerBusyAtCleaningTime` (**new**) | **no** | **no** |

**Why no hold.** A hold is first refusal on the first seat. `TakeOrder.cs:59` → `:145-161` refuses this
cleaner that seat for the whole window. The hold is therefore **100% of the first seat's fill window
spent on an outcome with probability zero** — not the bounded-risk trade Invariant H prices, but its
degenerate case. *Invariant H bounds what we cannot know; it is not a licence to spend what we can.*

**Why no push either — and this is ADR-0036's own rule, adopted, not a new one.** D5.1 says verbatim:
*"a hold for a cleaner `TakeOrder.cs:53` would reject is pure latency — **and so is a push**."* That
sentence was written about `ContractStatus`; it is true, word for word, about the time conflict. A
push about a job the recipient is gated out of taking is noise on the one channel ADR-0036 depends on
being worth reading. Placing this reason beside `ShortLeadTime` (notify, no hold) would be wrong for
exactly that reason: short lead means *we cannot hold*, busy means *they cannot take*.

**The D4.1 invariant survives unchanged:** `HoldUntilUtc != null ⇒ NotifyPreferred == true` — both are
false here.

**Where the check sits in the resolver, and why last.** It is the only check that costs a query with a
range scan. Every other gate (preference set · membership · lead time · approved · work country ·
mute · device reachability) is an equality or an arithmetic comparison on rows the resolver is already
fetching. **The busy check runs last, only when everything else has passed**, so the ordinary path
(no preference, or a non-member) pays nothing. ADR-0036's decision-tree diagram gains one node between
the reachability gate and the lead-time gate — see the living doc.

**No new error key. No customer-visible rejection.** See D8.

### D3 — The read path is **one set-based question**, not N independent ones

> **What the picker needs is *"which of these cleaners are busy in this window"*, which is one query.
> `HasOverlappingOrderAsync` answers *"is this one cleaner busy"*, which is the same question asked
> twenty times.**

**Ruling: a new repository method.** Specified:

```csharp
// Cleansia.Core.Domain/Repositories/IOrderRepository.cs
//
// Which of `employeeIds` hold a LIVE-COMMITMENT assignment overlapping
// [windowStartUtc, windowEndUtc)?  Returns the BUSY subset — never the free set.
//
//  * ONE query for the whole set. Calling HasOverlappingOrderAsync in a loop over a
//    candidate list is a hard reject (ADR-0039 D3).
//  * "Live commitment" is OrderRepository.SlotBlockingStatuses WITH the same
//    NULL-CurrentStatus latest-history fallback HasOverlappingOrderAsync uses
//    (patterns-backend B7's fail-CLOSED exception). This method must NOT invent a
//    second definition of "occupied" — see D3.2.
//  * The scan is bounded BELOW by windowStartUtc - BookingPolicy.MaxOrderSpanHours.
//    Without a floor the only sargable term is `CleaningDateTime < windowEnd`, i.e.
//    all of history. See D3.1.
//  * TENANT-SCOPED, deliberately — every caller is a request path with a tenant claim.
//    A background sweep MUST call the IgnoringTenant sibling. This method does not
//    choose tenancy for its caller (D6).
Task<IReadOnlySet<string>> GetBusyEmployeeIdsInWindowAsync(
    IReadOnlyCollection<string> employeeIds,
    DateTime windowStartUtc,
    DateTime windowEndUtc,
    CancellationToken cancellationToken);

// Same predicate, IgnoreQueryFilters(). Sole intended caller: NewJobsDigestService.
Task<IReadOnlySet<string>> GetBusyEmployeeIdsInWindowIgnoringTenantAsync( /* … */ );
```

Implementation shape — **drive from `Orders`, not from `OrderEmployees`**:

```csharp
var floor = windowStartUtc.AddHours(-BookingPolicy.MaxOrderSpanHours);
var busy = await GetDbSet()                                  // or .IgnoreQueryFilters()
    .Where(o => o.CleaningDateTime >= floor                              // NEW: the range floor
             && o.CleaningDateTime <  windowEndUtc
             && o.CleaningDateTime.AddMinutes(o.EstimatedTime) > windowStartUtc
             && ((o.CurrentStatus != null && SlotBlockingStatuses.Contains(o.CurrentStatus.Value))
                 || (o.CurrentStatus == null && o.OrderStatusHistory
                        .OrderByDescending(s => s.CreatedOn).ThenByDescending(s => s.Sequence)
                        .Take(1).Any(s => SlotBlockingStatuses.Contains(s.Status)))))
    .SelectMany(o => o.AssignedEmployees)
    .Where(ae => employeeIds.Contains(ae.EmployeeId))
    .Select(ae => ae.EmployeeId)
    .Distinct()
    .ToListAsync(cancellationToken);
return busy.ToHashSet(StringComparer.Ordinal);
```

**Why this side and not the other.** `IX_OrderEmployees_EmployeeId` exists (`Initial.Designer.cs:3188`)
and would serve an `EmployeeId IN (…)` drive — but the date band can only prune *after* the join, so
each candidate's whole assignment history is still walked. Driving from `Orders` puts the two selective
terms together on **`IX_Orders_CurrentStatus_CleaningDateTime`** (`OrderEntityConfiguration.cs:111`),
which already exists and is already the board query's index: a status-set + narrow-date-band range
scan, then a semi-join to `OrderEmployees` on **`IX_OrderEmployees_OrderId`** (`Initial.cs:2801` — the
third index this drive depends on, unnamed in the draft and supplied by CH-Q6). **No new index.
`D5.5`'s posture (no index for this feature) is preserved.**

> **[AMENDED BY PANEL — CH-Q1 / CH-Q6] The "no new index" claim is downgraded from a fact to a reasoned
> expectation with a named plan, a flip condition, and a measurement obligation.**
>
> The draft asserted *"a status-set + narrow-date-band range scan"* as if it were the plan. It is not
> necessarily the plan, and the reason is the predicate's own fail-closed NULL arm. The status term sits
> inside an `OR` (`OrderRepository.cs:300-305`), so it is not an unconditional index qual; the only
> unconditional sargable conjunct is a **range on `CleaningDateTime`, the index's SECOND key column**,
> and a PG16 btree cannot start a scan on the second column with the first unconstrained. The plan the
> claim actually depends on is a **`BitmapOr` of two index paths** (`CurrentStatus = ANY(arr) AND band`
> ∪ `CurrentStatus IS NULL AND band`) with the correlated `EXISTS` re-checked on the heap scan. That is
> plausible and it is *not* guaranteed.
>
> **Ruling.** The claim stands as the **expected** plan, not as a fact, on three grounds the challenger
> supplied and the lead verified: the second arm is **empty on any database created from the single
> `Initial` migration** (`src/Cleansia.Infra.Database/Migrations/` contains exactly one migration, so
> there are no pre-backfill rows); the tenant global filter is **not** a second planner hazard (its
> three operands but one fold to constants and EF keys its SQL cache on parameter nullability —
> `CleansiaDbContext.cs:262-264`); and the tenant filter is the **only** global filter in the context
> (`:126`), so `IgnoreQueryFilters()` un-filters nothing else.
>
> **Obligation, not a checklist line: `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` on the emitted SQL, in
> `src/Cleansia.IntegrationTests`, as an AC on A1.** The rig exists — `PostgresContainerFixture` runs a
> real PostgreSQL via Testcontainers and `BaseIntegrationTest.MigrateAsync()` applies the real
> migrations, so all three indexes are present exactly as in production. ADR-0036's "priced by
> reasoning" caveat was honest *because it had no rig*; repeating it here when the rig is one
> `SqlQueryRaw` away is not honesty, it is inheritance.
>
> **Flip condition, precise:** if the measured plan is a seq scan or a full index scan on `Orders`, the
> answer is **A19 (the `Concat` restructure)**, not a new index — A14's rejection of an extra index on
> the hottest insert path survives either way. **A18 (`CurrentStatus NOT NULL`)** is the cheaper and
> more general answer and is escalated in §Escalations rather than decided here.
>
> **Also corrected:** the draft never costed the side it rejected. Driving from
> `IX_OrderEmployees_EmployeeId` is ≤20 SAOP index scans plus PK probes into `Orders`; driving from
> `Orders` scans a platform-wide band **independent of candidate count**. **A crossover exists** — at
> low order volume the rejected side is cheaper. The chosen side is chosen for its behaviour *at scale*
> and for its single-query shape, and this ADR says so instead of asserting a strict win.

> number must dominate every *producible* span and the producible maximum is 58.25 h** — 7 days gives

> draft would not: **"Assumed, not enforced."**

> **The panel's addition, and it is what makes the assumption stop being one — see D3.4.** A floor that
> may only ever be too generous, sitting above a span that nothing caps, is a comment. D3.4 caps the
> span on the write path so the floor's premise becomes true by construction, which is what D3.1
> claimed and never had.

**The drafted (superseded) form, kept for the record:**

```csharp

/// overlap scans start at windowStart - MaxOrderSpanHours instead of at the beginning
```

**The number is chosen by its failure asymmetry, not by measurement:**

| If the floor is | Cost |
|---|---|
| **too generous** (hours earlier than any real order) | a slightly wider index range scan on a band that is nearly empty |
| **too tight** (by ten minutes) | an overlapping order is invisible → the picker says *available* → **and the same predicate is the `TakeOrder` write gate, so a cleaner is double-booked** |

So: **when in doubt, widen it.** ~~`EstimatedTime` is the sum of the booked services' estimates
(`OrderFactory.cs:145-146`) for a single appointment on a single day; 24 h exceeds any single-day span
by construction.~~ **[STRUCK BY PANEL — CH-Q2.]** There is no construction; see D3.1's amendment and
D3.4's cap, which supplies one. The asymmetry table above **survives intact** and is the reason 168
was chosen over 24.

**[AMENDED BY PANEL — CH-Q2] The one-line check is a sample, not a bound, and it is now the *second*
line of defence.** `SELECT MAX("EstimatedTime") FROM "Orders"` observes created orders at a point in

`UpdateService`, or a probe that never creates an order. Nothing runs it on a schedule. It stays as
verify #6 (rewritten) because a cheap observation is worth having — but **D3.4's enforced cap is what
makes the floor safe**, and the query is now a *falsifier* for the cap, not a substitute for it.

**The durable alternative, recorded with its flip condition:** persist the appointment's **end**
instant on `Order` and index it, making both sides of the overlap sargable and the floor unnecessary.
Rejected for now — a migration plus a backfill plus a second column to keep in sync — but it is the
right answer and the flip condition is precise: **`MAX(EstimatedTime)` approaching the floor, or the
floor showing up in a slow-query report.** `Order.EstimatedTime` is written once
(`OrderFactory.cs:147`, no reschedule path exists — ADR-0036 CH-V9), so the denormalization would be
cheap to maintain when it is wanted.

#### D3.3 — **[NEW — panel]** Two fan-out shapes, and only one of them is this ADR's to build

CH-Q3 is upheld: the specified signature is **many employees, one window**, and two of the four callers

  definition of occupied" true across all three shapes, which is the property D3.2 protects.

**And the trap that composing with ADR-0037 creates — recorded because it fails OPEN.**
ADR-0037 (`accepted`) makes offerability `STATUS(o) ∧ NotRetractable(o)` where `NotRetractable(o) ⟺
PaymentStatus == Paid ∨ (PaymentType == Cash ∧ RecurringTemplateId == null)`. It is tempting to reuse
that predicate here — both are "is this order real?" questions and both key on status.

> **`NotRetractable` must NOT become a conjunct of the occupancy predicate. Adding it fails OPEN.**
> Offerability is an **entry** condition: post-ADR-0037 nothing can be *assigned* unless it was
> offerable, so every occupying order already satisfies `NotRetractable` by construction and the term
> would be redundant on the happy path. Where it would **not** be redundant is the case that matters:
> an order whose payment state changed *after* a cleaner took it. The cleaner is committed; the slot is
> occupied; adding `NotRetractable` would make that order stop blocking and **double-book a cleaner who
> is standing in someone's flat.** Retraction is expressed the one correct way — a sweep moves the
> order to `Cancelled`, which is terminal and already absent from `SlotBlockingStatuses`
> (`OrderRepository.cs:263-270`). **Occupancy asks about a commitment already made; offerability asks
> about work still available. One predicate each.**

#### D3.4 — **[NEW — panel]** The span the floor assumes is now **enforced on the write path**

The floor (`Order.MaxOrderSpanHours = 168`) documents itself as *"assumed, not enforced"*. CH-Q2 is
upheld: a safety-asymmetric constant with nothing enforcing its safe side is a comment. CH-D2 is upheld
on the same lever from the other lane: because the picker's window end is derived from a
**client-chosen selection**, the request carries a range parameter under another name, and the shipped
catalog makes that range **58.25 h wide today**.

**Ruling: one enforced cap, at the single writer, and it closes both findings at once.**

```csharp
// Cleansia.Core.AppServices/Features/Orders/BookingPolicy.cs — a POLICY number, in the layer

/// The longest span a single booking may be CREATED with. Enforced, not assumed:
/// OrderFactory rejects above it, so Order.MaxOrderSpanHours' premise becomes true by construction.
/// It is also, unavoidably, a CREW cap — RequiredEmployees = ceil(EstimatedTime / 120), so 24 h
/// implies a maximum crew of 12 (Order.CalculateRequiredEmployees, BookingPolicy.SpareSeatsPerOrder = 0).
public const int MaxBookableOrderSpanHours = 24;
```

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| `OrderFactory.cs:147` (**the single production writer** of `EstimatedTime` — CH-Q2 grepped every `UpdateEstimatedTime` call site and the other four are tests) | reject above the cap. Covers `CreateOrder` **and** `MaterializeRecurringBookings` with one guard. Must run **before** `CalculateRequiredEmployees` (`:148`) so an absurd span never mints an absurd crew |
| `CreateOrder.Validator` + `QuoteOrder.Validator` | mirror it, so the customer gets a business error rather than an exception. New `BusinessErrorMessage` key + 5 translations |
| the picker's handler (D5) | apply the **same** cap to the derived window. A span the platform will not create is a question the picker will not answer — that is what removes CH-D2's range parameter |
| `Cleansia.Tests` | `Assert.True(BookingPolicy.MaxBookableOrderSpanHours <= Order.MaxOrderSpanHours)`. **The relationship between the two numbers is the whole safety argument; a unit test is cheaper than a comment and does not rot** |

**Why 24 and why two numbers instead of one.** They are different kinds of number in different layers
and conflating them is what produced D3.1's compile error. `Order.MaxOrderSpanHours` is a **query
safety margin** in Domain that must dominate every *producible* span; `BookingPolicy
.MaxBookableOrderSpanHours` is a **product policy** in AppServices that decides what a booking may be.
24 h rejects nothing a real appointment does (the whole seeded service catalog is 20.25 h) and rejects
every selection that only a probe or a mis-click produces. With the cap at 24 and the floor at 168 the
floor carries **7× headroom over an enforced bound** instead of 3× over an unenforced hope — and the
blind spot pinned as an accepted `Assert.False` in
`HasOverlappingOrderTenancyAndScanFloorTests.An_Order_Longer_Than_The_Span_Bound_Starting_Before_The_Floor_Is_Outside_The_Guarantee`
becomes **unreachable by construction**, which is the first time that has been true.

**Blocking status, ruled precisely because the two lanes disagree about urgency:**
- On the **double-booking** lane (CH-Q2) it does **not** block. At a 168 h floor the failure needs a
  seven-day order; the catalog produces 58.25 h. Real, bounded, not live.
- On the **disclosure** lane (CH-D2) it **does** block A3. 58.25 h of caller-controlled window is a
  binary-search primitive the day the flag ships, and it is *the* thing that turns the ADR's
  "one instant" claim into fiction. **D12.2.**

### D4 — The window must be the **real** one, and its duration has exactly one definition

The overlap window is `[cleaningDateTimeUtc, cleaningDateTimeUtc + estimatedMinutes)`. `estimatedMinutes`
is not optional and not nominal:

| If the window is | Failure |
|---|---|
| **too short** | we say *available* for a cleaner who is busy — **the exact failure the owner is closing** |
| **too long** | we grey out a cleaner the customer could have had, invisibly and unappealably |

So a nominal window (e.g. `StandardWorkUnitMinutes`) is wrong in both directions and is rejected. The
duration must be the one the created order will carry.

**`QuoteOrder.Response` does not carry it** (`:42-57` — pricing only), so the client cannot supply it.
**And it must not**: a client-supplied number that decides a server answer is an S1 violation, and a
tampered one silently un-greys a busy cleaner.

**Ruling: the server derives it, from one definition, used by both writers.** `OrderFactory.cs:145-146`
computes it inline today and would gain a second reader:

```csharp

public static int EstimateMinutes(IEnumerable<Service> services, IEnumerable<Package> packages);
```

`OrderFactory.cs:145-147` calls it instead of summing inline.
~~The picker's handler loads the selection through `IServiceRepository` / `IPackageRepository` exactly
as `QuoteOrder.Validator` already does (`:61-65`) and calls the same function.~~

> `OrderFactory.cs:131-140`'s shape: `GetByIds` plus a two-level `Include(p => p.IncludedServices)

### D7 — What the customer sees, and the copy constraints

#### D7.1 — The treatment

| | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Row visibility | **shown** — never removed from the list |
| Row state | greyed (reduced-emphasis foreground on both clients' existing token), **not tappable**, no selection ring, no checkmark |
| Row text | name, unchanged, **plus one neutral line**: *"Not available for this date and time"* |
| Clearing | if the currently-selected cleaner becomes unavailable when the customer changes the slot, the selection is **cleared** and the picker's persistent explanatory line (ADR-0036 C2c) returns. **No toast, no dialog, no "your cleaner was removed" alert** — the row is visibly marked and that is the disclosure |
| The **unmarked** rows | **nothing changes about them.** See D7.3 |

#### D7.2 — The copy constraint set (binding on whoever writes the strings — T-0491's lane)

1. **One string, five languages, both customer clients.** Android `values*/strings.xml` (5 files), iOS
   `Localizable.xcstrings` (1 file / 5 languages). The web customer wizard has **no picker at all**
   (`order-wizard.facade.ts:576-580` sends `undefined` unconditionally), so there is no web cost today
   — and the ticket that builds the web picker inherits this constraint.
2. **It names no reason.** Not *"already booked"*, not *"busy"*, not *"has another job"*, not
   *"working elsewhere"*, no time, no count, no place, nothing about anyone else's booking. **It is a
   statement about what Cleansia can offer, not about what the person is doing.**
3. **It promises no other time.** No *"try a different time"* suggestion, no *"next available"*, no
   affordance that leads to a calendar. We do not have that answer and we are not building the claim.
   *(This is the sprint's own lesson applied: ADR-0036 C0 is deleting the unbacked "Within 1 hour"
   claim right now. Do not ship its replacement in the same feature.)*
4. **It stays true if the predicate widens.** *"Not available for this date and time"* remains true if
   a later revision folds approval, work country or anything else into the flag. *"Already booked"*
   becomes a lie the moment it does. **Pick the label that survives the next version of the rule.**
5. **It is never shown for `null`.** An unevaluated answer renders as an ordinary selectable row.
6. **It must not upgrade the promise for the others** — see D7.3.

#### D7.3 — The subtle one: marking three rows must not silently promise the other two

Greying two of five cleaners implies the remaining three are *available to you*, which is a stronger
claim than ADR-0036 D1 permits — the perk is **first chance, never "your cleaner"**, and every gate in
`TakeOrder` still applies to the preferred cleaner exactly as to anyone else (ADR-0036 D5.1's AC6
interaction table).

> **Constraint: ADR-0036's persistent explanatory line (C2c) is UNCHANGED and still renders on the
> picker row.** The unavailable marking is subtractive only. Nothing in this feature may be written as
> *"these cleaners are available for your booking"* — the honest frame is *"these ones we already know
> we cannot offer"*.

A reviewer checks this by reading the two strings side by side: the C2c line still says *first chance*,
and the new line says only *not available*. If the C2c line was changed to sell availability, that is a
finding against this ADR, not a copy improvement.

#### D7.4 — What is disclosed, stated plainly rather than argued away

**This flag does disclose something about the cleaner.** No amount of neutral wording changes that, and
pretending otherwise would be exactly the kind of unbacked claim this platform is currently removing.

- **What is disclosed:** that a cleaner **who has personally completed a job for this customer** is
  occupied during **the single window this customer chose**.
- **What is not:** who booked them, where, for how long, what kind of job, or anything about any other
  window the customer did not ask about.
- **Why it is accepted:** there is no way to tell a customer *"you cannot have Anna at 10:00"* without
  telling them Anna is unavailable at 10:00. The feature is the disclosure. The alternative the owner
  overturned — offer her, take the booking, silently drop the preference — discloses nothing and lies.
- ~~**The residual, named:** a determined customer can probe by re-opening the picker across slots.
  Bounded by the serving-cleaner set (people who have been in their home), and by the fact that a
  `false` names no reason.~~

> **[AMENDED BY PANEL — CH-D1 / CH-D4 / CH-D5. The residual is restated as a number, and the word
> "determined" is withdrawn.]**
>
> **The residual, quantified.** After D12: the reachable **set** is the customer's own serving cleaners
> (≤20, accumulated one completed paid cleaning at a time — structural, verified). The reachable
> **depth per request** is one window of at most `BookingPolicy.MaxBookableOrderSpanHours` (24 h,
> enforced — D3.4). The reachable **rate** is 30 requests/min per `sub`, shared with every other `auth`-
> policy endpoint that account uses (D12.1). At that rate a full week of hour-resolution answers costs
> ~3 minutes of sustained requests and burns the account's entire auth budget while it runs; 30 days
> costs ~12 minutes. **The owner is accepting those numbers, not an adjective.** No mechanism in this
> ADR makes bulk reconstruction impossible; the rate limit makes it slow, budget-consuming and
> countable, which is what a rate limit is for.
>
> **And "determined" is the wrong word after A4 ships — CH-D4 is upheld.** Both clients fetch the list
> **once** today (`PreferredCleanerPicker.kt:85-92`, `LaunchedEffect(isPlus)` behind a one-shot
> `loaded` latch, with a doc comment at `:56-58` explaining that the set only grows on order
> completion; `PreferredCleanerViewModel.swift:27-29`, once per view-model lifetime). D7.1's *clearing*
> rule invalidates that premise — the answer now depends on the chosen slot — so A4 must re-fetch on
> slot change. **After which probing is the ordinary interaction**, performed by customers who are
> attacking nothing, once per scrub of the date control. The system will emit a stream of occupancy
> answers as a side effect of normal use. That is accepted; it is also **why the rate limit is a
> precondition and why A4 owes a debounce** (D12.1) — otherwise honest scrubbing spends the same
> budget an attacker would, and the first 429 lands on a customer.
>
> **A4 is therefore not an "S".** Slot-keyed fetching, a `loaded`-latch removal, selection-clearing
> state, in-flight cancellation, a debounce, **two mapper changes with their own tests (CH-D3)**, and
> one string × 5 locales × 2 clients. Re-costed **S → M** in D10.

> **The line this ADR draws and does not cross: no date-range parameter, no calendar view, no
> "next available" suggestion, ever.** Those turn a per-booking answer into a schedule feed about a
> worker, and they are a **separate decision with a separate privacy analysis**, not an extension of
> this field. An implementer who "helpfully" adds a range parameter has changed the decision.
> **[AMENDED — CH-D2] And the line is now enforced by a bound, not by the absence of a parameter**:
> the request *does* control the window end (via the selection), so "no range parameter" was never a
> true description. What makes the line real is D3.4's cap and verify #11's rewritten assertion on
> `windowEnd − windowStart`.

**Escalated to the owner (`Q-AVAIL-04`, §Escalations):** whether cleaners should be told — in the
partner app or the terms — that past customers can see, at booking time, whether they are free for a
specific slot. **[AMENDED BY PANEL — CH-D9. The escalation is re-scoped from *notice* to *lawful
basis*, and the mechanism it could change is reserved now.]** The platform already carries a real
consent posture — `ConsentType` (`TermsOfService`, `PrivacyPolicy`, `MarketingEmails`,
`DataProcessing`) and `UserConsent` with grant/withdraw, `IpAddress` and `UserAgent`, and cleaners are
`User`s — so the question in front of the owner is not manners: it is **which basis covers disclosing a
worker's occupancy to a third party for that third party's convenience**, and any balancing test has to
survive D7.4's *quantified* residual rather than the one-slot story.

**Why it is still non-blocking, and what makes that true rather than assumed.** The most likely
outcome of a balancing test on worker data is *cleaners may object / opt out*, which would need a
per-employee suppression flag the busy check honours. Of the three ways to render a suppressed cleaner,
two are wrong: **`false`** (mark unavailable) **leaks the opt-out itself** — the classic
opt-out-reveals-membership disclosure — and silently costs that cleaner every preferred booking;
**`true`** asserts something the server declined to evaluate. **`null` is the only honest value**, and
D5's contract already renders `null` as an ordinary unmarked selectable row. **Ruling: D5's `null`
doc-comment names suppression as a third `null` producer today.** It is a comment now and a working
seam later, it costs nothing, and it is what converts *"changes text, not the mechanism"* from an
assumption into a fact.

### D8 — The race, and the tri-state, are the **same** ruling

The cleaner is free when the picker renders and taken by the time the order submits. There is no lock
and there must not be one — **a picker render must never reserve a stranger's calendar.**

**The ruling, and it is one sentence: absence of a `yes` is never a `yes`, and it is never a rejection
either.**

| Moment | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Picker says **available**, submit finds them **busy** | **The order is created. Normally. Fully.** `Order.PreferredEmployeeId` is **stored** (ADR-0036 D2: *"we stored your preference but could not act on it"* must be expressible). `PreferredHoldUntilUtc` is **null**. No targeted push. `Reason = CleanerBusyAtCleaningTime`. |
| The check **could not run** (query failed) at the picker | `IsAvailableForRequestedSlot = null` → **no marking, row selectable.** Degraded to today's behaviour, which is a degradation, not a lie. |
| The check **could not run** at the resolver | **The booking is never failed for it.** Treated as *unknown* ⇒ **no hold, no push**, same as busy. A perk fails closed; a booking never does. |
| No slot in the request (old client, or the confirm step before a slot exists) | `null` → no marking. The clients that ship today are exactly this case and keep working unchanged. |

**What the customer is told when they lose the race: nothing new.** ADR-0036 D6 and A8 stand
unamended — no push, no state, no *"Anna couldn't take it"*. The picker's answer was **true when it was
given**; the perk was never a guarantee (D1); and a notification whose entire content is bad news about
something the customer did not know was in doubt manufactures a disappointment out of a normal outcome.

**And this is deliberately NOT ADR-0036 D7's treatment.** D7 *rejects the whole booking* when the
membership gate fails. The difference is not severity, it is **agency**, and ADR-0036 D8 already named
the rule this ADR is applying: ***reject where someone can react; degrade where nobody can.***

| | Membership (D7) | Busy at submit (here) |
|---|---|---|
| ~~Fact is~~ **[STRUCK BY PANEL]** | ~~**static** — true before the customer opened the app~~ | ~~**dynamic** — became true seconds ago, possibly mid-checkout~~ |
| Customer can fix it in one tap | **yes** (remove the request, or subscribe) | **no** — the only fix is to move their own appointment |
| Therefore | **reject**, with a message that names the tap | **degrade**, silently, and create the order |

> **[AMENDED BY PANEL — the `Q-PLUS-05` premise, which the ADR itself flagged for review.] Row 1 is

> **Reject-vs-degrade turns on agency, not on staleness** — *"can the customer fix it in one tap?"* —
> and that answer is unchanged: **yes** for membership, **no** for busy. A criterion that survives its

**Flip condition, so this is falsifiable:** support evidence that customers believe the preference was
honoured when it was not. **The answer then is copy (ADR-0036 C0/C2c), not a push** — and it is
foreseen here so the next reader does not reopen A8.

### D12 — **[NEW — panel]** The three preconditions of A3, and why they gate the *server*, not the UI

> The draft's safety argument had two structural limits; the panel found one real and one absent (D5's
> amendment). These three restore the missing bound. **Each gates A3 — the ticket where the server
> starts answering — because the oracle exists the moment the server answers, whatever any client
> renders.** Gating A4 would have been the natural call and it would have shipped the oracle.

#### D12.1 — The endpoint is rate-limited, and the flag is Plus-gated **server-side**

**The rate limit is `[EnableRateLimiting("auth")]` — the existing policy, on both customer hosts.** The
challenger asked for a new narrow per-`sub` window and rejected `interactive` (60/min) as too loose;
the lead rules the **cheaper** answer that the challenger's own evidence supports: the `auth` policy is
already **30/min per `sub`** (`RateLimitPolicies.cs:47-49` `DefaultAuthAuthenticatedLimit = 30`,
partitioned `auth:sub:{sub}` at `:64-68`), it is what `CancelOrder` uses **eleven lines above (`:171` vs `:182`) in the same
controller**, and a new named policy would mean editing `Cleansia.Config`, which is registered **once
for all five hosts** (ADR-0003 D5) — a shared-config change and an ADR-0003 amendment to buy a number
we already have. **One attribute × two files. No new policy, no config change, no ADR-0003 edit.**

Two things this rules that the challenger did not:
- **Sharing the `auth` partition with login/cancel is a feature, not a collision.** The budget is per
  `sub` across all `auth`-policy endpoints, so sweeping the picker spends the account's ability to do
  anything else. That is the correct shape for a probe.
- **Therefore A4 owes a debounce** (D7.4's amendment). Once the fetch is slot-keyed, an honest customer
  scrubbing a time control is the caller most likely to hit 30/min. **A rate limit whose first victim
  is a real customer gets removed; one with a debounce in front of it does not.**

**The classification is corrected, and the correction is the durable output.** The challenger labelled
this **S5 FAIL**. `security-rules.md:112-134` scopes S5 to *"auth + side-effecting endpoints"* — this is
a read that mutates nothing, so **by the catalog as written it is not an S5 violation, which is exactly
why it was missed.** The finding is real and the rule is missing. **The rule this ADR sends to the
catalog:** *a read endpoint whose answer is per-subject and whose repetition reconstructs a dataset is
an **oracle**, and is rate-limited like a side-effecting one.* (Filed as an `S5` amendment; the ADR does
not edit the catalog in the same breath as ruling on itself.)

**The Plus gate goes server-side — on the FLAG, not on the list.** The owner ruled the perk Plus-only
(`Q-PLUS-03`); it is enforced today only in mobile UI (`PreferredCleanerPicker.kt:70,85-92`,
`PreferredCleanerViewModel.swift:34`), which is a client-side authorization for a server-side answer.
The handler gains the **one** live-membership predicate
(`UserMembershipRepository.ActiveForUserQuery:20-31` — never a second one) and:

| Caller | List | Flag |
|---|---|---|
| Plus member, slot supplied | rows returned | `true` / `false` |
| **non-member** (incl. `PastDue` — `Q-PLUS-05`) | **rows returned, unchanged** | **`null` on every row — never `true`, never `false`** |

**Gating the flag rather than the list is the load-bearing choice.** Gating the list would change a
shipped endpoint's contract, break clients that render it, and re-decide ADR-0036's surface. Gating the
flag closes the oracle for every non-member, changes nothing that ships today, and lands on the
tri-state's third state **which D5 already defines as "not evaluated"** — the same state CH-D9's
suppression seam wants. One state, three producers, no new concept.

#### D12.2 — The window's depth is capped on the write path

D3.4, in full. The picker will not answer a question about a span the platform will not create. This is
what removes the range parameter CH-D2 found under another name.

#### D12.3 — The list carries an `IsActive` filter, and the resolver checks it too

**CH-D6 is upheld, and the lead escalates its severity: this is not only ADR-0039's problem.**

The chain the challenger traced is verified. `GetMyServingCleaners.cs:26-33` filters on
`o.UserId == userId && o.CurrentStatus == Completed` and nothing else; `BaseRepository.cs:148-158`
applies the tenant filter only and EF `Include`s are never filtered; a departing or GDPR-erased cleaner
is **soft-deleted, not removed** (`GdprDeletionService.cs:235-241` → `user.Employee.Deactivated(...)`
→ `Auditable.cs:35-42` sets `IsActive = false`) while the historical `Completed` order survives. So a
cleaner who left the platform is returned by the picker — and, having no live orders, **computes as
free**, renders as an ordinary unmarked selectable row, and is selectable. `CreateOrder`'s eligibility
validator does not catch it either: `UserHasCompletedOrderWithEmployeeAsync` (`OrderRepository.cs:309-320`)
asks only whether a `Completed` order exists, never whether the employee still works here.

**Two rulings, because there are two failures and only one of them is new:**

1. **The list filter (`IsActive` on the `Employee` and on its `User`) is a precondition of A3.** Before
   this ADR, a stale row was a cosmetic wart on a list that made no claim. After it, **the absence of a
   mark is an affirmative claim**, and the claim is false. D11 deferred this to A17 as "a filter, not a
   flag" — that shape ruling is **correct and unchanged**; what changes is that the filter now **gates**
   the flag instead of following it. Shipping the flag onto an unfiltered list ships the exact lie the
   ADR's own title forbids. It is `S10` (`security-rules.md:261-268`: *no global `IsActive` filter
   exists; every query that should hide deactivated rows must filter itself*) — this one should.
2. **The resolver's approval gate must read `IsActive`, not `ContractStatus` alone — and that defect is
   ADR-0036's, live today, without ADR-0039.** `HoldDeclineReason.CleanerNotApproved` keys on
   `ContractStatus`, but `Deactivated(...)` sets `IsActive = false` and **leaves `ContractStatus`
   untouched**, so an erased cleaner can still be `Approved`. Under ADR-0036 alone that means a hold
   plus a targeted push granted to someone who left the platform — **100% of the first seat's fill
   window on a zero-probability outcome, which is verbatim the failure D2 exists to eliminate**, and
   with `SpareSeatsPerOrder = 0` it is now the *only* seat. **Added as an AC on T-0515 (A5), and it
   does not wait on this ADR.**

## Consequences

**Cheaper / safer**
- **The picker and the grant cannot disagree** — same predicate, same window, one definition (AC2).
- **One busy query per picker render**, replacing up to twenty unbounded history scans, **expected** to
  be servable from an index that already exists. **[AMENDED — CH-Q1] "No new index" is now an
  expectation with a named plan (`BitmapOr`), a measurement obligation and a flip condition (A19) —
  not an assertion.** ADR-0036 D5.5's posture is preserved either way; A14 (add an index) stays
  rejected on its own merits.
- **[AMENDED — D0] The write gate already has the floor, and it kept `AnyAsync`'s early exit** — the
  shipped convergence shares the *predicate*, not the terminal operation, so the draft's "for free"
  turned out to be true in a way the draft did not specify (it had traded early exit for the bound).
  **Two pin classes** now hold it: `HasOverlappingOrderStatusTests` (11 status cases) and
  `HasOverlappingOrderTenancyAndScanFloorTests` (5 tenancy + 3 floor cases, every tenancy case seeding a
  **non-null `TenantId`**).
- **Zero data risk.** No migration, no backfill, no schema change, no new index, one nullable DTO field.
- **[AMENDED — CH-D3] The shipped clients keep working unchanged *on the wire*** — every new request
  field is optional, Android's `Json { ignoreUnknownKeys = true; explicitNulls = false }` and iOS's
  `Codable`/`CodingKeys` both tolerate the added field (verified, not assumed), and `null` is the honest
  answer for them. **But the clients do NOT keep working if the new field is mapped in either mapper's
  house style** — that is A4's binding contract, not a wire-compat property.
- **[AMENDED — D3.4] The span the floor assumes is enforced at the single writer**, so
  `Order.MaxOrderSpanHours`' premise is true by construction for the first time and the pinned blind
  spot becomes unreachable.
- **[NEW — D12] The disclosure has a stated bound with numbers in it**, and the ADR no longer claims a
  structural limit it does not build.

**More expensive (accepted, and named)**
- **[AMENDED — CH-Q5] The picker render is not "one extra query".** It is the list (now a single
  projected, no-tracking query after A6, down from 2 split queries materializing **127 columns to use
  4**, including `IBAN` and `PassportId`), **plus two scalar `Sum` projections** for the duration, **plus
  one busy query** — four round trips, all bounded, none materializing an entity graph. The draft said
  "one" and cited a `CountAsync` as the precedent for two entity loads. **Corrected.**
- **One extra bounded query on the create path** when a preference is set and every cheaper gate has
  passed. **[AMENDED — CH-Q6] No longer priced by reasoning alone**: A1 carries an `EXPLAIN` AC against
  real PostgreSQL in `Cleansia.IntegrationTests`. ADR-0036's "reasoning, not measurement" caveat was
  honest *because it had no rig*; inheriting it here, where `PostgresContainerFixture` applies the real
  migrations, would not be.
- **[NEW — D12.1] Every picker fetch now spends the account's `auth` budget**, shared with login and
  cancel. Accepted deliberately (a probe should cost the prober something), and **paid for by A4's
  debounce**, without which the first 429 lands on a customer scrubbing a time control.
- **[NEW — D10] A4 is an `M`, not an `S`** — the fetch lifecycle, the mappers and their pins were
  budgeted at zero by the draft.
- **⚠️ `nswag-regen`** on the customer clients, and a new string × 5 languages × 2 clients.
- **A real, bounded disclosure about a cleaner's schedule** to customers they have served (D7.4), with a
  worker-transparency question escalated rather than assumed.
- **The 2–8 h short-lead band now has two ways to get no hold** (lead time, and busyness) and one of
  them also removes the push. The band's copy must not imply otherwise — ADR-0036 §Copy already
  requires a sentence true in both outcomes; this adds a third outcome it must also survive.

## Escalations (owner) — listed here, **not** written to `questions/open.md` by this ADR

- **`Q-AVAIL-05` — ⏳ TIME-BOXED, and the box is open right now. Should `Orders.CurrentStatus` become
  `NOT NULL` while the database is being dropped and `Initial` regenerated?** (Alternative A18, raised
  by CH-Q1 and the cheapest option anywhere in this document.) The column is nullable **only** to serve
  rows written before the backfill (`OrderEntityConfiguration.cs:98-105`); the repo carries **exactly
  one migration**, so the population it protects is empty by construction. `NOT NULL` deletes an `OR`
  disjunct, a correlated `EXISTS`, a planner risk and a fail-closed special case **for every consumer
  of the column** — this ADR's predicate, the availability spec, `OrderMappers.GetCurrentOrderStatus`'s
  `CurrentStatus!.Value`, and `TakeOrder.cs:191`'s request-path dereference.
  **Why the owner and not the panel:** it supersedes an **`accepted`** ruling (ADR-0037 D3's NULL
  asymmetry — which becomes *vacuous*, not wrong), it changes a domain type across every call site, and
  **migrations are owner-only**. **Why now:** it is additive and near-free during a regeneration, and a
  backfill-plus-supersede afterwards. **Needs its own ADR** — ADR-0039 does not decide it and does not
  depend on it; A1 ships correctly either way, and if the answer is yes, A19 becomes moot and D3's
  measurement obligation becomes trivial.
- **`Q-AVAIL-04` — worker transparency. [RE-SCOPED BY PANEL — CH-D9] The question is *lawful basis*,
  not notice.** Not *"should cleaners be told"* but *"which basis covers disclosing a worker's
  occupancy to a third party for that third party's convenience"* — and any balancing test must be run
  against D7.4's **quantified** residual (≤20 named cleaners, 24 h per request, 30 req/min per `sub`),
  not against the one-slot story. The platform already carries `ConsentType` + `UserConsent` with
  grant/withdraw, `IpAddress` and `UserAgent`, and cleaners are `User`s, so there is a real posture to
  answer within. **Still not blocking, and now for a reason rather than an assumption:** the most
  likely outcome (cleaners may object/opt out) needs a per-employee suppression flag, and D7.4's
  amendment **reserves `null` for it today** — `false` would leak the opt-out itself, `true` would
  assert what the server declined to evaluate, and `null` already means *not evaluated*. The seam is
  reserved at the cost of one doc comment, so the answer changes text unless it changes nothing.
- **`Q-AVAIL-03` (the seat cap) — ANSWERED 2026-08-03: _"Seats = RequiredEmployees."_** Raised by
  ADR-0037 D9; the full ruling and its consequences are recorded there. Listed here because it changes
  a number this ADR reasons about: **there is no longer a spare seat**, so `MaxEmployees` equals the
  crew the work requires.

  **What that means for this ADR specifically:** the availability check gets *more* load-bearing, not
  less. With a spare seat, a busy preferred cleaner cost the board a seat it could afford to lose;
  with seats capped at the requirement, every seat withheld is a seat the order actually needs to be
  fulfilled. D3's ruling — a busy cleaner earns no hold and no push — was argued on latency alone and
  is now additionally justified on capacity. Invariant H's per-seat restatement is unaffected in
  form, and strictly more important in effect.

  It does **not** reopen `Q-AVAIL-01`: a second cleaner may still join a partly-crewed booking. The
  ruling bounds how many seats exist, not who may fill them.

## Verdict

**CONSENSUS REACHED. Zero blocking challenges remain. Status → `accepted`, 2026-08-03.**

**Sixteen findings: fourteen upheld in whole or part, two rebutted on their remedy or classification
while their substance was upheld, and zero dismissed.** Both challengers did the job — one report
falsified the ADR's central safety claim, the other falsified its central cost claim, and neither
inflated a boundary that held (the disclosure lane recorded nine sound findings it *could not* break,
including the serving-set gate it then argued against using as a control; the cost lane recorded eleven).

**What actually changed, in order of consequence:**

1. **The ADR claimed a bound it did not build, and now builds it.** D5's "two structural limits" were
   one real and one imaginary. Three preconditions (D12) restore it: a rate limit, a **server-side**
   Plus gate on the flag, and an `IsActive` filter on the list. **All three gate A3, not A4** — the
   oracle exists when the *server* answers, and gating the UI would have shipped it. This is the single
   most important line in the verdict: the natural sequencing was the wrong sequencing.
2. **A safety-asymmetric constant with nothing enforcing its safe side is a comment.** D3.4 adds
   `BookingPolicy.MaxBookableOrderSpanHours = 24`, enforced at the single writer, pinned by
   `cap <= floor`. Two lanes found the same lever from opposite ends: the cost lane saw a double
   booking (real, not urgent at a 168 h floor), the disclosure lane saw a binary-search primitive (live
   the day A3 ships). **The disclosure lane is why it blocks.**
3. **The ADR's own verification would have hard-rejected its own end state**, and two more of its steps
   were rituals defending properties that regress silently. Five steps rewritten, three added; verify
   #11 in particular **passed green on a violating implementation**, which is worse than having no step.
4. **The tri-state's third state is now doing three jobs, not one** — unevaluated, non-member (D12.1),
   and reserved for cleaner suppression (CH-D9). One state, three producers, no new concept. That is
   the seam holding, and it is why the Plus gate belongs on the flag rather than the list.
5. **The tree moved and the ADR was wrong about the tree.** A0 shipped; the floor shipped in a
   different assembly with a different number; the convergence shipped in a **better** shape than
   drafted (predicate-shared, early exit kept). Two challenges were answered by `master` rather than by
   argument, one challenge's feared outcome did not occur, and **A1 re-costs M → S because the
   convergence is now a deletion.** D0 exists so the next reader does not implement the draft.

**On the challengers, per the evidence-discipline instruction:** one **under-called** its own finding
(CH-D1's 84 requests assumes a 12-slot grid the server does not enforce — `CleaningDateTimeUtc` is a
free `DateTime`); one **got a direction backwards** (CH-D2's grid-slot remedy under-greys, it does not
over-grey — refuted as A20 while its *finding* was upheld and became D3.4); one **over-called a
classification** (CH-D1's "S5 FAIL" — S5 as written covers auth and side-effecting endpoints, and this
is a read, which is precisely why the gap existed and why the catalog needs the rule rather than the
endpoint needing a scolding); one **slightly over-counted** (CH-Q3's "one caller of four" — the
resolver-via-`CreateOrder` caller has no loop, so there was no N+1 there to collapse). Every one of
those four is a wording correction on top of a finding that stood.
