# ADR-0054 — Cleaner job reminders are three non-mutable events whose "already sent" lives on a stamp **per recipient** — the assignment row for the two per-job reminders, a column of its own for the evening digest — because the outbox key collapses a duplicate only inside one request and turns the next tick into an unhandled 23505

- **Status:** `accepted`
- **Date:** 2026-08-22
- **Supersedes:** —
- **Superseded by:** —
- **Applies to:** backend, mobile

## Context

Cleaners were forgetting jobs they had taken. Nothing in the platform told them: every push on the
partner side was about work *appearing* — the new-jobs digest, the preferred offer, an admin
assignment — and none about work already on their own schedule. The customer, by contrast, has been
getting `SendPreCleaningReminders` at T-1h since it shipped.

Three moments are worth a message, and they are not the same message: the evening before ("you have
two jobs tomorrow"), about two hours out ("this one starts soon"), and shortly before the start when
the cleaner still has not set off. The third is the only one with a precondition beyond the clock.

Two seams are under pressure. First, **mutability**: the partner apps let a user silence categories,
and a reminder about your own booked work is exactly the thing a person who is about to no-show would
mute. Second, **idempotency**: these are timer sweeps, so every message must survive the next tick,
and the platform already has two dedupe mechanisms that look like they should serve — the outbox's
unique `(QueueName, MessageKey)` and the consumer's `ProcessedMessage` claim row. Neither can.

## Decision

**Three non-mutable partner events, driven by two sweeps, each deduped by a stamp written on the row
that identifies the recipient of *that* message.**

| Event key | Cadence | Recipient stamp |
|---|---|---|
| `order.reminder_soon` (`NotificationEventCatalog.cs:87`) | every 5 min, fires at T-110…130 min | `OrderEmployee.ReminderSoonSentAt` (`OrderEmployee.cs:33`) |
| `order.reminder_not_started` (`NotificationEventCatalog.cs:98`) | same sweep, T-25…40 min | `OrderEmployee.ReminderNotStartedSentAt` (`OrderEmployee.cs:41`) |
| `order.reminder_tomorrow` (`NotificationEventCatalog.cs:78`) | hourly, fires at 18:00 **local** | `Employee.LastTomorrowDigestAt` (`Employee.cs:94`) |

**0 — Both per-job reminders are `Confirmed`-gated by ONE shared query term, and neither has a
precondition the other lacks.** `SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:78` puts
`o.CurrentStatus == OrderStatus.Confirmed` on the single query that feeds both windows, so a cleaner
who has already tapped "On my way" receives **neither** the nudge **nor** the two-hour notice. The
shipped test says so: `CleanerReminderSweepTests.cs:114-128` asserts `SoonSent == 0` alongside
`NudgesSent == 0` for `OnTheWay`, `InProgress`, `Completed` and `Cancelled`. This is stated first and
in bold because the obvious "finish the job" edit — moving the status term off the shared query onto
the nudge branch so the notice becomes "unconditional as documented" — would silently widen
`order.reminder_soon` to fire on `Cancelled` and `Completed` orders. That is
`patterns-backend.md §"Moving a gate onto a new column: the term you delete is the outage"`.
The behaviour is currently harmless because ADR-0055 forbids `OnTheWay` before T-60 and the soon
window is T-110…130, so the two cannot overlap today; harmlessness is why it would survive review, not
a reason to leave the record claiming a distinction the code does not make. Three in-tree comments
still assert that distinction and are listed in *Roles affected* as required changes.

**1 — Non-mutable, and each of the three earns it on its own facts.** `GetCategoryFor`
(`NotificationEventCatalog.cs:154-174`) has no arm for any of the three, so all three fall to
`_ => null` — mutability here is granted by an explicit arm and withheld by omission, which is the
right default direction but means the *absence* is the decision and has to be stated somewhere. It is
stated here, **three times, because one justification does not cover three events**:

- **`order.reminder_soon` and `order.reminder_not_started`** inherit the sentence already written
  about `OrderAssigned` (`NotificationEventCatalog.cs:130-132`) — *"a cleaner must not be able to
  silence a job appearing on their own schedule and then not turn up"* — word for word. They are the
  last line before a no-show, and [ADR-0055](./adr-0055) names the nudge as *the* remedy for lateness
  (it deliberately refuses to gate a late cleaner), so silencing them silences the platform's only
  late-cleaner response.
- **`order.reminder_tomorrow` does not inherit that sentence** and is defended separately. It fires
  14+ hours out, it is a count with no per-job action attached, and its supposed safety net — the T-2h
  notice — is a *reminder*, not a *planning* notice. That is the whole difference: the board is
  country-wide by default for a cleaner with no `JobRadiusKm` (`Employee.cs:104-112`) and the radius
  ceiling is 500 km (`JobProximity.cs:49`), so a cleaner with a 90-minute journey learns at T-2h that
  they have 30 minutes of slack. Muting the evening digest does not shift the same information later;
  it removes the only notice that arrives in time to **arrange** the day — transport, childcare, a
  second job. It cannot produce a no-show the T-2h notice would not catch, and that is the wrong test:
  it produces a **lateness** the T-2h notice cannot prevent and which ADR-0055 deliberately declines
  to refuse. That is what the digest's non-mutability buys, and it is not zero.
- **The cost of the digest's arm is real and is now recorded**, not waved past: an unsilenceable 18:00
  push, plus a stacked, undated feed row every evening the cleaner has work — retained 90 days, not
  permanently (see *Consequences*, required change 4, where the first draft's "permanent" and its
  365,000-rows figure are corrected). Whether that trade is the owner's to re-take is escalated as
  **`Q-PUSH-01`**.

The contrast inside the same file remains the point: the preferred-offer push **is** mutable
(`:106-107`), because a cleaner who silenced new-job notifications must not receive a push-shaped
bypass of that mute. Work you have already committed to is not new work.

**2 — The two per-job stamps live on `OrderEmployee`, not on `Order`.** An order's crew is
`ceil(EstimatedTime / 120)` and the seeded catalogue contains single 180- and 240-minute services, so
**two-seat orders exist in production today** — a scalar on `Order` would remind the first cleaner and
silently skip the second, and the failure would be invisible because nobody complains about a push
they never knew was coming. `CompleteOrder` carried a comment asserting crews are always size 1; it
was removed on 2026-08-22 and `CompleteOrder.cs:310-312` now says the opposite. The two
`OrderEmployee` stamps are `??=`, so a re-entrant sweep cannot move one forward and re-open a second
send (`OrderEmployee.cs:44-55`) — this is scoped to those two; the digest watermark is an
unconditional `=` and §3 says why. An unassign hard-DELETEs the row, so a reassignment reminds the
replacement with a fresh null rather than inheriting the previous cleaner's receipt.

> **The fresh null is not free, and it is the one place the stamp and the outbox key disagree.**
> `MessageKeys.Push(userId, eventKey, subject)` (`NotificationProducer.cs:28`, `MessageKeys.cs:25-26`)
> keys on the **user**, not on the assignment row, and a dispatched outbox row is `MarkDispatched`
> (`OutboxDrainerService.cs:63`), never deleted at send time — `PruneOutbox` removes it only after
> `DispatchedRetentionDays`, default **14** (`OutboxRetentionConfig.cs:14`). So
> `push:{userId}:order.reminder_soon:{orderId}` stays taken under
> `IX_OutboxMessages_QueueName_MessageKey` for a fortnight. Reassign A→B and back B→A **inside the
> residual of the same 20-minute soon window**, after that window's reminder has already fired — the
> first draft's "T-100m / T-95m" does not reproduce, because 95 and 100 are outside `[110, 130]` and
> nothing is sent there; the lead corrected the example (2026-08-23), and note the sequence completes
> only on a **guest** order, since on a registered-customer order the second reassignment 500s first
> on `push:{customer}:order.cleaner_assigned:{orderId}` (`TakeOrder.cs:309` vs
> `AdminReassignOrder.cs:107` — the live instance of this same class, see required change 2).
> `AdminReassignOrder.cs:93` is the one production caller of `UnassignEmployee`: A's assignment
> row is new, `ReminderSoonSentAt` is null, the sweep decides to send, and the key collides. The 23505
> is raised inside `unitOfWork.CommitAsync` at `SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:150`, which is **outside**
> the per-item `try` at `:120-136`, so it discards every stamp and every message in that tenant group.
> **The stamp does not avert that failure; the stamp is what unlocks it.** Nothing in the tree would
> catch it — `CleanerReminderSweepTests` mocks `IUnitOfWork` (`:29`) and `INotificationProducer`
> (`:27`), so no outbox row is written and no index is consulted. This is a **required code change**,
> listed in *Consequences*: either the sweep's commit survives a unique violation (the shape
> `TakeOrder.cs:289-307` already uses), or the subject carries something that changes with the
> assignment row.

**3 — The digest gets its own column, not a reuse of `LastNewJobsDigestAt`.** Two writers on one
watermark suppress each other. That is not a hypothesis: the preferred-offer notification already
refuses to stamp `LastNewJobsDigestAt` (`Employee.cs:70`) for precisely this reason, recorded in the
catalog entry itself (`NotificationEventCatalog.cs:103-107`). *"Have I told them about tomorrow"* is a
different question from *"what is new on the board since I last looked"*, and one scalar cannot hold
two answers. It is per-cleaner rather than per-order because the message is a **count over N orders** —
there is no single order it is about, so no order column could express it.

**4 — The digest sweep runs hourly and selects on the cleaner's LOCAL hour.** An Azure timer fires on
a UTC cron and cannot be timezone-aware, so the cron is `0 0 * * * *` (`appsettings.json:6`) and the
handler keeps only cleaners whose local clock has just struck 18 (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:151-154`).
The zone comes from `Employee.WorkCountryId` → `CountryConfiguration.TimeZoneId`, resolved through
`TimeZoneResolution` (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:94-100`), which never throws and falls to UTC on an
unknown id (`TimeZoneResolution.cs:17-19`). One lookup per distinct country, not per cleaner. The
`X-Time-Zone` header is unauthenticated and is banned on any path that decides something — it is
read-only presentation, and this decides whether a person is disturbed. The watermark is compared in
the cleaner's own zone too (`:156-164`): around midnight UTC and local disagree about which day it is,
and a UTC comparison sends a second digest for the same evening to everyone east of Greenwich.

**4a — DST is the other half of that seam, and the arithmetic does not survive it.** The tomorrow
window is built by handing **local midnight** to `TimeZoneInfo.ConvertTimeToUtc`
(`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:168-170`). `TimeZoneResolution` does not protect that call — it guards only
`FindSystemTimeZoneById` against `TimeZoneNotFoundException` / `InvalidTimeZoneException`
(`TimeZoneResolution.cs:25-36`). In a zone whose DST transition is *at* midnight that `DateTime` does
not exist on spring-forward day and `ConvertTimeToUtc` throws `ArgumentException`; the throw is
swallowed by the per-cleaner `catch (Exception)` at `:120-125`, so an entire country loses its digest
as a `LogWarning`, annually. On the fall-back side local midnight is *ambiguous* and
`ConvertTimeToUtc` resolves to the **standard** offset, so `windowStart` lands an hour late and jobs
booked in the repeated hour are undercounted — silently, in the count that is the whole payload.

**Three magnitudes in the first draft of this paragraph were wrong and the lead corrected them
(2026-08-23), each re-derived by executing the conversion rather than by reasoning about it:**

- **The zone list.** Paraguay, Jordan and Azerbaijan **no longer observe DST at all** (measured
  Jan-15 vs Jul-15 2026 offsets: −03:00/−03:00, +03:00/+03:00, +04:00/+04:00) and must come out. The
  live midnight-transition zones are **America/Santiago, America/Havana, Asia/Beirut** — and three
  the draft missed: **Atlantic/Azores, America/Scoresbysund, Africa/Cairo**.
- **"Every seeded market is European, so local midnight is always valid" is the wrong predicate, and
  it invites exactly the wrong inference.** Atlantic/Azores is **EU**, on the identical 01:00-UTC
  transition rule, with a standard offset of **UTC−1** — so the EU transition *is* local midnight
  there (invalid 2026-03-29, 2027-03-28, 2028-03-26, 2029-03-25, 2030-03-31). The safe predicate is
  **non-negative UTC offset**, not "European". Ship Portugal and this is live.
- **The outage is two consecutive evenings, not one**, and the fall-back has two directions, not one.
  Replicating `:168-170` at 18:00 local for America/Havana 2026: the send on **S-2** throws building
  `windowEnd`, the send on **S-1** throws building `windowStart`. On fall-back, the 10-30 send
  produces a **25-hour** window (an over-count) in addition to the 10-31 under-count. Both are
  confined to jobs scheduled 00:00–01:00 local, a slot with effectively no cleaning jobs.

This is **latent, not live, and further from live than the draft implied**: every local midnight of
all ten seeded `TimeZoneId`s (`sql-scripts/insert_seed_data.sql:944-1044` — CZE, SVK, POL, DEU, AUT,
GBR, FRA, ITA, ESP, USA) was scanned for 2026–2030 and **none** is invalid or ambiguous, including
America/New_York (02:00) and Europe/London (01:00); only CZE is `IsServiced` (`:140`); and there is
**no application write path to `CountryConfiguration.TimeZoneId`** at all — only the domain factory
and the seed SQL. It goes live the first time a seed or a migration names a midnight-transition zone,
which is precisely the event §4 exists to be ready for. **The constraint is recorded here and the fix
is a required code change** (listed in *Consequences*): map a local wall-clock day boundary to the
**earliest real instant** for that boundary — `IsAmbiguousTime` → `GetAmbiguousTimeOffsets().Max()`,
`IsInvalidTime` → advance to the first instant that exists, otherwise `GetUtcOffset` — which is the
one rule that is correct at *both* ends of the window and removes the over-count as well as the
under-count.

> **The dedupe that already existed looked like it would do — and it cannot.** Both sends pass a
> deterministic subject to `NotifyAsync`: the order id (`SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:175`) and, for the
> digest, `localToday` — **the local date the digest is SENT on** (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:159`,
> passed at `:196`), *not* the date being summarised, which is `localTomorrowStart` one day later
> (`:168`). As a value that is the better of the two, because it is the same key the watermark
> compares against (`:159-164`) so the two agree by construction; as a **documented formula** the
> distinction matters, because `MessageKeys` is the frozen registry (`MessageKeys.cs:8-13`) and this
> repo reads `MessageKey` **structurally**, not only by equality — `OutboxMessageRepository.cs:32-47`
> derives a per-subject prefix from the frozen `MessageKeys.Email` formula for GDPR erasure. A record
> that misstates which date a frozen key carries is the input to the next erasure or reconciliation
> predicate. The code's own comment at `SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:194-196` repeats the same wrong
> description and is a required change. That makes the outbox row exactly
> one-per-(cleaner, event, subject), which is the whole shape of a dedupe key — so storing a stamp as
> well looks redundant. It fails twice. `OutboxPendingDispatch` collapses a repeat only **inside one
> request**: `_seen` is a field on a scoped instance (`OutboxPendingDispatch.cs:25`), empty again at
> the top of the next tick. Across ticks the only thing left is the unique index
> `IX_OutboxMessages_QueueName_MessageKey` (`OutboxMessageEntityConfiguration.cs:40-45`), which speaks
> as a Postgres 23505 raised **inside `unitOfWork.CommitAsync`** (`SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:150`,
> `SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:128`) — a line with **no catch**, outside the per-item `try`. One repeat
> would abort the whole tenant group's commit, discarding every stamp and every other message in it.
> And `ProcessedMessage` is the **consumer's** guard, written when the message is drained
> (`ProcessedMessage.cs:11-15`); it dedupes *delivery*, and cannot stop an *enqueue* that has not
> happened yet. A deterministic key is still worth passing — it is what makes the two mechanisms
> coherent — but the sweep must not need it to be correct.
>
> **And the stamp does not arbitrate a CONCURRENT sweep, only the next tick — latent, not live.** In
> `SendTomorrowJobDigest` the watermark is written *after* the enqueue and inside the same uncommitted
> transaction (`:186-200`, committed at `:128`), so two sweeps running in the same hour would both
> read `LastDigestAt == null`, both enqueue the identical key, and the second would 23505 inside
> `CommitAsync`. Everything argued above is argued against the *next* tick; against a *simultaneous*
> one the only arbiter is the unique index, and it speaks on the uncaught line. **The lead re-derived
> the reachability (2026-08-23): there is no second sweep today.** `SendTomorrowJobDigestFunction.cs:17`
> is `TimerTrigger`-only, `Cleansia.Functions` exposes no HTTP or manual trigger for it,
> `functionApp.bicep` declares no staging slot, and a Functions timer trigger holds a singleton
> listener lock. It becomes live the day any of those three changes. Same required change as §2's
> reassignment collision.

## Alternatives considered

- **Two features instead of one for the per-job pair.** They share a query, a cadence, a tenant loop
  and a stamp discipline, and differ only in which window an assignment falls into. Two features
  means two timers, two cron app-settings and two near-identical files to keep in step — and the
  first drift is silent. One sweep, one query spanning both windows (`SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:74-84`).
- **A daily cron for the digest, at 17:00 UTC.** Correct for every seeded market today, because all of
  them are CET. It is wrong the first day a non-CET country is added, and by then the digest would
  have been arriving at the wrong hour for months with nobody able to say when it broke. The seam this
  protects is `CountryConfiguration`-driven variation, which does not survive a hard-coded hour.
- **Reuse `LastNewJobsDigestAt` for the evening digest.** One fewer column, and it re-creates the
  exact suppression the preferred-offer entry already refuses to cause.
- **A stamp on `Order` for the per-job reminders.** One column instead of two on a smaller table —
  and it silently drops the second cleaner on every two-seat job.
- **Make the reminders mutable under a new "reminders" category.** Consistent with how the apps treat
  everything else, and it hands the cleaner most likely to no-show the switch that stops us reminding
  them.
- **Send the digest when the count is zero.** Rejected: no jobs tomorrow is not news, and a digest
  saying "0" trains cleaners to ignore the one that says 2. The watermark is deliberately **not**
  stamped in that case (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:178-184`) — **but the recovery that justifies not
  stamping it does not exist today**, and the first draft of this entry repeated the code's own false
  comment. Under the equality test at `:151` there is no next hourly tick: hour 19 is rejected before
  the count is ever taken, so a cleaner with no jobs at 18:00 who takes one at 18:30 is **never** told.
  This is the guaranteed-today arm of required change 1, it needs no failure of any kind, and the
  bounded catch-up window is what makes this sentence true.
- **Resolve the countries' local hour BEFORE reading `Employees`, and return early when no configured
  country is at `LocalSendHour`.** Not rejected — **adopted as the named follow-on**, and it should
  have been in the first draft. The candidate query materializes every Approved/Active cleaner with a
  work country on every hourly tick (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:76-84`) and the hour test that discards
  23 ticks in 24 happens afterwards, in memory (`:151`). The information that decides whether *any*
  work exists lives in `CountryConfiguration.TimeZoneId`, read through a repository the handler already
  injects (`:64`), so this is a re-order of two existing steps with no new collaborator. It was left
  out of the first draft because "acceptable at current scale" was true; it is in *Consequences* now
  with a **number** attached rather than a feeling, because there is no meter that will ever say
  otherwise.
- **A self-healing send hour instead of `==`.** Not rejected — **adopted as a required change**, and
  its absence is the sharpest hole in the first draft. See *Consequences*: the equality test gives the
  digest exactly one attempt per day per zone, and every way of losing that attempt is silent.
  **The first draft prescribed a bare `localNow.Hour >= LocalSendHour` and said it "costs nothing".
  The lead re-derived that and it is wrong on three counts (2026-08-23), so the adopted form is a
  BOUNDED catch-up window, not an open `>=`:**
  1. An unbounded `>=` fires on ticks 18…23 local. `ReminderTomorrow` has no `GetCategoryFor` arm
     (`NotificationEventCatalog.cs:154-174`, re-opened — it falls to `_ => null`) and there is no
     quiet-hours suppression anywhere in `src/`, so it produces an **unsilenceable 23:00 push**. §1
     defends the digest's non-mutability on the ground that it *"arrives in time to arrange the day —
     transport, childcare, a second job"*; a 23:00 digest for a day starting in nine hours forfeits
     the only justification the record gives for making it unsilenceable.
  2. The per-cleaner `CountAsync` at `:172-176` runs **after** the hour test and **before** the
     `count == 0` return at `:181`, so an open `>=` multiplies it by six for the zero-job majority —
     the read cost this same ADR sizes elsewhere, paid on the cleaners who get no message.
  3. It **unlocks a 23505 that `==` structurally cannot reach.** The stamp at `:199-200` is
     `employee?.MarkTomorrowDigestSent(...)` — null-conditional — and the catch at `:120-125` does not
     roll back the outbox row already tracked at `:186`, so a null or a throw between the two commits
     a message with no watermark. Under `==` that state is inert. Under an open `>=` the next tick
     re-enqueues the identical key and the unique index speaks on the uncaught commit at `:128`.
  The adopted shape is `LocalSendHour ≤ localNow.Hour < LocalSendHour + CatchUpHours` with
  `CatchUpHours = 3` (validated 1–6): three attempts, matching the sibling sweep's ~3, stopping at
  21:00. Required change 1 states it, and states that the bare `>=` is superseded so the next reader
  does not "simplify" it back.
- **Two OR'd windows and a stamp-null term in SQL, instead of one continuous 105-minute span.** Not
  rejected — **adopted as a required change**. The first draft weighed only round trips
  (*"cheaper than two round trips"*) and never rows materialized, which is the term that grows with the
  business. The reference implementation is in the same folder: `SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:68` prunes
  on `o.PreCleaningReminderSentAt == null` **in SQL** and `Include`s nothing (`:65-74`).
- **Nudge on "this cleaner has not set off", not on "this order is not `OnTheWay`".** Not rejected —
  **adopted as a required change**. The shipped predicate is about the reminded order
  (`SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:78`), which is not the proposition the copy states. See *Consequences*.
- **One recipient predicate shared by all three keys.** Not rejected — **adopted as a required
  change**. Today the digest filters `Approved || Active` (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:79-81`) and the
  per-job sweep filters the employee on nothing at all (`:77-84`, pushing to `assignment.Employee?.UserId`
  at `:114`). See *Consequences*.

## Consequences

**Cheaper.** A reminder is per-person by construction, so crew size stops being a correctness
question. Adding a fourth reminder is a stamp and a window, not a new mechanism. A non-CET country
needs a `TimeZoneId` in its `CountryConfiguration` and nothing else.

**More expensive.** Two more columns on `OrderEmployee` and one on `Employee`, all nullable
(`20260822150939_Initial.cs:1783-1784`, `:870`). Three more locKeys in three partner clients — web,
Android and iOS — and each must exist in all five locales or the push arrives with a raw key. Two
more timer schedules to deploy; a sweep whose cron is absent does not fail, it silently never runs.

**The digest sweep's read cost, with a number instead of a feeling.** It walks every Approved/Active
cleaner with a work country on every hourly tick to answer a question that is false on 23 of 24 ticks:
100 cleaners = 2,400 rows/day ≈ 876 k/year; 1,000 = 24,000/day ≈ 8.8 M/year; 10,000 = 240,000/day
≈ 88 M/year. The first draft filed this as *"acceptable at current scale, and the first thing to
revisit when it is not"* — a deferral with no trigger, no metric and no owner, and there is nothing
that will ever say "now": the only custom meters subscribed anywhere are `Cleansia.Integration` and
`Cleansia.RateLimiting` (`src/Cleansia.ServiceDefaults/Extensions.cs:59-60`). **The trigger is
therefore a number, not an alert: at 1,000 approved cleaners the country-first re-order in
*Alternatives considered* ships.** It is a re-order of two existing steps and needs no new
collaborator.

**Required code changes this decision names rather than hides.** Each is a defect in shipped code, not
in this record; none is fixed by an ADR:

1. **The digest gets exactly one attempt per day per zone, and loses a whole market silently when it
   misses.** `localNow.Hour != command.LocalSendHour` (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:151`) is an equality
   test on one hourly tick. **The lead re-derived every arm of this in the tree (2026-08-23); the
   claim survives and its shape changed in three places:**
   - **The arm that is guaranteed today and that the first draft never named** is the `count == 0`
     branch at `:178-184`. A cleaner with no jobs at the 18:00 tick who takes a job for tomorrow that
     evening is **never told** — `:151` rejects hour 19 before the count is retaken. No failure, no
     misfire and no scale is required; `SendNewJobsDigest` runs on the same hourly cron, so the
     platform actively pushes cleaners to take evening jobs it will then never digest. The code
     comment at `:178-180`, the test doc-comment at `TomorrowJobDigestTests.cs:162-166` and this ADR's
     own *Alternatives* entry all claimed this case recovers. It does not. **This is the fifth false
     in-tree comment** (see *Roles affected*).
   - **Narrower than stated:** a failure *inside* `TrySendAsync` does **not** abort the group —
     `:120-125` catches and continues. Only a throw raised **at** the commit on `:128` qualifies, and
     the 23505 route needs two concurrent sweeps, which the Functions timer's singleton listener lease
     makes unlikely on a single Function App with no staging slot. The timer-misfire arm is narrower
     too: the Functions `ScheduleMonitor` issues a catch-up invocation on host start, so a sub-hour
     outage recovering at 18:07 local still satisfies `Hour == 18`; losing the day needs an outage
     that straddles the hour boundary.
   - **Wider than stated:** when `:128` does throw, **nothing swallows it** —
     `AuditFailureCaptureBehavior` rethrows (`:55`), the UnitOfWork behaviour has no catch, the timer
     handler has no catch, and `FunctionInvocationErrorMiddleware` rethrows by design. So it kills
     every **remaining** tenant group and fails the invocation, not only the current group. Today the
     distinction is moot and the phrase *"a whole market"* is if anything an understatement: one
     serviced country (CZE, `insert_countries.sql:74`), all CET, single tenant ⇒ exactly **one** group
     at `:103` ⇒ the whole platform's digest for that evening.
   Contrast the sibling sweep in this same decision: a 15-minute **range** at a 5-minute cadence gives
   ~3 attempts and prunes on its own stamp **in SQL** (`SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:62-63`, `:68`);
   this gives one and prunes in memory. Fix: the **bounded catch-up window** of *Alternatives
   considered* — **not** the bare `>=` the first draft prescribed, which is wrong for the three
   reasons recorded there — plus the existing watermark check, plus the stamp/commit pairing (drop the
   `CleanerCandidate` projection and materialize the tracked `Employee` in the candidate query, which
   also deletes the N+1 re-read at `:199`). Scale makes the commit arm likelier, not rarer: at 10,000
   cleaners in one zone the 18:00 tick accumulates 10,000 `CountAsync` round trips, 10,000
   `GetByIdIgnoringTenantAsync` re-reads, 10,000 tracked employees and 10,000 outbox rows into **one**
   transaction.
2. **The outbox key survives the assignment row that the stamp resets** (§2's block quote).
   **Reachability resized by the lead (2026-08-23), in both directions.** The reminder-key collision
   as this ADR describes it is far narrower than the draft implied: it needs a **guest** order
   (`Order.UserId` null — on a registered-customer order the second reassignment dies first on
   `push:{customer}:order.cleaner_assigned:{orderId}`), **two** hand-typed admin reassignments, both
   landing inside the residual of the same 20-minute soon window after that reminder already fired,
   and a tick in between. On today's deployment that is effectively nil. The two-concurrent-sweeps
   variant is **not reachable at all** — timer trigger only, no HTTP or manual trigger anywhere in
   `Cleansia.Functions`, one Function App with no staging slot, and the timer holds a singleton
   listener lock. **But the same mechanism ships broken on `master` today at a different call site**:
   `TakeOrder.cs:309` and `AdminReassignOrder.cs:107` mint the byte-identical customer key, so any
   admin reassigning a registered-customer order whose cleaner took it inside the 14-day retention
   window gets a **500 and no reassignment**. That is the instance that earns the fix, and it is why
   the fix belongs at the seam — `OutboxPendingDispatch.Enqueue` making a re-mint of an identical
   `(QueueName, MessageKey)` a no-op, which is by construction the same logical effect the drainer
   already sent — rather than as a `catch` bolted onto each sweep's commit.
3. **DST**: local midnight handed to `ConvertTimeToUtc` (§4a) — **latent, not live**, with the zone
   list, the two-evening outage and the fall-back over-count corrected there. Fix it once, in
   `TimeZoneResolution`, because a **second live caller already exists**: `GetDashboardStats.cs:64-81`
   and `:239-243` do the identical local-midnight conversion on a zone taken from the unvalidated
   `X-Time-Zone` header (`UserSessionProvider.cs:39-50`, no allow-list), which an authenticated caller
   can 500 today by naming a midnight-transition zone on its transition day. That header finding is
   routed to the Security Reviewer separately; it is not this decision's to rule on.
4. **`order.reminder_tomorrow` stacks an undated feed row every evening a cleaner has work.**
   `ReminderTomorrow` is in the Partner feed keyset (`NotificationFeedEventKeys.cs:49`), so every send
   also writes a `UserNotification` — and `NotificationProducer.UpsertFeedRowAsync` special-cases
   **only** `NewJobsAvailable` for `RefreshDigest` (`NotificationProducer.cs:47-56`); every other key
   takes the `Add` path at `:58`. **The mechanism is confirmed; three of the first draft's four
   supporting claims are not, and the lead corrected them (2026-08-23):**
   - **"Permanent" is false.** `DataRetentionBackgroundService.cs:242-263` hard-deletes every
     `UserNotification` older than 90 days (`RetentionDefaults.DefaultNotificationsDays = 90`) and
     `:265-285` prunes any user beyond the newest 500. The resident set is bounded.
   - **"365,000 rows/year" is wrong three ways.** It is a *write* count, not a table size; the digest
     sends only when the count is non-zero (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:181-184`), i.e. ~250 days a
     year, not 365; and 1,000 cleaners is not this platform. At the ~25-cleaner reality it is about
     **6,250 writes/year and ~1,600 resident rows** (~1 MB). Even at the draft's own 1,000 cleaners
     the honest figures are ~250,000 writes/year and ~64,000 resident.
   - **"The unread badge counts every row in the keyset" is a doc comment, not the query.** The badge
     is `UserNotificationRepository.cs:21-27`, which counts only `ReadOn == null`, and a single visit
     to the inbox zeroes it (`NotificationsViewModel.kt:142-153`,
     `NotificationsInboxViewModel.swift:114`), with the display capped at "99+". "A badge reading 365
     by year end" cannot happen.
   - **"The exemption `NewJobsAvailable` was given exists for exactly this" is false.**
     `NewJobsAvailable` is *hourly* with no per-day cap — up to 24 rows/cleaner/day — against this
     digest's hard cap of one per cleaner per local day. Twenty-four times the collapse pressure.
   **The harm that survives is the one the draft did not name, and it is the one that is visible:**
   `:189-192` puts only `["count"]` in the args, with **no date**, and the client renders *"Jobs
   tomorrow: %1$d"* (`strings.xml:1263`) — so Monday's row still reads "Jobs tomorrow: 3" on Thursday,
   with nothing in the payload to disambiguate. A stack of contradictory claims about a moving date is
   a correctness defect, not a storage one. Fix: **the `RefreshDigest` arm, not removal from the
   keyset** — removal fights three artifacts that encode the opposite decision on purpose
   (`NotificationFeedEventKeysTests.cs:88-97`, `:31-57`, `FeedKeysetClientReadinessTests.cs:75-92`)
   and strands shipped client copy on both platforms, deleting the recovery path for a cleaner who
   missed the push. Widen the `if` at `NotificationProducer.cs:47` to a membership test against a
   named `CollapsingDigestKeys` set so a third count digest inherits the collapse by construction
   rather than by a third `if`. Note the coverage hole this exposes: `RefreshDigest`/`UpsertFeedRowAsync`
   have **zero** test references in the tree, so the existing `NewJobsAvailable` collapse is unproven.
5. **The nudge's precondition is about the order, not about the cleaner, and it misfires on any
   cleaner with two same-day jobs.** `order.reminder_not_started` fires while the *reminded order* is
   `Confirmed` (`SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:78`), which is not the proposition *"this cleaner has not
   set off"*. **The lead re-derived the trigger and the harm (2026-08-23): the trigger is broader than
   the draft's example and two of its three severity claims are false.**
   - **Broader trigger.** `HasOverlappingOrderAsync` is a **half-open** interval test
     (`OrderRepository.cs:346-350`), so it admits **zero-gap** back-to-back work as well as the
     draft's 30-minute gap: 10:00 + 120 min ends at 12:00, a 12:00 booking is permitted because
     `12:00 > 12:00` is false. On an hourly booking grid (`BookingPolicy.cs:42-43`) that is the
     natural shape, and there the nudge at 11:20–11:35 fires while job 1 is *scheduled* to run to
     12:00 — deterministically, not probabilistically. The worked example is corrected to that pair.
   - **"They cannot act on it" is false**, and so is *"the platform is asking for something it would
     itself refuse"*. The message asks the cleaner to **set off** (`strings.xml:1267`), not to start.
     `NotifyOnTheWay` has **no** concurrent-`InProgress` gate (`NotifyOnTheWay.cs:42-56`) and
     `IsTooEarlyToStart` permits the tap from T-60 (`BookingPolicy.cs:156-159`), so the requested
     action is available throughout the nudge window. The `StartOrder.cs:117-128` citation the author
     added as *"the evidence that makes it non-arguable"* is evidence about the wrong verb, and this
     ADR's own line about the nudge being skipped for a cleaner who taps "On my way" before T-40
     already contradicted it.
   - **Blast radius is one push**, ever, per (cleaner, order): `MarkReminderNotStartedSent` is `??=`
     (`OrderEmployee.cs:51-55`). Not a repeating stream.
   **The residual harm, restated at the size the tree supports:** the message is factually wrong about
   the recipient's state, and a cleaner who *complies* fires `order.on_the_way` to job 2's customer
   (`NotifyOnTheWay.cs:136-147`) while still mid-job somewhere else — telling that customer something
   untrue. Fix: exclude cleaners holding another assignment `OnTheWay`/`InProgress` at the nudge
   instant, on the **nudge branch only**. Do **not** move the `Confirmed` term at `:78` to achieve it
   (§0). Do **not** reuse `GetBusyEmployeeIdsInWindowAsync`: its `SlotBlockingStatuses` set includes
   `Confirmed` (`OrderRepository.cs:270-277`) and would suppress every nudge.
6. **The single-span query loads every order in the window ~21 times to send at most 2 messages.**
   `SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:74-84` scans one continuous 105-minute span every 5 minutes with
   `.Include(AssignedEmployees).ThenInclude(Employee)` to read one field, `assignment.Employee?.UserId`
   (`:114`). An order sits in that span for 21–22 ticks and does useful work on at most 2. **Four
   numbers in the first draft were wrong and the lead corrected them (2026-08-23):**
   - **70 of the 105 minutes**, not 65, are in *neither* window (105 − 20 soon − 15 nudge) — the draft
     named the correct interval, "T-40 to T-110", in the same sentence and then miscomputed it, and
     the error **understated** its own case (66.7% dead, not 61.9%).
   - **The two prescribed terms remove ~60%, not ~90%.** The uncorrelated
     `Any(ae => ae.ReminderSoonSentAt == null || ae.ReminderNotStartedSentAt == null)` is close to a
     no-op, because the nudge stamp is written last and the term stays true through the entire soon
     window and the entire dead gap. Ninety percent needs each stamp **correlated with its own range**:
     `(soon range && Any(ReminderSoonSentAt == null)) || (nudge range && Any(ReminderNotStartedSentAt == null))`.
   - **"Full `Order` aggregates" overstates it.** `Order` exposes eight collection navigations plus
     four references and the chain pulls exactly **one**; with no lazy-loading proxies configured
     anywhere, what materializes is the Order row + its `OrderEmployee` rows + each crew member's
     `Employee` row. Three tables. And **tracking is required here**, not gratuitous — `:126`/`:133`
     write the stamps and `:150` commits — so the fix is to load less, never to add `AsNoTracking`.
   - **The D=2,000 / D=20,000 volumes are unanchored** — no orders-per-day figure exists anywhere in
     the repo. Re-derived against the bookable day: rows in the span at any instant ≈ D × 105/720, so
     ~**3 orders per tick at 20 orders/day** and ~29 at 200/day, served as an index range scan by
     `IX_Orders_CurrentStatus_CleaningDateTime` (`OrderEntityConfiguration.cs:130`, whose own comment
     names this predicate shape) — so the cost is proportional to matching rows, not to table size.
     The handler's *"the handful of rows this returns"* (`:72`) is factually correct today.
   Fix: two OR'd ranges with per-range stamp pruning, per `SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:65-74`. **This
   is deferred with a threshold rather than shipped now** — reopen it when the sweep's `Considered`
   count exceeds ~250 per tick.
7. **The three keys give two different answers to "who is a valid recipient", and neither is stated.**
   The digest filters `Approved || Active` (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:79-81`); the per-job sweep
   filters the employee on **nothing** (`:77-84`, `:114`) — one query serves both per-job keys, so it
   is two answers across three keys, not three. **One of the two directions the draft named is dead
   code, and the lead struck it (2026-08-23):**
   - **LIVE.** A cleaner an admin has just `Rejected` is still on their assignments —
     `RejectEmployee.cs:81` touches no order, and `UnassignEmployee` has one caller — and will be told
     *"your job starts in about two hours"* for work they are barred from. The door that refuses them
     is `[RequireCompleteProfile]` (Partner `OrderController.cs:16`), with `StartOrder.cs:71` as the
     inner backstop; the draft attributed it one gate too deep.
   - **UNREACHABLE.** *"An `Active` cleaner is told how many jobs they have tomorrow"* cannot happen:
     `Employee.UpdateContractStatus` (`Employee.cs:338`) has **zero production callers**, no command
     carries a settable `ContractStatus`, and the reachable set is `{Pending, Approved, Rejected}`. The
     `|| Active` arm is dead code in four places, not a live divergence. **Do not delete
     `ContractStatus.Active`/`.Terminated` under this ADR** — they are on the wire in generated
     clients; that is a separate decision.
   Fix: one predicate — `e.IsActive && e.ContractStatus == ContractStatus.Approved`, matching the four
   money/work gates and picking up `IsActive` for the reason already written at
   `PreferredCleanerHoldResolver.cs:55-57` — declared once beside the existing employee
   specifications, applied in SQL **and** re-tested per assignment before `NotifyAsync` (an `Any()`
   term proves *some* crew member qualifies, not the one being pushed to).
8. **Five in-tree comments assert things the code does not do** — listed in *Roles affected*.
9. **Filed separately, not folded in:** `RejectEmployee.cs:81` leaves a rejected cleaner assigned to
   live `Confirmed` orders and nothing re-offers the seat. The reminder merely makes it audible. That
   is a larger defect than anything above and it is not this decision's.

**The pre-dawn cost, accepted deliberately.** `SendCleanerJobReminders` is pure UTC arithmetic
(`:70-84`, `:100`), which is correct for a *relative* lead and means nothing bounds where a reminder
lands on a wall clock. `BookingPolicy.FirstWindowHour = 8` (`BookingPolicy.cs:42`), so an 08:00 local
booking fires `order.reminder_soon` at **06:00 local** and the nudge at **07:20–07:35 local**; add the
previous evening's digest and one early job costs a cleaner three non-mutable pushes, two of them
before 07:30. There is no quiet-hours suppression anywhere in the notification path (`rg QuietHours`
returns nothing). **This is accepted, not overlooked**: a reminder about an 08:00 job is worth exactly
its lead time, so clamping the *soon* notice to a civil local floor would suppress it precisely for
the bookings where it matters most — the earliest ones, which are the ones a cleaner must leave home
in the dark for. The asymmetry the challenge names is real (this decision reasons about local time for
the 18:00 message and not for the 06:00 one) and the answer is that the digest's hour is a *choice*
while the reminder's hour is a *consequence*. Recorded here so that it is chosen rather than assumed.

**The attention cost runs the other way from the code.** Deleting a cron stops a sweep, so the
mechanism is trivially reversible; the volume is not. This adds three keys to the six partner-targeted
keys in the catalog (`NewJobsAvailable`, `PreferredOffer`, `OrderAssignmentCancelled`, `OrderAssigned`,
`OrderAssignmentRevoked`, `InvoicePaid`) — a 50% increase — and a working cleaner with two jobs a day
now receives a non-mutable **floor of 3** and **ceiling of 5** pushes a day (2 × soon + 1 digest, plus
up to 2 nudges; the nudge is skipped for any cleaner who taps "On my way" before T-40, which ADR-0055
permits from T-60), on top of an hourly `order.new_available`. Once a cleaner learns partner pushes
are mostly ignorable, that lesson is not reversed by deleting a cron — and the messages that pay for
it are `order.assignment_cancelled` and `order.assignment_revoked`, the two that cost a cleaner a
day's earnings if missed and are non-mutable precisely because they must be read. **The T-30m nudge is
the marginal message**: highest false-positive rate (required change 5), lowest cost to remove. If one
of the three has to go, it is that one.

**A trap left standing on purpose.** The tenant loop commits **inside** the group
(`SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:146-150`), because rows are stamped from the ambient tenant at commit
time. Hoisting that commit out of the loop is the platform's oldest sweep bug and it would stamp every
group with the last tenant seen. → `/flows/cross-cutting`

## How a reviewer verifies compliance

- `GetCategoryFor` has no arm for any of the three keys; adding one is a behaviour change to a
  decision, not a config tweak, and needs an ADR.
- Every send is guarded by its own stamp's null check and writes that stamp in the same iteration; a
  second `Handle` on unchanged state sends nothing (`CleanerReminderSweepTests.cs:87-96`).
- **The two `OrderEmployee` stamps are `??=`; the digest watermark is `=`, and that asymmetry is the
  decision.** `MarkReminderSoonSent` / `MarkReminderNotStartedSent` (`OrderEmployee.cs:44-55`) are
  first-stamp-wins because a per-job reminder is sent once, ever.
  `Employee.MarkTomorrowDigestSent` (`Employee.cs:96-101`) is an unconditional overwrite because it is
  a **recurring daily watermark**: `??=` there would freeze every cleaner at their first digest
  forever — a permanent platform-wide outage of the feature this ADR exists to add. A reviewer who
  applies the `??=` rule to the digest is shipping that outage.
- Both reminders are `Confirmed`-gated by **one** shared query term (`SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:78`).
  Moving that term onto the nudge branch to "finish" a difference the comments describe widens
  `order.reminder_soon` onto `Cancelled` and `Completed` orders.
- No sweep passes a client-supplied timezone, and no handler reads `X-Time-Zone`.
- The tenant override is set per group and `CommitAsync` is called inside the loop.
- The digest's watermark comparison and its tomorrow-window bounds are both computed in the cleaner's
  zone, then converted back to UTC for the query (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:166-176`).
- Both crons are present in deployed app-settings, not only in `appsettings.json`.

- **`OrderEmployee.cs:37-39`** — *"that one is unconditional at T-2h"*, about `ReminderSoonSentAt`.

## Challenge

*(Architect panel, challenger mode, 2026-08-22. Angle: operations, cost and the human consequence —
not code-vs-record, which is the other challenger's pass.)*

### C1 — The digest sweep reads the whole cleaner table to answer a question only the country table can answer
**Angle:** operations
**Why it matters:** The candidate query (`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:76-84`) materializes **every**
Approved/Active cleaner with a work country on **every** hourly tick; the hour test that discards 23
ticks out of 24 happens afterwards, in memory (`:151-154`). Derived cost, at one CET market:

| Cleaners | Rows read/day | Employee scans/day | Rows read/year |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 2,400 | 24 | ~876 k |
| 1,000 | 24,000 | 24 | ~8.8 M |
| 10,000 | 240,000 | 24 | ~88 M |

…to send at most one message per cleaner per day. The information that decides whether **any** work
exists on a given tick lives in `CountryConfiguration.TimeZoneId` — a table with a handful of rows, read
through a repository the handler **already injects** (`countryConfigurationRepository`, `:64`). Reversing
the two steps — resolve every configured country's local hour first, and `return` before touching
`Employees` when none is at `LocalSendHour` — turns 23 of 24 ticks into one small read. That is a
re-order of two existing steps, not a new mechanism, and it needs no new collaborator. *Consequences*
names the cost (*"acceptable at current scale, and the first thing to revisit when it is not"*, `:130-132`)
but the deferral has no trigger, no metric and no owner: the only custom meters subscribed in the tree
are `Cleansia.Integration` and `Cleansia.RateLimiting` (`src/Cleansia.ServiceDefaults/Extensions.cs:59-60`),
so nothing will ever tell anyone that "not now" became "now". A cost accepted without an instrument is a
cost accepted forever.

### C2 — The digest gets exactly one attempt per day and loses the whole market silently when it misses
**Angle:** operations
**Why it matters:** `localNow.Hour != command.LocalSendHour` (`:151`) is an **equality** test evaluated
on a single hourly tick. Two ordinary events lose an entire day's digest for every cleaner in a zone,
and neither raises anything a human sees:
(a) `unitOfWork.CommitAsync` at `:128` sits **outside** the per-cleaner `try/catch` (`:113-125`). One
failure — including the 23505 this ADR documents at length in its own block quote — aborts the whole
tenant group: no `LastTomorrowDigestAt` is stamped and no outbox row survives. At 19:00, `Hour == 19`,
the predicate is false, and nobody is told about tomorrow.
(b) A timer misfire (host scaled to zero, a deployment, a slow tick) landing at 19:0x has identical
effect, with no error at all.
Contrast the sibling sweep in the same decision: 5-minute cadence over a 20-minute window = **4**
attempts per message. This one has **1**. The self-healing predicate is already half-written in this
file — `localNow.Hour >= LocalSendHour` combined with the existing "no digest stamped for this local
date" watermark check (`:159-164`) — and costs nothing extra. It is not in *Alternatives considered*.
Scale makes (a) likelier, not rarer: at 10,000 cleaners in one zone and one tenant, the 18:00 tick runs
10,000 `CountAsync` round trips plus 10,000 `GetByIdIgnoringTenantAsync` re-reads of rows the candidate
query already returned (`:199` — an N+1 against a projection that could have carried the entity), and
accumulates 10,000 tracked employees + 10,000 outbox rows + 10,000 feed rows into **one** transaction
before committing. One failure in any of them costs the market its day.

### C3 — `order.reminder_tomorrow` writes a permanent feed row per cleaner per day, and is denied the collapse the digest it copies was given
**Angle:** operations
**Why it matters:** `ReminderTomorrow` is in the Partner feed keyset (`NotificationFeedEventKeys.cs:49`),
so every send also writes a `UserNotification`. `NotificationProducer.UpsertFeedRowAsync` special-cases
**only** `NewJobsAvailable` for `RefreshDigest` (`NotificationProducer.cs:47-56`); every other key takes
the `Add` path at `:58`. So the platform's second recurring **count** digest is the one that does not
collapse. Consequence: one permanent row per cleaner per day — 1,000 cleaners = **365,000 rows/year** in
`UserNotifications` — and, because the unread badge *"counts every row in the keyset"*
(`NotificationFeedEventKeys.cs:21-23`), a cleaner who does not open the feed watches their badge climb by
one every evening, reading "365" by year end. The exemption `NewJobsAvailable` was given exists precisely
to stop a repeated digest inflating that badge. The ADR reasons carefully about which stamp dedupes the
*send* and not at all about what the *feed* accumulates. Note the file's own comment (`:46-48`) correctly
keeps `ReminderSoon`/`ReminderNotStarted` out of the feed as transient — the one key that stayed in is
the one whose shape most needs `RefreshDigest`.

### C4 — Non-mutable is defensible for two of the three; the digest inherits a justification that does not transfer
**Angle:** operations
**Why it matters:** The sentence being borrowed is about `OrderAssigned`
(`NotificationEventCatalog.cs:130-132`): a job *appearing* on your schedule **by someone else's action**,
which you could silence and then not turn up to. `ReminderSoon` and `ReminderNotStarted` inherit it
cleanly — they are the last line before a no-show, and ADR-0055 names the nudge as *the* remedy for
lateness (`adr-0055.md:63-65`), so silencing it silences the platform's only late-cleaner response.
`ReminderTomorrow` does not inherit it, on three counts: it fires **14+ hours** before any job; it is a
count with no per-job action attached; and its own safety net — the T-2h reminder — is itself
non-mutable and fires regardless. Muting the evening digest cannot produce a no-show the T-2h notice
would not catch, so non-mutability there buys **zero** and costs an unsilenceable 18:00 push plus the
daily feed row of C3. The record grants all three the exemption in one move — *"`GetCategoryFor` has no
arm for any of the three, so all three fall to `_ => null`"* (`:43-52`) — and states the absence as a
single decision. Three events, one justification, tested once: that is the same composition failure
ADR-0053 documents about the ladder (a defensible policy plus a defensible implementation that bite
only together). Grant the digest a category, or defend it on its own facts.

### C5 — For the platform's earliest bookable window the T-2h notice lands at 06:00 local, unsilenceable, with no local-time concept in the sweep at all
**Angle:** operations
**Why it matters:** `SendCleanerJobReminders` is pure UTC arithmetic (`:70-84`, `:100`). That is correct
for a *relative* lead, and it means nothing whatsoever bounds where a reminder lands on a wall clock.
`BookingPolicy.FirstWindowHour = 8` (`BookingPolicy.cs:42`), so an 08:00 local booking fires
`order.reminder_soon` at **06:00 local** and `order.reminder_not_started` at **07:20–07:35 local**. Add
the previous evening's 18:00 digest and one 08:00 job costs a cleaner **three non-mutable pushes, two of
them before 07:30**. There is no quiet-hours suppression anywhere in the notification path. The
asymmetry is the tell: this ADR reasons hard about the cleaner's local timezone for the message that
arrives at 18:00 (§4; `TimeZoneResolution.Resolve` over `CountryConfiguration.TimeZoneId`,
`SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:94-100`) and not at all for the two that can arrive at dawn. The fix reuses machinery this decision already built. Either clamp
the *soon* reminder to a local floor, or record in *Consequences* that a pre-dawn unmuteable push is an
accepted cost — the record currently does neither, which means nobody chose it.

### C6 — The nudge's precondition is about the order, not about the cleaner, and it misfires hardest on the busiest cleaners
**Angle:** operations
**Why it matters:** `order.reminder_not_started` fires while the *reminded order* is `Confirmed`
(`SendCleanerJobReminders.cs:78`, the single query's status term). That is not the proposition *"this
cleaner has not set off"*; it is *"this cleaner has not set off **for this order**"*. Scenario, entirely
ordinary: jobs at 10:00–12:30 and 13:00. At 12:20–12:35 the cleaner is `InProgress` on job 1 — which is

### C11 — The per-assignment stamp resets on reassignment. The outbox key does not. The collision lands on the exact uncaught `CommitAsync` this ADR's own block quote warns about

**Why it matters:** `MessageKeys.Push(userId, eventKey, subject)` (`NotificationProducer.cs:28`) —

### C12 — DST is not addressed once, and one arm of it throws inside the very conversion the local-hour design rests on

**Angle:** correctness

**Why it matters:** `SendTomorrowJobDigest.cs:168-170` hands **local midnight** to
`TimeZoneInfo.ConvertTimeToUtc`. In every zone whose DST transition is *at* midnight — Chile, Cuba,
Paraguay, Lebanon, Jordan, Azerbaijan, historically Brazil — that `DateTime` does not exist on
spring-forward day, and `ConvertTimeToUtc` throws `ArgumentException("The supplied DateTime represents
an invalid time")`. `TimeZoneResolution` does not protect this: it guards
`FindSystemTimeZoneById` against `TimeZoneNotFoundException`/`InvalidTimeZoneException`
(`TimeZoneResolution.cs:25-36`) and has nothing to do with this call. The throw is swallowed by the
per-cleaner `catch (Exception)` at `:120-125`, so an entire country loses its digest that evening as a
`LogWarning` nobody reads — and it recurs annually. On the fall-back side, local midnight is
*ambiguous*; `ConvertTimeToUtc` resolves ambiguity to the **standard** offset, so `windowStart` lands
an hour late and jobs booked in the repeated hour are undercounted, silently, in the count that is the
message's entire payload. The whole point of §4 and of the rejected "daily cron at 17:00 UTC"
alternative is that a hard-coded CET assumption does not survive the first non-CET country. DST is the
other half of that same seam, and CET is exactly the zone that hides it (its transition is at 02:00,
so local midnight is always valid). The record should either name the constraint ("`CountryConfiguration.TimeZoneId`
must not name a midnight-transition zone") or the arithmetic should be done on `DateTimeOffset` /
`TimeZoneInfo.IsInvalidTime`-guarded local noon rather than local midnight.

## Discharged — 2026-08-23 · required change 2, and it was larger than this record said

**Required change 2 is done, and the owner asked for it in this branch after all.** It was deferred as
"a pre-existing `master` defect at a different call site, not this branch's to absorb". That deferral
rested on a magnitude that was wrong.

**What the record missed.** The collision was described as needing an admin reassignment. It does not.
`OrderCleanerAssignedNotifier` keys on `push:{customerUserId}:order.cleaner_assigned:{orderId}` — the
customer and the order, with nothing naming *which assignment*. A crew is
`ceil(EstimatedTime / 120)`, so every 180-minute service in the seeded catalogue carries two cleaners,
and **the second cleaner to take an ordinary two-seat job minted a key the first cleaner's take had
already written.** The insert failed at the pipeline's commit, after the handler had returned, so
nothing could catch it; the seat rolled back with the 500. The job could not be fully crewed at all.
That is a core booking path, not an admin edge case, and it was live on `master`.

Two further corrections to this record's account of it. The retry is **deterministic, not transient** —
the surviving row is the *first* assignment's committed row, so every retry fails identically. And it is
**not bounded at 14 days**: `PruneOutbox` deletes only `Dispatched` rows, so a row that dead-letters to
`Failed` poisons its key permanently.

**The fix, and why not the one this record proposed.** Not a durable no-op in `Enqueue` — that turns a
crash into a silently dropped notification, and it would have inverted an executable contract ADR-0045
cites by name (`OrderCleanerAssignedNotificationTests`, which asserts the customer IS told on a
reassign). Instead the **subject** now names the assignment rather than the order, via
`AssignmentNotificationSubject`. `MessageKeys.Push`'s formula is untouched and stays frozen under
ADR-0002 D2.1 — it remains a pure function of its inputs; only what a caller passes as the subject
changed.

The discriminator is the `OrderEmployee` row's own id, not the employee's. An employee id fixes the
two-seat case and the plain reassign and still collides when an admin undoes their own reassign — A, B,
back to A. `UnassignEmployee` hard-deletes the row and `AddAssignedEmployee` creates a fresh one whose
client-generated ULID is available before commit, so the assignment row's id is unique per assignment
*event*, which is what these messages are actually about.

**It covers the reminder keys too**, which this branch introduced and which had the identical shape:
`push:{cleanerUserId}:order.reminder_soon:{orderId}` was one key for every assignment an order ever had,
while the stamps that suppress a re-send live on the assignment row a reassign deletes and recreates —
so the stamp reset, the sweep decided to send, and the key it minted was still taken. §2's block quote
called that out and it is now closed by the same change.

**Still not absorbed, and still true:** required changes 3 (DST) and 6 (query cost) stand deferred on
the magnitudes the lead re-derived, and the other members of the collision family the survey found —
`order.preferred_offer_closed`, `dispute.reply`, `order.refunded` — are separate defects with separate
blast radii and are not touched here.
