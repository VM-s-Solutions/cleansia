# ADR-0055 — A cleaner may set off or start only inside a 60-minute grace window before the booking — one predicate shared by both transitions, no upper bound, and placed **last on the caller-identity chain** so an unassigned caller learns nothing about another customer's schedule

- **Status:** `accepted`
- **Date:** 2026-08-22
- **Supersedes:** —
- **Superseded by:** —
- **Applies to:** backend, mobile, frontend (partner)

## Context

Until 2026-08-22 there was **no clock gate at all** on the two transitions a cleaner drives. Neither
`StartOrder` nor `NotifyOnTheWay` read `CleaningDateTime`, so a cleaner could tap "On my way" — or
"Start" — days ahead of the booking, and the platform accepted it.

> **The gate looked like it was already there.** Both validators carry a status rule that reads like a
> bound — `NotifyOnTheWay.cs:88` is `== Confirmed`, and `StartOrder.cs:101` is
> `is Confirmed or OnTheWay` — and reading either, it is easy to conclude the transition is already
> bounded. It is not. `Confirmed` is the most overloaded status in this domain — *money settled* OR
> *cleaner took it* — and `TakeOrder` writes it the instant a cleaner takes the job, which can be a
> week before the cleaning. A status axis cannot express a clock. The order was startable from the
> moment it was taken, and no test failed, because nothing had ever asserted a relationship between
> `CurrentStatus` and `CleaningDateTime`. → `/domain/order-lifecycle`
>
> **The asymmetry between the two is deliberate and is what closes the chain.** `NotifyOnTheWay` is
> the optional step between `Confirmed` and `InProgress`, and `StartOrder` accepts either as a prior
> state (`NotifyOnTheWay.cs:16-20`). So an early "On my way" writes `OnTheWay`,
> `SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:66` stops matching that order forever, and `StartOrder` still admits it
> — which is exactly how "the early tap cancels the customer's reminder and the job completes anyway"
> closes. A reader who takes the two transitions to be status-symmetric will conclude that a
> `Confirmed → InProgress` transition means the order was `Confirmed` an instant earlier, when it may
> have been `OnTheWay` for the previous hour.

Two consequences followed, and both were silent.

**The customer's own reminder is cancelled by the early tap.** `SendPreCleaningReminders` selects on
`o.CurrentStatus == OrderStatus.Confirmed` (`SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:66`). An early start writes
`OnTheWay` or `InProgress`, putting the order **past** the status the sweep filters on, so the order
is never picked up again. The customer who was promised "your cleaning starts in about an hour" gets
nothing, and no error is raised anywhere — a reminder that does not arrive produces no exception and
no log line.

**Pay is calculated for work not done.** `CompleteOrder` fans out one `CalculateOrderPay` per assigned
cleaner (`CompleteOrder.cs:305-322`). An early start followed by a complete moves money on an order
whose cleaning date has not arrived. The pay path deliberately does not recompute history, so undoing
it is a manual act against a settled row — the cheap fix is upstream, at the transition.

## Decision

**A cleaner may set off or start only once the booking is within `BookingPolicy.StartGraceWindowMinutes
= 60` (`BookingPolicy.cs:61`). One predicate, `IsTooEarlyToStart` (`BookingPolicy.cs:156-159`), is
asked by both transitions and refuses with one key.**

| Key | Refuses when |
|---|---|
| `order.too_early_to_start` | the caller is an assigned cleaner on the order and the booking is still more than 60 minutes away (`BusinessErrorMessage.cs:165`) |

**60 minutes because that is the customer's notice period where the customer gets one — and it is a
reasoned default, not a derivation.** `SendPreCleaningReminders` fires at T-55…70 minutes
(`SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:39`) and its copy promises "about an hour", so where that reminder
lands, a cleaner permitted from T-60 cannot be at the door before the customer has been told. That is
the *upper* bound on the number and it is the reason 60 rather than 120. The *lower* bound is the
cleaner: any narrower and someone who genuinely arrived early stands outside doing nothing. Between
those, 60 is chosen, not computed.

Three things stop this being a clean derivation, and the record states them rather than leaning on a
promise that does not always exist:

- **An entire booking class never receives it.** `SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:67` filters
  `RecurringTemplateId == null`, so **every recurring occurrence is excluded**. A recurring customer's
  last notice is `SendRecurringOrderReminders` — a daily 02:30 UTC prompt asking them to *confirm* an
  occurrence about 24 h out (`appsettings.json:3`), a different ask at a different lead. Recurring is a
  first-class product here, and for it the sentence *"the customer has been told to expect them"* is
  **never** true. This is knowingly uncovered.
- **It is not guaranteed for one-offs either.** The same query requires `PaymentStatus == Paid ||
  PaymentType == Cash`, `UserId != null` and `AssignedEmployees.Any()` (`:65-74`), and
  `order.starting_soon` is **mutable** (`OrderStartingSoon => NotificationCategory.OrderUpdates`,
  `NotificationEventCatalog.cs:169`), so any customer may silence it, and it needs push permission on a
  device to arrive at all. The window is also deliberately closed at the low end so *"a missed tick
  costs silence"* (`SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:30-31`).
- **The relation that actually delivers the guarantee is the sweep's cadence phase, not `60 < 70`.**
  With a `0 */5 * * * *` cron (`appsettings.json:4`) and a `[55, 70]` window, the **first** tick inside
  the window necessarily lands at `minutesOut ∈ (65, 70]` — if it landed at 65 or below, the previous
  tick would have been inside the window too. So the invariant is
  **`StartGraceWindowMinutes < LeadMinutesHigh − cronIntervalMinutes`**, i.e. `60 < 65`, which holds
  today with five minutes of margin — *and* the window must stay at least as wide as the cron interval
  so some tick always lands in it. **The first draft said "neither relation is pinned anywhere"; the
  lead re-opened the suite and that is false (2026-08-23).** The **width** relation is already pinned:
  `TimerScheduleConfigTests.Pre_cleaning_sweep_fires_at_least_once_per_reminder_window` (`:110-133`)
  asserts `widestGap ≤ LeadMinutesHigh − LeadMinutesLow` against the parsed production cron, and
  `The_pre_cleaning_window_brackets_the_promised_hour` (`:140-148`) asserts `60 ∈ [Low, High]` and a
  width of at most 20. The cron string itself is pinned at `:50-66` and its bicep↔appsettings parity at
  `TimerCronSettingsAreDeployedTests.cs:90-109`. **Exactly one relation is unpinned —
  `grace < LeadMinutesHigh − cronInterval`** — and of the three break cases named below, moving the
  cron to 15 minutes **reddens** the string pin (so it breaks loudly, then silently once someone
  updates the constant), narrowing the window to `[55, 62]` is green as claimed, and a run of missed
  ticks is untestable.
- **A fourth break case needs no configuration change at all, and it is live today.** The phase
  argument assumes the order is eligible at every tick from T-70. `SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:72`
  requires `AssignedEmployees.Any()`, and `TakeOrder` carries **no lead-time gate**
  (`OrderAvailability.cs:40-48` reads status and payment only), so an order taken at T-58 becomes
  eligible for the first time *below* the window's high edge — and its cleaner may legally start
  immediately, because 58 is inside the 60-minute grace. The order leaves `Confirmed`, `:66` stops
  matching it, and the customer's "starting soon" notice never fires. **This is knowingly uncovered,
  beside the recurring exclusion**: closing it means either a lead-time gate on `TakeOrder` or a sweep
  that does not key on `Confirmed`, both of which are their own decisions.

`StartEarlyGateTests.cs:120-124` is `Assert.Equal(60, BookingPolicy.StartGraceWindowMinutes);` — it
asserts the literal and **does not couple the grace to the reminder's lead**. Its doc-comment narrates
the derivation; a comment is not a pin. The earlier draft of this ADR called it one, and that claim
was the single citation behind rejecting two alternatives. See *Consequences* for what pinning the
real relation would require.

**There is deliberately no upper bound.** `IsTooEarlyToStart` is a one-sided comparison; a cleaner six
hours late is not too early (`StartEarlyGateTests.cs:107-113`). Refusing a late cleaner would strand a
job that is genuinely happening while the customer is standing there waiting for it. Lateness is a
real problem with a different remedy — the `order.reminder_not_started` nudge of
[ADR-0054](./adr-0054) — and a refusal is the one response that makes it worse.

**One predicate, not two inline comparisons.** `StartOrder` and `NotifyOnTheWay` bracket the same
moment; two copies of `> 60` would eventually answer differently, and the resulting state — allowed to
say you are on the way, refused permission to start — is incoherent to everyone who sees it.

**Placement is part of the decision, not an implementation detail.** The rule is the **last**
`MustAsync` on the `x => x` chain in both validators (`StartOrder.cs:49-58`, `NotifyOnTheWay.cs:51-56`),
and appears on **neither** `RuleFor(x => x.OrderId)` chain. Those chains
(`StartOrder.cs:40-47`) answer existence and status and run first for every caller. A clock rule placed
there would tell anyone who can name an order id whether that booking is more than an hour away —
which is **another customer's schedule**, disclosed by a refusal. `Cascade.Stop` makes the first
failing rule on *that chain* the only answer from it, so an unassigned caller stops at
`employee_not_assigned` and learns nothing about the clock. This is the same disclosure discipline
`patterns-backend.md §"A DISCLOSURE BLOCK is withheld by the server when its sentence stops being
true"` states generally: the *set of errors a caller can provoke* is itself an information channel.

**And the rule immediately above it leaks the same class of fact — that is pre-existing, it is not
fixed here, and the record must not describe it approvingly.** `RuleFor(x => x.OrderId)` runs
`ExistsAsync` and then `OrderIsConfirmedAsync` for **every** caller, before any identity term, on both
commands (`StartOrder.cs:40-47`, `NotifyOnTheWay.cs:42-49`), and those reads are `GetQueryable()` —
tenant-scoped, **not** caller-scoped. FluentValidation's class-level cascade default is `Continue`
(stated in-tree at `TakeOrder.cs:37-44`, which is exactly why `TakeOrder` uses one chain), so both
chains run and both verdicts are returned. So an authenticated partner who can name an order id learns
that it exists and whether it is currently `Confirmed`/`OnTheWay` — another customer's *fulfilment*
state, from a caller with no relationship to the order. `Order.Id` is a ULID (`BaseEntity.cs:5`), so
this is a targeted-probe leak rather than an enumerable one: the caller who can exercise it is a
cleaner who saw the order on the board and did not take it, which is precisely the population
ADR-0036 D4 protects with *"no cleaner ever learns they were passed over"*.

Placing the clock rule last is therefore **necessary and not sufficient**: it keeps this decision from
widening the channel, and it leaves the channel. Collapsing both commands onto `TakeOrder`'s single
ordered chain is the shape that closes it, and it is deliberately **not** done here — it changes the
error contract two mobile clients and the partner web app read, so it needs its own decision and its
own client work. Named as a follow-on in *Consequences* rather than folded in.

## Alternatives considered

- **Gate `StartOrder` only.** Half the fix and none of the benefit: `NotifyOnTheWay` writes `OnTheWay`,
  which is already past `Confirmed`, so it cancels the customer's reminder on its own. Gating one of
  the two is gating neither.
- **Derive the window from the express/standard lead times (2 h / 4 h).** They are *booking* numbers —
  how much notice the platform needs before it will accept work. This is an *execution* number — how
  much notice the customer has already been given. Coupling them means a future pricing change to the
  express tier silently moves when a cleaner may knock.
- **Make it a `CountryConfiguration` value.** Per-country variation is a seam worth protecting, and
  this is still not a candidate for it — but on a **narrower** ground than the first draft claimed.
  The first draft said the grace is *derived* from the reminder lead and so cannot be split from it;
  it is not derived, it is bounded by it (see the Decision), and there are already two independent
  literals. The surviving ground is that nothing about this number is a *country* fact: it is a
  property of how much notice the platform gives and how long a cleaner will stand outside a door,
  neither of which varies by market in anything we know. If the reminder lead ever becomes
  per-country, this moves with it — and the invariant to pin then is the cadence one, not equality.
- **A wider bound for `NotifyOnTheWay` than for `StartOrder`.** The strongest alternative the first
  draft did not state, and it is genuinely close. "On my way" is a *travel* signal and "Start" is a
  *work* signal, and their error costs point in opposite directions: a cleaner with no `JobRadiusKm`
  keeps the whole work country (`Employee.cs:104-112`) against a 500 km ceiling (`JobProximity.cs:49`),
  so a legitimately-taken job 90 minutes away must be travelled to from T-90 while the app refuses to
  record it until T-60 — and at T-25…40 that cleaner receives ADR-0054's `order.reminder_not_started`
  (*"you have not set off"*) on the motorway, unsilenceably, because we would not accept the tap that
  said so. The coherence argument does **not** require equality: both could derive from
  `BookingPolicy` without being the same number, exactly as `ComputePreferredHold` derives a window
  from a constant rather than being one (`BookingPolicy.cs:208-217`). **Rejected for this decision,
  and it is a judgement rather than a proof:** a second constant is a second thing to move, and the
  state it creates — permitted to say you are travelling, refused permission to start — is coherent
  only if someone maintains the relation between the two. Reconsider it the first time a cleaner
  complains about the motorway nudge; the cheaper first move is ADR-0054's required change 5, which
  stops nudging a cleaner who is demonstrably occupied.
- **Also gate `CompleteOrder` on the same predicate.** Not rejected — see *Consequences*: the pay
  defect is **bounded** at 60 minutes by this decision, not closed, and `CompleteOrder` reads no clock
  at all. The cheapest form is not a second constant; it is `CompleteOrder` asking the same
  `BookingPolicy.IsTooEarlyToStart`. Left out of this ADR because it is a second transition with its
  own refusal copy and its own client surface, and folding it in would make this two decisions.
- **Add an upper bound — "too late to start".** It refuses a cleaner standing at a door the customer
  has already opened, converting a recoverable lateness into an unrecoverable one.
- **Put the rule first, on the order-id chain, because it is the cheapest rule.** It is the cheapest,
  and it is the one that must run last. Cheapness is the wrong ordering criterion when a rule's
  *verdict* is the disclosure.
- **Fix `SendPreCleaningReminders` to tolerate a started order instead.** It treats the symptom, leaves
  the pay consequence untouched, and would have the platform reminding a customer about a cleaning
  that the record says is already underway.

## Consequences

**Cheaper.** The customer's T-1h reminder can no longer be cancelled by a cleaner's early tap. Moving
the window is one constant and **both transitions** follow, so `StartOrder` and `NotifyOnTheWay`
cannot be edited apart from each other.

**The pay defect is bounded at 60 minutes, not closed — and the residual is accepted.** An earlier
draft of this ADR said *"pay cannot be triggered for a date that has not arrived"*. That is false.
`CompleteOrder`'s validator chains are `NotEmpty`, `ExistsAsync`, `OrderIsInProgressAsync`,
`HasAfterPhotosAsync`, `CashIsCollectedIfCashPaymentAsync`, `CardPaymentIsConfirmedIfCardPaymentAsync`
(`CompleteOrder.cs:56-75`), then assignment, profile and approval (`:83-90`) — **`CleaningDateTime` is
read nowhere**. So "start at exactly T-60, complete at T-59" is fully legal and fans out
`CalculateOrderPay` per assignment (`CompleteOrder.cs:305-322`) before the booked start. The Context's
account of the *old* defect is right — it was unbounded, days-early — and this decision shrinks it to
an hour. The distinction is load-bearing for the same reason the Context gives: the pay path
deliberately does not recompute history, so the residual is still a manual write against a settled
row, just a smaller one. **Sixty minutes of it is accepted**; closing it is the `CompleteOrder`
alternative above. A future reader auditing pay-before-service must not find this ADR claiming it
cannot happen.

**What the residual costs, sized rather than implied** (lead, 2026-08-23): `CalculateOrderPay.cs:119`
books to the pay period active at **processing** time, not to the cleaning date, and the amount comes
from the pay configs over services and packages with no clock term at all — so an early completion
changes neither the amount nor, given the 08:00–20:00 booking window (`BookingPolicy.cs:42-43`), the
period it lands in. **The exposure is a pay row written up to an hour before the booked start, not a
wrong payment.** It is a records-and-detectability problem, and it takes a deliberate sequence to
reach — after-photos plus settled payment (`CompleteOrder.cs:56-75`) — not a mis-tap.

**The guarantee has one unpinned relation, not none.** As the Decision sets out, "the promise and the
gate can never disagree" is delivered by the cron's phase
(`grace < LeadMinutesHigh − cronInterval`, i.e. `60 < 65`), not by `60 < 70`. The equality
`StartGraceWindowMinutes == SendPreCleaningReminders.LeadMinutesHigh` is false today (60 vs 70) and
should stay false. **The first draft claimed nothing in the tree pinned any of this; the lead
corrected that (2026-08-23):** `TimerScheduleConfigTests.cs:110-133` already pins the width relation
(`widestGap ≤ LeadMinutesHigh − LeadMinutesLow`) against the parsed production cron, `:140-148` pins
`60 ∈ [Low, High]` and a width of at most 20, `:50-66` pins the cron string, and
`TimerCronSettingsAreDeployedTests.cs:90-109` pins bicep↔appsettings parity. The single unpinned
relation is `grace < LeadMinutesHigh − cronInterval`, and pinning it is **~4 lines inside a test that
already computes both operands** — `Pre_cleaning_sweep_fires_at_least_once_per_reminder_window` parses
the effective cron at `:115` and constructs `new SendPreCleaningReminders.Command()` at `:113`, so
`Assert.True(BookingPolicy.StartGraceWindowMinutes < window.LeadMinutesHigh - widestGapMinutes)` needs
no new capability, no configuration reader and no hoisted constant. The first draft overstated that
cost, and the pin is required rather than deferred. The number is not moved: 60 is right for the
reasons in the Decision and the margin today is five minutes.

**More expensive.** `order.too_early_to_start` is a new key, and its localisation **is already done**
on every partner surface: web (`apps/cleansia-partner.app/src/assets/i18n/{en,cs,sk,ru,uk}.json:1136`,
pinned at `apps/cleansia-partner.app/src/app/i18n/error-contract-parity.spec.ts:334`), Android
(`partner-app/src/main/res/values{,-cs,-sk,-uk,-ru}/strings.xml`, `error_order_too_early_to_start`),
and iOS (`CleansiaCore/Sources/CleansiaCore/Resources/Localizable.xcstrings:4342`, five `stringUnit`s
in state `translated`). What remains — and it is the visible half of this change — is that the "Start"
and "On my way" actions are now **sometimes refusable**, and a button that returns an error reads
worse than a button that is disabled: each client should hide or disable the action outside the window
and use the server refusal as the backstop, not the primary channel. **That behaviour is not built on
any client**, and until it is, a cleaner's first experience of the gate is an enabled-looking button on
the two taps they make on every single job. It fails with a translated sentence rather than a generic
error, which bounds the support cost, but it is still the wrong channel. Server-side the window is one
`const` (`BookingPolicy.cs:61`) and widening it is a deploy; the client behaviour is an app-store
release on two apps.

**A customer-side sentence this decision owes and does not yet have.** The window authorises a cleaner
to knock up to an hour early, and no customer surface says so. `order.on_the_way` is **mutable**
(`OrderOnTheWay => NotificationCategory.CleanerOnTheWay`, `NotificationEventCatalog.cs:157`) so it may
be off; `order.starting_soon` may never fire at all for a recurring booking (see the Decision); every
customer surface states the booked hour. The same window also authorises `StartOrder`, so the order
can go `InProgress` an hour early with no arrival or entry verification. *This ADR does not add the
copy* — it names the gap, because the customer is the party standing in a house that is not ready.
`docs/product/business-rules.md` is the place the number belongs.

**A pre-existing disclosure this decision declines to widen and declines to fix.** Per the Decision's
placement paragraph, the `RuleFor(x => x.OrderId)` chain on both commands answers existence and
fulfilment status to any authenticated partner who can name a ULID. Collapsing both commands onto
`TakeOrder`'s single-ordered-chain shape is the fix and is its own decision, because it changes an
error contract three clients read.

**A shape that is now load-bearing.** Both validators load the order themselves rather than inheriting
a prior rule's read, and both handlers guard their own load rather than null-forgiving the validator's
proof (`StartOrder.cs:149-154`, `NotifyOnTheWay.cs:118-126`). A validator and a handler whose queries
diverge is how a business error becomes a 500 the caller cannot act on.

## How a reviewer verifies compliance

- Both transitions call `BookingPolicy.IsTooEarlyToStart`; neither compares `CleaningDateTime` inline,
  and no third call site invents its own window.
- The rule is the **last** `MustAsync` on the `x => x` chain in both validators, and appears on no
  `RuleFor(x => x.OrderId)` chain.
- An unassigned caller gets `employee_not_assigned` **and not** `order.too_early_to_start` — the
  absence is the assertion (`StartEarlyGateTests.cs:131-145`).
- The comparison is strictly `>`. The **refusing** side of the boundary is covered:
  `StartEarlyGateTests.cs:55-60` (`TooEarlyMinutes`) includes `StartGraceWindowMinutes + 1`. The
  **admitting** side at exactly `60` is **not** covered — the admitting theory (`:86-100`) uses
  `30, 0, -15, -1440`. **The first draft said it must not be covered at the validator level because
  such a test would be inherently flaky; the lead re-derived that and it is wrong (2026-08-23).** The
  two clock reads cannot straddle the boundary in the admitting direction: `Arrange` sets
  `cleaning = t0 + 60` (`:93`) and the validator reads `t1 ≥ t0` (`StartOrder.cs:87`), so
  `(cleaning − t1) ≤ 60` always and `> 60` is always false — an `[InlineData(60)]` on the admitting
  theory is **deterministic**, and it belongs there. Pin the refusing side of the same boundary where
  it is a pure function: `A_Late_Cleaner_Is_Never_Too_Early` (`:107-113`) already calls
  `BookingPolicy.IsTooEarlyToStart` directly with a fixed `now`, so two lines beside it cover
  `== 60` (false) and `== 61` (true) with no clock at all. **Until those three lines exist, this
  bullet asserts the `>` and the 61 case only.**
- No upper bound is introduced — a negative lead is always startable
  (`StartEarlyGateTests.cs:86-100`).
- `order.too_early_to_start` resolves under `api.*` in all five locales of every partner client that
  can reach these endpoints; the parity guard is the app's `error-contract-parity.spec.ts`. All three
  clients carry it today — the citations are in *Consequences*; the outstanding client work is the
  disable/hide behaviour, not the string.
- `SendPreCleaningReminders` still filters on `Confirmed` — this ADR fixes the writer, not the sweep,
  and widening the sweep would re-open the defect from the other side.
- The grace is **not** equal to `SendPreCleaningReminders.LeadMinutesHigh` and must not be "fixed" into
  equality. The relation that matters is `StartGraceWindowMinutes < LeadMinutesHigh − cronInterval`;
  a change to the reminder cron or window is a change to this gate's guarantee even though it touches
  neither file.

## Roles affected

None created. `docs/flows/execution-and-completion.md` must show the grace window on both transitions
and name the reminder it protects. `docs/product/business-rules.md` gains the number beside the lead
times and the cancellation ladder — **and it is a customer-facing number, not only a partner one**:
the page must say that a cleaner may arrive up to an hour before the booked time, because no customer
surface says it anywhere today. `docs/domain/order-lifecycle.md` — where the two axes and
`Confirmed`'s overloading are documented — must record that `Confirmed → OnTheWay` and
`{Confirmed, OnTheWay} → InProgress` are now clock-bounded (note the asymmetry:
`StartOrder.cs:101` accepts either prior state, `NotifyOnTheWay.cs:88` accepts only `Confirmed`),
which is the fact its readers were entitled to assume and could not.

## Challenge

*(Architect panel, challenger mode, 2026-08-22. Angle: operations, cost and the human consequence —
not code-vs-record, which is the other challenger's pass.)*

### C1 — The 60 is derived from a promise a whole booking class never receives, and that no customer receives reliably
**Angle:** operations
**Why it matters:** The load-bearing sentence is *"a cleaner cannot be at the door before the customer
has been told to expect them"* (`:53-56`, repeated in `BookingPolicy.cs:49-54`). Check what the sweep
that makes the promise actually covers (`SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:65-74`): it filters
`RecurringTemplateId == null`, so **every recurring occurrence is excluded** — those customers' last
notice is `SendRecurringOrderReminders`, a daily 02:30 UTC prompt asking them to *confirm* an occurrence
~24 h out (`appsettings.json:3`), a different ask at a different lead. It also requires
`PaymentStatus == Paid || PaymentType == Cash`, `UserId != null` and `AssignedEmployees.Any()`. On top of
that, `order.starting_soon` is **mutable** — `OrderStartingSoon => NotificationCategory.OrderUpdates`
(`NotificationEventCatalog.cs:169`) — so any customer may silence it, and it needs push permission on a
device to arrive at all. And the window is deliberately closed at the low end so that *"a missed tick
costs silence"* (`SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:30-31`). So the promise holds only for a one-off, paid,
assigned order whose customer has push on, has not muted order updates, and whose tick fired. For a
recurring booking it is **never** true, and recurring is a first-class product here. That does not make
60 the wrong number — it makes the **derivation** wrong, and the derivation is what this ADR uses to
refuse `CountryConfiguration` (`:92-95`), to refuse the express/standard lead times (`:88-91`), and to
pin the constant in a test (`StartEarlyGateTests.cs:120-124`). A number whose only defence is a promise
that an entire booking class does not get needs a second defence, or an explicit "recurring is
knowingly uncovered" line in *Consequences*.

### C2 — One number for two acts whose error costs point in opposite directions; the coherence argument does not require equality
**Angle:** operations
**Why it matters:** "On my way" is a **travel** signal; "Start" is a **work** signal. The platform's own
board is country-wide by default — a cleaner with no `JobRadiusKm` keeps the whole work country
(`NewJobsDigestService.cs:20-27`, `Employee.cs:103-112`) and the radius ceiling is **500 km**
(`JobProximity.cs:49`), deliberately *"wider than the longest span of any country the platform serves"*.
So a cleaner who has legitimately taken a job 90 minutes away must set off at T-90 and the app **refuses
to let them record it** until T-60. The consequence compounds with ADR-0054: at T-25…40 that cleaner
receives `order.reminder_not_started` — *"the job starts shortly and you have not set off"* — while they
are on the motorway, because the platform would not accept the tap that says so; and per ADR-0054 §1
they cannot silence it. The stated reason for one predicate is that two inline comparisons *"would
eventually answer differently"* (`:67-69`). That risk is discharged by deriving both from one place
(`BookingPolicy`), not by making them the same number — `ComputePreferredHold` in the same file already
derives a window from a constant rather than being one. *Alternatives considered* contains "Gate
`StartOrder` only", which is a different question (whether to gate), and does **not** contain "a wider
bound for `NotifyOnTheWay` than for `StartOrder`". That is the alternative this decision dismissed
without stating.

### C3 — A 60-minute early arrival is a customer-facing policy recorded as a partner-side validator

### C4 — The guarantee has zero margin and is delivered by the sweep's tick phase, not by the constant the tests pin

### Magnitudes restated at the size the tree supports

1. **"Neither relation is pinned anywhere" is false, and the author conceded it into the record.**
   `TimerScheduleConfigTests.cs:110-133` already asserts the **width** relation
   (`widestGap ≤ LeadMinutesHigh − LeadMinutesLow`) against the parsed production cron — which is
   precisely the author's own "second, easily-missed condition" — and `:140-148` asserts
   `60 ∈ [Low, High]` plus a width of at most 20. The cron string is pinned at `:50-66` and its
   deployed parity at `TimerCronSettingsAreDeployedTests.cs:90-109`. Exactly **one** relation is
   unpinned: `grace < LeadMinutesHigh − cronInterval`. Two knock-on corrections: a 15-minute cron
   **reddens** the string pin rather than passing silently, and C8's *"change the defaults to 20/30 and
   it stays green"* is true of `StartEarlyGateTests` alone — `The_pre_cleaning_window_brackets_the_promised_hour`
   goes red. The Decision and Consequences are corrected; the ADR also overstated the **cost** of the
   missing pin, which is ~4 lines inside a test that already computes both operands.
2. **C4's "zero margin / the honest number is 45–50" does not survive**, and the author's rebuttal of
   it is upheld: the guarantee is delivered by the **first** in-window tick, bounded by the window's
   **high** edge, so the invariant is `60 < 70 − 5 = 65` — five minutes of margin — not
   `60 ≤ 55 − 5`. Re-derived by simulation: the first in-window tick lands at
   `minutesOut ∈ (65, 70]`, and at exactly 70 for any booking aligned to five minutes, which is every
   booking the platform sells. **60 stays.**
3. **The pay residual is smaller than "money moved early" suggests.** `CalculateOrderPay.cs:119` books
   to the period active at processing time and the amount carries no clock term, and 08:00–20:00
   bookings cannot straddle a period boundary in 60 minutes — so an early completion changes neither
   the amount nor the period. The exposure is a **pay row written before the booked start**, reachable
   only through a deliberate sequence (after-photos + settled payment), not a wrong payment. Added to
   Consequences so the next auditor sizes it correctly instead of re-litigating it.
4. **The "inherently flaky" reason for not testing the admitting boundary is wrong, and the author
   conceded it into the compliance checklist.** In the admitting direction the two clock reads cannot
   straddle: `Arrange` sets `cleaning = t0 + 60` and the validator reads `t1 ≥ t0`, so
   `(cleaning − t1) ≤ 60` always and `> 60` is always false. `[InlineData(60)]` on the admitting theory
   is deterministic. The checklist is corrected, and the missing coverage becomes three lines rather
   than a design problem.
5. **A break case neither side found, live today, needing no configuration change.** The cadence-phase
   argument assumes eligibility at every tick from T-70, but `SendPreCleaningReminders.cs:72` requires
   `AssignedEmployees.Any()` and `TakeOrder` has no lead-time gate — so a job taken at T-58 first
   becomes eligible *below* the high edge, its cleaner may legally start immediately inside the
   60-minute grace, and the customer's notice never fires. That is this ADR's own defect arriving from
   a side it did not guard. Added to the Decision as **knowingly uncovered**, beside the recurring
   exclusion; closing it is a separate decision (a `TakeOrder` lead gate, or a sweep that does not key
   on `Confirmed`).
