# ADR-0057 — `OrderStatus.Confirmed` means **a cleaner took this job**, and nothing else; a paid card order rests at `New` and reaches the job board through the **money** term alone

- **Status:** `accepted` — **2026-09-08, by owner ruling.** Not a panel decision: the owner was asked
  directly whether `Confirmed` may keep meaning two things, and answered *"No, confirm is when the
  cleaner actually took the job and new is new. Keep separate."*
- **Date:** 2026-09-08
- **Supersedes:** [ADR-0037](./adr-0037) **D1's status term only** — the clause
  "concretely `Confirmed`, plus `New` **only for `PaymentType.Cash`**". Everything else in ADR-0037
  stands: the two-axis shape, the one-rule-one-place rule, `TakeOrder`'s status gate, the SQL/C#
  equivalence pin, and the declaration that `OrderStatus.Pending` is dead.
- **Superseded by:** —
- **Applies to:** `Cleansia.Core.Domain.Orders.OrderAvailability`,
  `Features/Payments/HandlePaymentNotification`, `Features/Orders/ConfirmRecurringOrder`,
  `Features/Orders/AdminReassignOrder`, and the customer order-status pipes in `Cleansia.App`

## Context

ADR-0037 D1 was right that offerability spans two axes. It was also right that the fulfilment axis
alone could not express the rule. What it did not do was ask **why** the fulfilment axis was
insufficient — and the answer is that one of its members was carrying a fact from the other axis.

`Confirmed` had two unrelated producers:

| Producer | What actually happened | Cleaner involved? |
|---|---|---|
| `HandlePaymentNotification` | money settled | no |
| `ConfirmRecurringOrder` (cash) | the customer confirmed their own occurrence | no |
| `TakeOrder` | a cleaner accepted the job | **yes** |

D1's own status term — `Confirmed OR (New AND Cash)` — is the shape of that overload. The `AND Cash`
qualifier exists **only** because a paid *card* order had already been moved to `Confirmed` by the
webhook, so `New` needed to admit cash and nothing else. The panel saw the problem and compensated for
it (§D1, "the state the take assumes") rather than separating the meanings.

The cost was not theoretical. `CancellationAssessor` carries a comment explaining that it reads
`AssignedEmployees` **because** `Confirmed` is overloaded; `BookingPolicy` carries another; so does
`OrderCleanerAssignedNotifier`. Three separate pieces of code had to be told not to trust a status
word. And the reported defect that produced `CancellationAcceptanceSignalTests` was exactly this: a
customer who paid by card and cancelled twenty minutes later was billed a 25% "acceptance" fee for a
cleaner who did not exist, because the webhook's `Confirmed` track read as acceptance.

## Decision

**`Confirmed` means a cleaner took this job.** It has one producer class: acceptance, whether by the
cleaner (`TakeOrder`) or by an admin assigning on their behalf (`AdminReassignOrder`).

**A paid card order rests at `New` + `PaymentStatus.Paid`** — the same fulfilment state as a cash order,
differing only on the money axis, which is where the difference actually lives.

**The offerability status term stops qualifying payment.** It becomes the plain fulfilment question:

```
(New ∨ Confirmed ∨ OnTheWay ∨ InProgress)          -- the work is not over
∧ (Paid ∨ (Cash ∧ RecurringTemplateId is null))    -- the money is where the take assumes
```

This is **narrower in intent and simpler in form** than D1's version. The money term was always doing
the payment qualification; the `AND Cash` on the status term was a second copy of that question,
needed only to compensate for the overload. Removing it flips exactly one input — `New + Card + Paid`,
the state this decision creates.

## Consequences

- **`Confirmed` can still be true with zero assignees.** A drop, a cover request or an admin rejection
  removes the assignment without walking the status back, so an order can read `Confirmed` after every
  cleaner has left. That is a pre-existing behaviour this record does **not** change, and it means the
  new meaning is precisely *"a cleaner took this job"*, not *"a cleaner is currently on it"*. Walking
  the status backwards would be a new transition and `AdminOverrideOrderStatus` forbids backward moves;
  it is a separate decision and is deliberately left open.
- **The push key `order.confirmed` keeps its name** while describing a money event. Renaming costs ten
  locale files across two mobile platforms plus the feed catalogue, and the customer still needs to be
  told their payment landed. The two copy guards that forbid it claiming a cleaner
  (`PushLocKeyCatalogTests`, `NotificationTemplatesTest`) are unaffected and remain true.
- **Nothing on the wire moves.** The enum members and their integers are unchanged, so both committed
  mobile specs and all three generated clients are byte-identical. No migration, no DEV drop.
- **The mobile booking timelines were already correct** and needed no change. Both key their assignment
  step on `cleanerAssigned` rather than on the status, and their confirmation step is captioned
  *"Cleaner confirmed"* — so under the old meaning a paid card order **falsely** showed a cleaner
  confirmed, and under the new one it honestly shows the search still running. This decision makes two
  customer-facing screens more truthful without touching them.
- **`New` gained an explicit case in the customer status pipes.** It had been falling through to the
  default arm — a generic dot — which is the same defect shape as the admin blank status pill
  (T-0687): a zero-valued enum member with no case of its own, rendering as an absence.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the overload (i.e. leave ADR-0037 D1 standing).** Rejected by the owner. The maintenance cost
  was already visible as three separate "do not trust this status" comments and one shipped money
  defect.
- **Introduce a new status member for "paid, unassigned".** Rejected: it moves the wire, forces both
  mobile specs and all three generated clients to regenerate, and adds a member to describe a state the
  two existing axes already express exactly.
- **Change only the Stripe webhook.** Rejected: `ConfirmRecurringOrder` produces the same defect from a
  different door, so the word would still mean two things and the exercise would buy nothing while
  paying the full documentation and test cost.
