### What could not be proven about a customer (the gap this ADR closes)

1. That they accepted the terms, when, from where, and **which text** (T-0686).
2. The fee tier, free window, "cleaner had accepted" flag and refund figure **at the moment they
   cancelled** — and any cancel attempt that was **refused**.
3. The price, discounts and policy figures **they were shown** when they booked.
4. Any refused or validation-rejected attempt at all (the gate dropped them before the failure sink).
5. Membership subscribe / swap / cancel, recurring template edits, notification-preference flips
   ("I was never told it was cancelled") — all in-place, no history.
6. A per-customer timeline: admin rows, cleaner rows and customer rows were three tables with no
   screen that interleaves them, and the order/dispute screens linked to only one of them.

## Decision

### D0 — The rule

**A money-relevant customer act is recorded as an append-only row the moment it commits, with the
figures and versions the customer was shown, the request context it came from, and its outcome —
and support can read the customer's whole trail on one screen and hand it over as one file.**

Data-minimised by construction: a row holds **identifiers, money, enums, versions and request
context** — never a name, contact detail, address text, free text the customer typed, card data or a
token. The domain row (`Order`, `Dispute`, `UserConsent`) stays the home of the customer's text; the
audit row *references* it by id and records that it was submitted, when, from where and how long it
was.

### D1 — The store: a third table, written by the shipped pipeline through an audience-aware gate

**`CustomerActionAudit : BaseEntity, ITenantEntity`** beside `AdminActionAudit` and
`EmployeeActionAudit` — its own table (owner ruling 2026-09-06: actor kinds apart; the roster's
"ActorEmail is the ADMIN who acted" sentence must stay true), written by the **same two behaviors**,
the **same factory**, the **same writer/sink seams** and the **same snapshot buffer** as the admin
table. Nothing about the pipeline's shape changes; the gate learns to answer *which* table.

```
CustomerActionAudit : BaseEntity, ITenantEntity            -- NOT Auditable (ADR-0012 D6 reasoning)
    TenantId        varchar(26)    NOT NULL                -- stamped by the writer/sink from the ambient tenant (D7; ADR-0061 D8)
    UserId          varchar(26)?                           -- the customer; NULL for a guest act
    ClientAudience  varchar(40)    NOT NULL                -- IHostAudienceProvider.Audience: the JWT audience of the host that SERVED the request
                                                           --   (filled for anonymous acts; never read off the token). The customer web and the
                                                           --   customer app share one audience, so it names the client FAMILY; DeviceLabel tells them apart
    IpAddress       varchar(45)?                           -- IRequestMetadataProvider.IpAddress
    DeviceLabel     varchar(120)?                          -- UA or X-Device-Label (already truncated by the provider)
    DeviceId        varchar(64)?                           -- a signed-in row: the session's signed device_id claim; an anonymous row: the X-Device-Id header
    Action          varchar(100)   NOT NULL                -- frozen [AuditAction] label, e.g. customer.order.cancel
    ResourceType    varchar(50)?                           -- Order | Dispute | User | UserMembership | RecurringBookingTemplate
    ResourceId      varchar(26)?                           -- clamped to the column on a failure row (a malformed-id probe is recorded, not lost)
    Success         bool           NOT NULL
    ErrorCode       varchar(100)?                          -- the BusinessErrorMessage KEY (AuditErrorCode.Resolve) or the exception type name; clamped
    OccurredOn      timestamptz    NOT NULL
    PayloadJson     jsonb?                                 -- the typed evidence record (D3), success rows only
    CorrelationId   varchar(64)?                           -- Activity.Current.TraceId
  IX (TenantId, OccurredOn DESC) · (UserId, OccurredOn DESC) · (ResourceType, ResourceId) · (OccurredOn)
     -- the last serves the tenant-agnostic retention scan (D5); no (Action, ...) index — the action filter runs inside the tenant-scoped list
```

- **Private setters, one static `Create(...)` factory, no public mutator but `Pseudonymise()`.** The
  draft said `init`; the shipped entity is stricter — a row can only be made whole, by the factory
  the two behaviors call through `AuditEntryFactory`.
- **No `Reason`, no `BeforeJson/AfterJson`.** A customer's act is a statement, not a diff; the diff-shaped
  actions (D3: notification preferences, plan swap, the recurring-template acts) put `{ before, after }`
  *inside* the payload. The customer's free text is never copied (D0).
- **No `ActorEmail`.** The admin table carries it because the actor is the accountable party whose
  identity must outlive the subject; here the actor *is* the subject and the erasure blanks their
  email on the `User` row — a copy would be the "second uncontrolled copy" ADR-0012 D4.1 forbids.
- **`UserId` is a bare scalar, no navigation, no FK** (the `EmployeeActionAudit.EmployeeId` shape):
  the row must outlive everything it names.
- **`DeviceId` on a signed-in row is the claim, not the header.** The `X-Device-Id` header is the
  client's word alone and re-sendable per request; the signed `device_id` claim is the device the
  token was minted for — the one device revocation acts on (ADR-0026). A signed-in row records the
  claim or nothing; only an anonymous act falls back to the header.

**The gate.** `AdminMutationGate` became `AuditGate.Resolve(request, descriptor, session) →
AuditAudience?` with two arms:

```csharp
if (!descriptor.Audited || !name.EndsWith("Command")) return null;
var role = session.GetTypedUserClaim(ClaimTypes.Role)?.Value;
if (role == Administrator)                                                  return AuditAudience.Admin;    // ADR-0012 D3, byte-for-byte
if (descriptor.Audience == AuditAudience.Customer
    && (role == Customer
        || (role is null && descriptor.AllowsAnonymousActor)))              return AuditAudience.Customer; // opt-in; anonymous only where the marker says so
return null;                                                                                               // Employee role, unmarked customer commands, system jobs: unchanged
```

- **Admin is opt-out, customer is opt-in.** ADR-0012 D3 audits *every* admin mutation because the
  owner asked for "all admin mutations"; here the owner asked for *proof for incidents*, and ADR-0045
  D13 refuses collection just in case. A customer command is recorded **only** when it carries
  `[AuditAction("customer.…", Audience = AuditAudience.Customer, ResourceType = …)]`. Of the 44
  customer commands, D3 marks **sixteen** (fifteen labels). `QuoteOrder`, `ValidatePromoCode` and
  `GetMyNotificationPreferences` are reads named `Command` — opt-in is what keeps them out.
- **The admin arm wins.** An Administrator running a customer-marked command lands in the admin
  table (accountability of the privileged actor first). An Employee running one lands nowhere, as
  before. **`EmployeeActionAudit` is untouched** — it is not wired into the pipeline by this ADR; its
  doc comment now cites this ADR for why.
  > **As amended 2026-09-15 (owner ruling *"change to be as admin"*, T-0755) — the admin arm writes an
  > admin label, and the admin sign-in is a row.** An administrator's act under a customer-marked
  > command is an *admin* act and is read under an admin label: the marker gains an optional
  > **`AdminAction`** (`[AuditAction("customer.session.logout", Audience = Customer, ResourceType =
  > "User", AdminAction = "admin.session.logout")]`), and `AuditEntryFactory.Build` writes
  > `descriptor.AdminAction` on every admin row — the marker's `AdminAction` where one is declared,
  > else the frozen label as before (an admin-audience marker declares none; its one label is already
  > the admin one). `Logout` is the only customer-marked command the admin host dispatches, so it is
  > the only marker that carries one today. **`AdminLogin` carries `[AuditAction("admin.session.login",
  > ResourceType = "User", AllowsAnonymousActor = true)]`** — an admin-audience marker with an
  > anonymous arm, which `AuditGate` now admits on the **admin host** (`HostServes`: a customer marker
  > on a customer host, an admin marker on the admin host); success and failure rows both, a refused
  > sign-in on a known account naming the account and stamped with its company (D3/D7 as amended), an
  > unknown address naming nobody and carrying no e-mail. `CustomerAuditActionRosterTests`' "a marker
  > carries nothing else" is updated for the one extra member, `AuditActionDescriptorTests` pins the
  > fallback, and `SessionAuditRouteTests` asserts `admin.session.logout` on the admin host. Admin
  > labels render raw in the admin web (no catalogue) — nothing to add there.
- **Anonymous is a customer only where the marker says so.** `Register` and guest `CreateOrder` have
  no role claim and are the acts the owner most needs proof of, so their markers carry
  `AllowsAnonymousActor = true`. Every other customer-marked command requires the `Customer` role: a
  system job (no session at all) that one day dispatches `CancelOrder.Command` or
  `ConfirmRecurringOrder.Command` must produce **no row**, not a row that reads as a guest act.
  (`role is null` alone cannot tell an anonymous HTTP request from a Functions-host dispatch; the
  marker can.)
  > **As amended 2026-09-14.** `Resolve` takes a fourth argument, `IHostAudienceProvider`, and the
  > anonymous clause reads `role is null && descriptor.AllowsAnonymousActor &&
  > hostAudience.Audience == JwtAudiences.Customer`: an anonymous act is a customer act **only on a
  > customer host**. Nine markers allow an anonymous actor now — the two above plus the session acts
  > of D3 as amended (`Login`, `MobileLogin`, `GoogleAuth`, `AppleAuth`, `ConfirmUserEmail`,
  > `RequestPasswordChange`, `ChangePassword`), several of which are routed on the partner hosts too,
  > where the caller is a cleaner whose session history belongs in no table. The admin arm is
  > unchanged; a Customer-role caller needs no host check (a customer token is only valid on a
  > customer host).
- **An anonymous refusal is a row too, and it carries the caller's IP.** A refused `Register` or a
  refused guest `CreateOrder` (validation reject, handler failure, exception) writes an out-of-band
  failure row with `UserId = null` and the request's IP and device label — the "they tried to book at
  09:41 with a wrong price and were refused" proof. The volume is bounded by the same window that
  bounds the request: both routes are `[EnableRateLimiting("auth")]`, 10 requests per minute per real
  client IP, so an unauthenticated caller cannot write more failure rows than that. Two things a row
  still needs: an ambient tenant and a resolved operator. A refusal raised **before** the operator is
  resolved (`country.not_serviced`, `tenant.not_found` — the `OperatorTenantScopeBehavior` refusals,
  ADR-0061 D3) has no tenant to be stamped with and is **skipped with one warning**, not written and
  not raised as an error; a refusal raised after it is stamped with that operator.
- **The error code is the refusal KEY, not the field — on both arms.** `AuditEntryFactory.CreateFailure`
  used to store `result.Error?.Code`, and `Error(code, message)` puts the **field name** in `Code`
  (`new Error(nameof(command.OrderId), BusinessErrorMessage.OrderNotFound)` gives `Code = "OrderId"`,
  `Message = "order.not_found"`); a validation reject's top-level `Error` is the generic
  `ValidationError` sentinel. A failure row whose `ErrorCode` reads `OrderId` proves nothing. One static
  `AuditErrorCode.Resolve(BusinessResult)` records `Error.Message` for a handler-returned failure and
  the first `IValidationResult.Errors[i].Message` for a validation reject (falling back to `Code` only
  when the message is blank); the exception path keeps the exception type name. **Owner ruling
  (Q-AUD-O2): the admin arm adopts the same helper**, so admin failure rows carry the key from T-AUD-1
  on; rows written before it are not rewritten (there are none in production).
- **`ResourceId` on a customer row comes from the snapshot or an exact property — never the `Id` /
  single-`*Id` fallbacks.** `AuditResourceResolver` falls back to "the one string property ending in
  `Id`", which on `CreateMembershipCheckoutSession.Command(PlanCode, CountryId)` returns the **country
  id** labelled as a `UserMembership`, and on `CreateDispute.Command(OrderId, …)` the order id labelled
  as a `Dispute`. The customer arm uses `AuditResourceResolver.ResolveExact(request, resourceType,
  resourceIdProperty)`: the `{ResourceType}Id` property, or the property the marker names in
  `ResourceIdProperty` — added so the three recurring-schedule acts, whose command carries
  `TemplateId` and whose resource is `RecurringBookingTemplate`, record the probed template id on a
  refusal instead of nothing. The admin arm keeps its fallbacks (unchanged, ADR-0012).
  `CreateDispute` therefore marks `ResourceType = "Order"` (the act is against the order; a refused
  filing is evidence about that order) and its success snapshot re-labels the row `("Dispute",
  disputeId)` — the snapshot wins, as before.

**Factory / writer / sink.** `AuditEntryFactory` gained `CreateCustomerSuccess` / `CreateCustomerFailure`
returning `CustomerActionAudit` and takes `IRequestMetadataProvider` and `IHostAudienceProvider`;
`IAuditWriter.Add` and `IAuditFailureSink.RecordFailureAsync` gained a `CustomerActionAudit` overload;
each behavior branches once on the resolved audience. The latch, the out-of-band scope, the "success
rides the single commit" placement and the "failure is best-effort and swallowed" rule are inherited
unchanged. `IAuditContext` gained **`RecordEvidence(resourceType, resourceId, object payload, string?
actorUserId = null)`** — the customer-side twin of `RecordChange`: one payload, no diff; `actorUserId`
exists for `Register`, whose actor does not exist until the handler creates them (the session has no
user id; the row must carry the new one — the session still wins when it has one, S1).

**Enums are serialised by name, camelCase, on all three tables.** `AuditContext`'s JSON options gained
`JsonStringEnumConverter(JsonNamingPolicy.CamelCase)`, so a payload reads `"tier":"plus"`,
`"consentType":"termsOfService"`, `"status":"onTheWay"` — a row a support agent can read without the
enum source. The admin snapshots changed shape with it (they used to carry integers); there is no
production data, and the admin int-pinning tests moved to names.

### D2 — Reuse over invention (why not the two cheaper-looking shapes)

- **Rejected — widen `AdminActionAudit` with an `ActorKind`.** It breaks the 2026-09-06 ruling, makes
  the roster sentence false, puts customer rows in the admin feed, and the customer row needs four
  columns (audience, IP, device label, device id) the admin row must *not* grow (ADR-0012 D4.1 keeps
  the admin row minimal; adding actor-IP to an admin accountability row is a new question nobody
  asked).
- **Rejected — handler-explicit writes, the `EmployeeActionAudit` shape.** It cannot see a refusal: a
  handler-returned business failure does not commit, and a validation reject never reaches the
  handler — yet *"the customer tried to cancel at 09:41 and was refused because the job was in
  progress"* is exactly the proof a dispute turns on (gaps 2 and 4). Only the two behaviors see both
  shapes, and they already do it right (ADR-0012 D2.1/D2.2). The employee table stays handler-written
  because its two acts have no refusal branch worth a row.
- **Rejected — the request log as the trail.** Information-level, `Warning` floor on every deployed
  host, no query surface, S6 forbids the body anyway.

### D3 — What is recorded, and what each row carries (*A-1 … A-5*)

Ranked by what money disputes turn on. Each command carries a frozen label; each success row carries
a **typed, pre-redacted evidence record** the handler emits. Every record implements
`ICustomerAuditPayload` (a marker) so one reflection test can walk them all (Verification #5).

### D4 — Terms and consent get a version, and the acceptance is recorded server-side (absorbs T-0686)

- **The version is a code constant, pinned to the copy by the parity checker.**
  `Cleansia.Core.Domain/Legal/LegalDocumentVersions.cs`: `CustomerTerms = "2026-09-draft"`,
  `CustomerPrivacy = "2026-09-draft"`, `For(ConsentType)` (null for the marketing and data-processing
  consents, which have no document). The locale keys `terms_page.version` / `privacy_page.version`
  render it on the customer web legal pages in all five locales; `check-booking-policy-parity.mjs` pins
  the five locale values to the C# constant (§4 of the checker, with its own self-test). **The rule for
  a legal-text edit: bump the constant *and* the key in the same change** — the same "figure comes from
  one place" discipline ADR-0060 D4 built. (Q-AUD-L2 — ruling: a dated string is what identifies a
  version; the draft handle stands.)
- **Why not ADR-0041's `AgreementVersion` tables now.** They are accepted and unbuilt; they carry text
  bodies, per-language rows, review status and a content hash — the right shape for *final* legal
  texts. Today's texts are drafts with a blank date and a draft banner; a version string is what a
  draft can honestly carry. **The upgrade path is preserved:** `AgreementVersion.Version` is a
  `varchar(32)` external handle — the constant becomes that row's handle the day the lawyer's texts
  land, and every recorded `"2026-09-draft"` still identifies its text. Nothing recorded now has to be
  re-keyed.
- **`UserConsent.DocumentVersion` `varchar(32)?`** — the version in force when the row was granted or
  re-granted (`UserConsent.Grant/Regrant` take it; `ConsentService.TryGrantAsync(userId, type,
  documentVersion)`). Existing rows stay `null` = "accepted before versioning" — **no backfill, no
  re-prompt** (Q-AUD-L2 ruling). **Stamped only for a `Customer` subject**: `GrantConsent.Command` is
  also routed on both Partner hosts and an employee accepts a *different* document (ADR-0041), so the
  handler passes `LegalDocumentVersions.For(type)` only when the caller's role claim is `Customer`;
  an employee's `TermsOfService` row keeps `null` until `AgreementVersion` lands. **A re-acceptance
  under a *different* version updates the row**: `TryGrantAsync` returns `false` on an already-granted
  row with the same version (or no version), and calls `AcceptVersion(version, ip, ua)` when the
  version differs — *different*, not *newer*, because versions are opaque strings and "different" is
  all the row can tell. The `customer.consent.grant` audit row is still written either way and is the
  history; the `UserConsent` row is the truth about *now*, which is why `UserConsent` is **not** made
  append-only (T-0686 open decision 2 — answered without changing the consent model).
- **Registration writes the consent, server-side, in the same commit.** `Register.Command` gained
  `bool? TermsAccepted`; Google/Apple already carried it. When `true`, the handler grants
  `TermsOfService` and `PrivacyPolicy` with the current versions and IP/UA (the `ConsentService`
  shape) and `Register` records `RegistrationEvidence`; the Google/Apple provisioning branches grant
  the same two rows with the version and record no audit row (D3). The customer web register form
  and the two social sign-ups send the tick; the customer-side `signup-consent.service.ts` and its
  localStorage flush are **deleted**. A client that sends nothing — every shipped mobile build —
  lands `null`, recorded as "not asserted", **not refused** (Q-AUD-L4 ruling: record only, refuse
  nothing until the legal texts are final).
- **The order wizard's terms tick reaches the server.** `CreateOrder.Command` gained `bool?
  TermsAccepted`; the booking row records it with `termsVersionAccepted`. Guest checkout is the case
  this exists for. This ADR records, it does not gate. The web wizard sends the tick only when the box
  was shown and ticked; an account that already consented sees no box and asserts nothing new, and
  every shipped mobile build sends nothing — both land `null`, "not asserted".

### D5 — Immutability, retention, erasure and export

**Append-only.** Private setters, one factory, one sanctioned mutator — `Pseudonymise()`, which nulls
`IpAddress`, `DeviceLabel`, `DeviceId` and nothing else. `ICustomerActionAuditRepository` exposes
`Add`, reads, `PseudonymiseForSubjectAsync` and `DeleteExpiredAsync`. The two holes the base types
leave open are closed by a test, not a type: `BaseEntity.IsActive` is a **public setter** and
`IRepository<T>` exposes `Remove`, `RemoveRange`, `Deactivate`, `DeactivateRange` on every repository
including this one — `CustomerActionAuditImmutabilityTests` walks `Cleansia.Core.AppServices` and
`Cleansia.Infra.Database` by reflection and fails on any call site that invokes one of the four on the
type or sets/reads `IsActive` on it.

- **Rejected — a hash chain / signed rows.** The threat model that could alter a row is a human with
  `psql` (ADR-0041 D3.3's threat model); a chain kept in the same database is re-chained by the same
  human, and a key-signed chain needs key management this platform does not have. The proportional
  integrity guarantee is the append-only code discipline plus the fact that the export (below) is
  re-generatable from the database. Revisit if a court asks for it.
- **Rejected — `REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE` at the database.** Erasure must pseudonymise and the retention
  sweep must delete; both are application writes.

**Retention (*A-6*, legal basis — Q-AUD-L1 ruling).** The rows are kept under **legitimate interest
— establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims** (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f), with Art. 17(3)(e) as the
erasure exemption). **Window: three years from the act, per row** — the general subjective limitation
period of the Czech Civil Code (§ 629) runs per claim, i.e. per act, and three years also comfortably
covers card-scheme chargeback windows. Implementation: tenant setting `retention.customer_audit.years`
(`RetentionDefaults.CustomerAuditRetentionYearsKey`, default `DefaultCustomerAuditRetentionYears = 3`),
one `RunSafeAsync("CustomerActionAudits", …)` task on the Functions timer under the `DataRetention:Enabled`
master switch, calling `DeleteExpiredAsync(cutoff)` — tenant-agnostic, per row by its own `OccurredOn`,
in `RetentionDefaults.BatchSize` batches until a batch is empty; the `(OccurredOn)` index serves it.
**A window at or below zero is a misconfiguration, not an instruction**: this is the one delete the
append-only discipline sanctions, and a cutoff of "now" would empty the evidence table on the next tick,
so the task logs a warning and keeps the default. **Why per row and not "three years after the last
act"** (the author's first draft): the anchor form kept an active customer's IP addresses and device
labels for the life of the account — a ten-year customer's row from year one still carried their IP —
which is the data-minimisation failure ADR-0045 D13 names, and it needed a `GROUP BY` sweep that no
other retention task has. Per row, an active customer's trail is a rolling three-year window; an erased
customer's pseudonymised rows expire three years after each act. The sweep never touches the admin or
employee tables (ADR-0012 D6 stands; asserted by row count). Q-AUDIT-01 is re-filed under Q-AUD-L1.

> **As amended 2026-09-15 (ADR-0061 O-4 as ruled, T-0759) — the window is the company's own, and the
> floor moved to the writer.** `retention.customer_audit.years` is one of the nine catalogued
> per-company settings an admin sets on the *Company settings* page; the sweep runs **once per
> operating company** under its override and `DeleteExpiredAsync` reads through the filter, so
> "tenant-agnostic" above is superseded — a one-year window on company B deletes B's rows and keeps
> A's (`CustomerActionAuditRetentionTests`). The floor of one year is enforced by the catalogue at
> write time (`tenant_setting.invalid_value`), not by the task, and a stored value the catalogue no
> longer accepts resolves to the default; the "misconfiguration, not an instruction" reasoning is
> unchanged and now lives once, on the catalogue's definition.

**Erasure.** Verdict **`AnonymizedInPlace`** on the roster: the subject handle is already
pseudonymous (the `User` row keeps its id); the three request-metadata columns are personal data and
are blanked by `customerActionAuditRepository.PseudonymiseForSubjectAsync(userId)` — a **tracked
load** through `GetQueryableIgnoringTenant()` (defence in depth like the sibling walks) followed by

### D7 — Tenant scoping (ADR-0061)

`CustomerActionAudit` is `ITenantEntity` and its column is **NOT NULL** like every stamped table
(ADR-0061 D8; `TenantIdRequiredModelTests` walks it). `DbContextAuditWriter` and
`OutOfBandAuditFailureSink` stamp `TenantId ??= tenantProvider.GetCurrentTenantId()` exactly as they do
for the admin row — **the ambient tenant at write time**. For an authenticated customer that is the
`tenant_id` claim; for an anonymous act it is the market's operator that `OperatorTenantScopeBehavior`
set before validation ran (ADR-0061 D3), so the audit row and the `User`/`Order` row it describes are
stamped from the same source in the same request and agree by construction. The one row that cannot be
stamped — an anonymous refusal raised *by* the scope behaviour, before any operator exists — is skipped
with a warning (D1). This ADR derives no tenant of its own. Admin reads see their own tenant through
the global filter; the erasure's pseudonymisation and the retention sweep read with
`GetQueryableIgnoringTenant()` like every other sweep.

> **As amended 2026-09-14.** The session acts (D3 as amended) are anonymous requests that name no
> market: their refusal row is stamped with the **default market's operator** (the explicit
> `CountryId => null` on the command, ADR-0061 D3 as amended) and their **success row with the
> account's own operator** — `TokenService` adopts `user.TenantId` before the confirmation check on
> every sign-in, and the two reset handlers adopt it themselves since they mint no token (ADR-0061 D4
> as amended). Four Postgres cases in `SessionAuditTests` seed the subject under a second operator and
> assert the row's `TenantId` is that operator. The retry sweep (D5 as amended) sets the request row's
> tenant per candidate scope before dispatching, so the validator, the walk and the sink all see it.

> **As amended 2026-09-15 — a refusal that names a subject is stamped with that subject's operator
> (T-0750).** Tenancy stays out of the validators (ADR-0061 D3): `OutOfBandAuditFailureSink` stamps a
> customer row that names a subject with **that subject's `TenantId`**, read in the sink's own scope
> past the tenant filter, and a row that names nobody keeps the ambient stamp — the default market's
> operator on an anonymous request. The read runs for every subject-named customer row and is
> *decisive* only when a validator or handler named the subject on an anonymous request; for a
> signed-in subject it returns the operator the session claim was minted from, which is the ambient
> tenant anyway. So a second operator's customer refused a sign-in lands in the second operator's feed
> (`OutOfBandAuditFailureSinkTenantTests` over SQLite: the subject's operator over the ambient tenant,
> the ambient stamp for a nameless row and for an unknown subject, the subject's operator with no
> ambient tenant at all, the skip with neither, the admin row untouched). The five session commands'
> explicit `CountryId => null` still decides the stamp of a refusal on an **unknown** address.

> **As amended 2026-09-16 — order ownership and account ownership are separate (T-0765).** A
> customer’s successful order-scoped or dispute-scoped act is stamped with the order’s operator,
> including when the account belongs to another company. The handler adopts the operator from the
> owned order (or, for creation, from the service address) before the success row is written.
>
> A refused order/dispute act adopts that operator only when the failure sink can prove that the
> referenced order or dispute belongs to the caller. An arbitrary foreign or missing resource id
> proves nothing: its refusal keeps the caller’s account company and the uniform not-found result.
> Account/session acts retain the account rule above. This replaces the earlier assumption that a
> signed-in caller’s account claim and every order’s operator are always the same.
>
> The admin by-user timeline and paged customer-action list read across operators, pinned by the
> requested user id, **after a tenant-filtered user read has proved the admin’s access to that
> account**. The account’s admin can therefore see its customer’s acts on another operator’s order;
> this does not grant a foreign admin a customer listing or unrestricted audit access. Resource reads
> keep their operator scope. The order-keyed customer panel is a separate, masked surface governed
> by [ADR-0061 D6](./adr-0061#d6-tenant-country-and-currency-agree-by-construction-and-two-validators-refuse-the-cases-that-could-break-it).
