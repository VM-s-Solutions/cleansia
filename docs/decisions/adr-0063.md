# ADR-0063 — Legal documents are versioned by effective date, stored per market, and a consent stamps the version

- **Status:** `accepted` — **2026-09-14**, owner ruling on Q-AUD-L2, implemented in T-0742 (backend +
  db, customer web, admin web) and consumed by T-0743 (the export) and T-0745 (the terms gate).
- **Date:** 2026-09-14
- **Supersedes:** [ADR-0062](/decisions/adr-0062) D4's first paragraph — *"the version is a code
  constant, pinned to the copy by the parity checker"* — and the Q-AUD-L2 default it recorded. Every
  other sentence of ADR-0062 D4 (server-side grant, `UserConsent.DocumentVersion`, a re-acceptance under
  a different version moves the row, employee subjects unversioned) stands and is amended in place there.
- **Builds on:** [ADR-0041](/decisions/adr-0041) D10 (versioned agreement text is *platform config*,
  tenantless — this is the customer-side realisation of the same idea; the employee agreement stays
  ADR-0041's and is not built here); [ADR-0060](/decisions/adr-0060) D3 (a money figure or a currency
  in customer copy comes from the market, never from the text); [ADR-0058](/decisions/adr-0058)
  (a customer's market is the `countryId` they chose).
- **Applies to:** `Cleansia.Core.Domain/Legal`, `Cleansia.Infra.Database/Seed/Legal` (+ the embedded
  markdown), `LegalDocumentRepository`, `Cleansia.Core.AppServices/Features/Legal` +
  `Services/LegalDocumentResolver` + `ConsentService`, `LegalController` on the two customer hosts,
  `AdminLegalController`, the customer `legal-pages` lib, the admin `legal-documents` lib,
  `agents/tools/check-booking-policy-parity.mjs`.
- **Owner ruling (2026-09-14, Q-AUD-L2):** *"I'd pick an identifier as an effective date from the
  beginning. That would be beneficial for us to keep track of all of the consents and documents that we
  ever had. I'd maybe have a separate storage for all of the documents. No real customers exist, so no
  reason to worry about someone who didn't accept a document without a date."*

> **One decision:** *what a consent row points at.* Until this ADR it pointed at a string in a C#
> constant (`"2026-09-draft"`) whose text lived in five locale JSON files that anybody could edit
> without moving the string. Now it points at a **stored document** whose identity is the date it
> started applying, whose text can never change once that date has passed, and whose every version the
> platform ever showed is still in the database. A dispute about *"which terms did I accept"* is
> answered by a row, not by `git blame` on a translation file.

**Why the constant was not enough.** A version string in code is a promise that *someone* bumps it
when the text changes; nothing enforced that the *text* a customer read on 2026-09-13 is the text the
row named `"2026-09-draft"` still names a year later. The owner's ask is the opposite guarantee: keep
every document we ever showed, under an identifier that says *when it applied*, so that a consent row
resolves to one immutable text forever.

## Decision

### D1 — Two tenantless entities; the version *is* the effective date

```
LegalDocument : BaseEntity                                  -- tenantless: platform copy per market, like CountryConfiguration's figures
    Audience        enum   Customer | Employee              -- only Customer is seeded or served today (D9)
    Type            enum   TermsOfService | PrivacyPolicy
    CountryId       varchar(26)?  FK Countries (Restrict)   -- the market this copy is for; NULL = the platform-wide text a market without its own falls back to
    EffectiveFrom   date                                    -- the day the text starts applying (UTC)
    Version         varchar(32)   NOT NULL                  -- EffectiveFrom as yyyy-MM-dd; the handle a consent row and an audit row carry
    Notes           varchar(500)?
  UNIQUE (Audience, Type, CountryId, Version) · IX (Audience, Type, EffectiveFrom) · IX (CountryId)

LegalDocumentText : BaseEntity
    LegalDocumentId varchar(26)   FK LegalDocuments (Cascade)
    Language        varchar(2)                              -- primary subtag, lower-case
    Title           varchar(200)
    ContentMarkdown text
    ContentHash     varchar(64)                             -- SHA-256 hex of ContentMarkdown; what the seeder compares and the admin sees
  UNIQUE (LegalDocumentId, Language)

UserConsent
  + LegalDocumentId varchar(26)?  FK LegalDocuments (Restrict)   -- the stored text the version names; NULL where DocumentVersion is
    DocumentVersion varchar(32)?                                 -- unchanged column, now always yyyy-MM-dd when set
```

- **The identifier is the date, not a name.** `LegalDocument.Create(...)` computes `Version =
  EffectiveFrom.ToString("yyyy-MM-dd")`; there is no other constructor and no setter. Two documents
  of one (audience, type, market) cannot share a date — the unique index says so — so *"which text"*
  and *"from when"* are one question.
- **Tenantless on purpose.** The texts are the brand's, shown identically by every operating company;
  what differs per market is the *copy* (a `CountryId` row), which is the same axis
  `CountryConfiguration` already uses. A `TenantId` here would make one company's customers unable to
  read a text another company's admin seeded.
- **The FK from the consent row is `Restrict`.** A document a customer accepted can never be deleted
  from under the row that names it; a `LegalDocument` has no delete path at all (no repository `Remove`
  caller, no admin action), and the seeder only adds.
- **`UserConsent.DocumentVersion` stays.** It is the human-readable handle the export and the audit
  rows already carry; `LegalDocumentId` is the join. The two are written together and are never allowed
  to disagree (`UserConsent.Grant/Regrant/AcceptVersion` take both).

### D2 — A document in force is immutable; a wording change is a new document

`LegalDocument.IsInForceOn(today) ⇔ EffectiveFrom <= today`. The seeder (D3) **refuses to change the
texts of a document already in force**: a seed file whose content hash differs from the stored text of
an in-force version is logged at Warning and **skipped** (`LegalSeedOutcome.SkippedImmutable`). The
entity does not know the date — `LegalDocumentText.Replace` exists for a document *not yet* in force,
which the seed may still correct up to the day before it applies — and the guard lives in the one
writer. What a customer accepted must read the same forever; a wording change is a **new folder with a
new effective date** (D3), and the previous document stays in the database, still pointed at by every
consent that was granted under it.

### D3 — The seed files are the source; every host seeds at start

The texts are **embedded markdown resources** in `Cleansia.Infra.Database`:

```
Seed/Legal/{customer|employee}/{terms-of-service|privacy-policy}/{ISO3|any}/{yyyy-MM-dd}/{xx}.md
---
title: Terms of Service
---
# …markdown…
```

- **The path is the identity** — audience, type, market (`any` = platform-wide, else the alpha-3 code
  resolved against `Countries`), effective date, language — and the file is the text. A file that does
  not fit the shape throws at read (`LegalSeedResource.Parse`), so a mis-filed version fails the seed
  loudly rather than becoming a document nobody meant. Line endings are normalised to LF before hashing
  so a CRLF checkout and an LF checkout store the same hash.
- **`LegalDocumentSeeder.SeedAsync(today)`** upserts: a new (audience, type, market, date) becomes a
  document with all its language texts; an existing one gains a missing language, has a not-yet-in-force
  text replaced, or is skipped per D2. Idempotent by the five-tuple plus the content hash; a country
  the catalogue does not know is skipped with a warning; a unique violation from a second host booting
  at the same moment is caught and treated as "already seeded".
- **It runs at every host start, in every environment**, through `LegalDocumentSeedHostedService`,
  registered in `AddDbContextBindings` **after** `NpgsqlTypeCatalogInitializer` so it waits behind an
  in-flight migration rather than retrying against it. Five bounded attempts; a host that cannot seed
  logs at Error and **starts anyway** — a customer can still book while the legal page serves the
  previous version. A deploy is therefore how a new version reaches the database; there is no admin
  authoring and no CMS (D8). → [Local orchestration](/architecture/local-orchestration#legal-seed)
- **The first version is `2026-09-14`**, customer terms and privacy, market `any`, five languages
  (`en`, `cs`, `sk`, `uk`, `ru`), the then-current locale copy moved verbatim with its draft notice and
  its `{{currency}}` placeholder. The five locale files keep only the page chrome (`legal.*`:
  eyebrow, contents, version, effective-from, load-error; `terms_page.title`, `privacy_page.title`).

### D4 — The document in force for a market, today

`ILegalDocumentRepository.GetInForceAsync(audience, type, countryId, today)`:

```
WHERE Audience = @audience AND Type = @type
  AND EffectiveFrom <= @today                                  -- UTC date
  AND (CountryId IS NULL OR CountryId = @countryId)
ORDER BY (CountryId IS NOT NULL) DESC, EffectiveFrom DESC      -- the market's own copy beats the platform-wide one,
LIMIT 1                                                        -- then the newest date wins
```

- **Market-specific over platform-wide, then newest.** A market with its own copy reads it even when a
  newer platform-wide version exists; a market without one reads the platform-wide text. A document
  dated in the future is invisible until its day.
- **Language:** `TextForOrFallback(language)` — the request's primary subtag (`cs-CZ` reads `cs`), else
  `en`, else the first text by ordinal. A legal page in the wrong language beats no legal page.
- **Which market:** `LegalDocumentResolver.ResolveInForceAsync(type, countryId)` takes the caller's
  `countryId` and falls back to the **default market** (`CountryConfiguration.IsDefaultMarket`) when
  none is given; it logs a Warning and returns `null` when nothing is in force, and the read answers
  `legal.document_not_found`.

### D5 — Placeholders are filled by the server, before parsing

`LegalMarkdownRenderer.Render(markdown, placeholders)` substitutes `{{name}}` tokens **before** the
markdown is parsed, so a market's figure is escaped like any other text, and renders with Markdig's
pipeline built with `DisableHtml()` — raw HTML in a seed file is rendered as text, never passed
through. The one placeholder today is `{{currency}}`, filled from the market's `DefaultCurrencyCode`
(ADR-0060 D3: the currency is the market's, never written into a text). `PlaceholdersIn(markdown)`
enumerates a text's tokens for the seed tests and the admin preview.

The parity checker keeps its **no-baked-figures** rule (`1 000 000 Kč` in a legal text is a red build)
and now reads it over the seed files — every language of the **newest** version per type
(`newestSeedVersion`) — instead of the locale JSON; its §4 (the constant-to-locale version pin) is
deleted with the constant.

### D6 — A consent stamps the document in force for the subject's market

`IConsentService.TryGrantAsync(userId, type, LegalDocument? document)` — the caller resolves the
document; the service stamps it:

- **Who resolves it.** `Register`, `GoogleAuth`/`AppleAuth` (provisioning) and `GrantConsent` on a
  customer host resolve `(Customer, LegalDocument.TypeFor(consentType), market)` through
  `ILegalDocumentResolver`, where the market is the request's `countryId` for an anonymous act
  (ADR-0061 D3's marker) and the default market otherwise. `RegisterEmployee` and the partner hosts'
  `GrantConsent` pass **no document**: an employee's row stays `DocumentVersion = null,
  LegalDocumentId = null` until ADR-0041's agreement lands (D9).
- **What is stamped.** A new row: `Grant(..., document?.Version, document?.Id)`. A withdrawn row:
  `Regrant(...)` with both. An already-granted row: **the document's identity is compared, not the
  version string** — `existing.LegalDocumentId == document.Id` is a no-op (`false`), a different
  document is `AcceptVersion(version, ip, ua, id)` (`true`). Identity rather than string because a
  market's own copy can legitimately be seeded under the **same date** as the platform-wide text, and a
  customer who accepted the platform-wide copy and then the market's has accepted two texts.
- **Consent types with no document** (`Marketing`, `DataProcessing`, …) resolve to `null` and stamp
  nothing — `LegalDocument.TypeFor` answers only `TermsOfService` and `PrivacyPolicy`.
- **The audit rows carry the string.** `RegistrationEvidence.termsVersion`/`privacyVersion` and
  `OrderBookingEvidence.termsVersionAccepted` are unchanged in shape and now read `yyyy-MM-dd`; the
  export's consent section carries `documentVersion` and `legalDocumentId` (ADR-0062 D5 as amended).

### D7 — What a new version means, and what it does not

- **From its effective date it is what the pages show and what a new consent stamps.** Nothing else
  moves: every `UserConsent` row granted under the previous document keeps its `LegalDocumentId`, and
  the previous document keeps its texts (D2).
- **No re-prompt.** A customer who accepted an earlier version is not asked again on sign-in, and a
  booking by a signed-in customer whose account holds both consents — under *any* version — needs no
  tick (ADR-0062 D4 as amended). Re-acceptance on a new version is a product ticket if the lawyer ever
  wants it (the old A6 "revisit"), and D6's `AcceptVersion` is the row it would move.
- **`CreateOrder` records the tick and grants no consent rows for it.** A signed-in customer who has
  no consent rows is asked to tick at checkout, the booking row records `termsAccepted` with the
  version, and the account is *not* given `UserConsent` rows by the booking — in production every
  account has both rows from registration (server-side, D6), so the case only arises for DEV accounts
  created before the grant existed. Reported here as the shipped state, not hidden.
- **A version is never rewritten to look like a previous one.** There is no admin edit, no rename, no
  "supersede" flag: the date order is the history.

### D8 — Read surfaces

| Surface | Route | Policy | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer web + customer mobile hosts | `GET api/Legal/GetDocument?type=&countryId=&language=` | `[AllowAnonymous]`, `interactive` rate window | `LegalDocumentDto(Type, CountryId, Version, EffectiveFrom, Language, Title, ContentHtml, ContentHash)`; `country.not_serviced` for a non-market country, `legal.document_not_found` when nothing is in force |
| Admin host | `GET api/AdminLegal/get-versions?audience=&type=&countryId=` | `CanViewCountryConfigurations` | `LegalDocumentVersionDto[]` — every version with `IsInForce`, `Notes` and per-language `(Language, Title, ContentHash)` |
| Admin host | `GET api/AdminLegal/get-document/{id}?language=` | `CanViewCountryConfigurations` | `AdminLegalDocumentDto` — one language's `ContentMarkdown` and server-rendered `ContentHtml` |

- **The customer `/terms` and `/privacy` pages** render `ContentHtml` for the chosen market and the
  current UI language (SSR, `RenderMode.Server`; the HTML is ours and goes through Angular's sanitizer)
  with the title, *Effective from {date}* and *Version {version}*. The register form's and the wizard's
  "I accept the terms" links point at the same pages.
- **The admin `/legal-documents` page** (under the configuration area, nav gated like the endpoint by
  `CanViewCountryConfigurations`) lists every version per audience, type and market with its effective
  date, `yyyy-MM-dd` version, in-force flag and languages, previews one language at a time (opening in
  the admin's UI language, falling back to English) with its SHA-256, and says in one translated
  sentence that **a new version is a seed file plus a deploy**. Read-only: no authoring.
- Reads are not audited (ADR-0012 D7); the customer read is credential-less and lives in the SSR
  transfer cache like the other anonymous overviews.

### D9 — What stays out

No employee-audience document is seeded, served or stamped (ADR-0041 territory: the partner hosts'
`GrantConsent` and `RegisterEmployee` grant with no document, and both mobile partner apps still park
their tick for a first sign-in that delivers it). No re-prompt on a new version. No admin authoring,
edit, approval workflow or CMS. No in-app rendering on the mobile apps (they link to the web pages).
No change to the consent types. No second placeholder until a text needs one. No `AgreementVersion`
tables — this is their customer-side shape, and the employee side stays ADR-0041's to build.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|
| Keep the code constant and bump it on edits (ADR-0062 D4 as first accepted) | Nothing pins the constant to the *text*; a locale edit without a bump silently re-defines what `"2026-09-draft"` meant. The owner's ask is an identifier that says when a text applied and a store of every text. |
| Build ADR-0041's `AgreementVersion` tables and put the customer texts in them | Right shape, wrong audience and wrong workflow: they carry review status and an employee-facing acceptance flow, and the employee agreement is still unwritten. Two entities with the date as identity are the customer-side minimum; the employee side can adopt them or keep its own. |
| Admin authoring (a CMS page that writes `LegalDocuments`) | A legal text is reviewed by a lawyer and versioned by a date, not typed into a form on a Tuesday; the seed-file-plus-deploy path gives a diff, a review and a commit for free. The admin page is read-only by design. |
| A version string per language (five versions per document) | A consent is to a document, not to a translation; one date per (audience, type, market) with N language texts under it, each hashed, is what the row needs to point at. |
| Store the rendered HTML | The markdown is the source and is hashed; the HTML is a rendering with a market's figure in it (D5) and would have to be re-rendered per market anyway. |
| Re-prompt every customer on a new version now | No real customers exist to re-prompt, and whether a new version needs re-acceptance is the lawyer's call per version; the row-moving mechanism (`AcceptVersion`) exists for the day it is wanted. |

## Consequences

- **`LegalDocumentVersions` is deleted** with every reader; `grep -r LegalDocumentVersions src` is empty.
  The parity checker's §4 is gone; its placeholder rule reads the seed files.
- **The five customer locale files lost their `terms_page.section*` / `privacy_page.section*` keys**;
  the copy lives in ten seed files under `Seed/Legal/customer/…/any/2026-09-14/`. The
  *owner-blankable copy* idiom in `agents/knowledge/patterns-frontend.md` no longer has a user.
- **`Initial` was regenerated** for `LegalDocuments`, `LegalDocumentTexts`, `UserConsents.LegalDocumentId`
  and (in the same batch) `Disputes.TextRetainedUntil` — the shipped id is **`20260914115922`**; the
  one DEV drop already owed (MS-2) now belongs to it. The model has **87 tables**.
- **Markdig** joins `Directory.Packages.props`. **NSwag**: the customer client gained `LegalClient`, the
  admin client `AdminLegalClient`, and the customer mobile spec the legal read.
- **A new legal version is a deploy.** The runbook step is: add the folder with the date, all five
  languages, commit, deploy; every host seeds it at start; the pages and the consents switch on the
  date. An edit to an in-force folder is a Warning in the logs and no change in the database — which is
  the point.
- **The customer web `legal-pages` lib is a fetch-and-render**: facade + signals, `[innerHTML]` through
  the sanitizer, an error state with retry. Its markup is pinned by a component spec.
- `docs/domain/model.md`, `/product/business-rules#customer-record`, the auth flow, the admin and
  customer app overviews and the `legal-document` role card carry the current state; ADR-0062 D4 is
  amended in place to point here.

## Verification (how a reviewer checks compliance — every file named exists in the tree)

1. `LegalDocument.Create` derives `Version` from `EffectiveFrom` as `yyyy-MM-dd`; `TypeFor` maps only
   the two document-backed consent types; `TextForOrFallback` reads the primary subtag, then `en`, then
   the first by ordinal (`Cleansia.Tests/Domain/Legal/LegalDocumentTests.cs`).
2. The seeder is idempotent (a second run over the same files changes nothing), adds a missing language,
   replaces a not-yet-in-force text, **skips an in-force text whose hash differs with a warning**, and
   skips an unknown country (`Cleansia.Tests/Infrastructure/LegalDocumentSeederTests.cs`); the embedded
   files parse and the seed lands on a real Postgres and is read back rendered
   (`Cleansia.IntegrationTests/Features/Legal/LegalDocumentSeedAndReadTests.cs`).
3. In-force resolution: an older and a newer effective date, a future date, a market-specific copy
   over the platform-wide one, and the language fallback
   (`Cleansia.Tests/Infrastructure/LegalDocumentRepositoryInForceTests.cs`,
   `Cleansia.Tests/Services/LegalDocumentResolverTests.cs`, `Cleansia.Tests/Features/Legal/GetLegalDocumentHandlerTests.cs`).
4. The seed hosted service is registered **after** `NpgsqlTypeCatalogInitializer` and is a plain
   `IHostedService`, awaited inline, not a `BackgroundService`
   (`Cleansia.Tests/Startup/BootDatabaseIoTests.cs`).
5. A consent stamps the in-force document's version **and id**; a re-grant under a different document
   identity moves the row and one under the same identity is a no-op; an employee subject stamps
   neither (`Cleansia.Tests/Features/Auth/RegisterConsentAndEvidenceTests.cs`,
   `Cleansia.Tests/Features/Auth/RegisterEmployeeConsentTests.cs`,
   `Cleansia.IntegrationTests/Features/Auth/RegistrationConsentAndAuditTests.cs`).
6. Routes and policies: anonymous `200` on the customer and customer-mobile hosts, `404` on the partner
   hosts, admin `401`/`403`/`200` (`Cleansia.HostTests/Tests/LegalDocumentRouteTests.cs`).
7. `agents/tools/check-booking-policy-parity.mjs` reads the newest seed version per type for the
   no-baked-figures rule; its self-test turns red on a seed file with a baked `1 000 000 Kč`.
8. `grep -rn "LegalDocumentVersions" src` is empty; `grep -n "section" apps/cleansia.app/src/assets/i18n/en.json`
   finds no `terms_page`/`privacy_page` section key; `Seed/Legal/customer/*/any/2026-09-14/` holds
   five files per type.
