> **One decision:** *what happens to an operating company's rows, markets, people and money when the
> company stops trading — in three steps that each leave the platform consistent, run from the admin app
> by that company's own administrators, and never delete a row.* A company **deactivates** (its markets
> vanish from every directory and predicate at once, and its cleaners can no longer sign in), **winds
> down** (from a date, every customer and cleaner is told by e-mail, every open booking on or after the
> date is cancelled and refunded in full by the platform — and a refund that failed is re-driven until it
> succeeds — every recurring template is paused, every Plus is cancelled at period end, unspent credit is
> discharged once the door has closed, and its last pay period is closed and invoiced — idempotently, so
> the sweep can be re-run until nothing is left), and **archives** (once every live fact is settled and the
> chargeback horizon has passed, its books are frozen at the request, a sealed bundle is written to blob
> storage with its hash on the row, and from then on nothing can change the books except the law's own
> writes — retention and erasure). The rows stay for the ten years accounting law asks for. No holding
> role, no cross-company read, no per-company Stripe, no data deletion — those are named as not this ADR.

## Context (every citation opened in the working tree at `10cf6298`, the commit before T-0760)

### What existed, step by step

**The `Tenants` row was inert.** `Tenant : BaseEntity` — `Id` (assigned, `[MaxLength(26)]`), `Name`
(200), and `IsActive` inherited from `BaseEntity`; three columns in the DDL. **`Tenant.IsActive` had zero
readers**; the CRC card said so and named the retirement condition. The registry had one production reader
since Batch 1, `ITenantRepository.GetAllIdsAsync`, and it **deliberately returned deactivated companies
too** so the retention sweep kept running for them. `CountryConfiguration` carried the navigation
`OperatorTenant`; the stamped tables carry the FK **without** a navigation. The two Postgres fixtures
inserted the row by raw SQL with exactly three columns.

**"Is this country open for business?" was asked in one place and answered from one column pair.**
`CountryRepository.GetServicedAsync` and `IsServicedAsync`, both `IsServiced && IsActive` on `Countries`.
Fifteen files read through them and nothing else asked the question: `GetMarkets` (the directory behind
every customer surface and the partner picker), `OrderAddressResolver` (six calls; refuses
`country.not_serviced`), `QuoteOrder`, `QuotePlusSavings`, the three catalogue overviews,
`GetMembershipPlans`, `CreateMembershipSubscription`, `CreateMembershipCheckoutSession`, `ApproveEmployee`,
`UpdateEmployee`, `UpdateAddressInfo`, `SetDefaultMarket`, `GetServicedCountries`.
`OperatorTenantResolver.ResolveAsync` read the same two flags off the loaded entity instead of the
repository. **The default market is chosen by the directory**, and **every anonymous identity request
depends on it**: `Login`, `AdminLogin`, `MobileLogin`, `ConfirmUserEmail`, `RequestPasswordChange`,
`ChangePassword` implement `IOperatorScopedRequest` with `CountryId => null`, so
`OperatorTenantScopeBehavior` asks the resolver for the default market and, **when no default is listed,
refuses `tenant.not_found` before the handler runs**.

**Per-country delisting existed; per-company did not.** `SetCountryServiced` switches one country off
(never gated; switching on is gated by a configuration naming an active currency, **no operator term**).
A company serving two countries needed two switches and nothing refused its staff.

**Sign-in had one shared validator and one mint.** Each login handler refuses `!user.IsActive`; the
partner/admin ones gate the profile (T-0754). Every mint crosses `TokenService.GenerateTokenAsync`, which
enforces the audience/profile pair as an invariant — **an Administrator is admitted on the Partner and
Mobile audiences too** — and adopts the user's tenant. Rotation is the second mint (`RefreshToken`).
Access tokens live 15 minutes on the admin host and 30 on the partner mobile host. A cleaner may sign in
on a customer host as a customer, where the customer `GdprController` admits every profile.

**Cancelling with a full refund existed per order, twice — with two different refund keys.**
`AdminCancelOrder` — no fee, full refund through `IRefundService` with `RefundReason.CustomerCancellation`,
the customer told, credit returned when unpaid, the express waiver released, every assigned cleaner told,
loyalty revoked — a handler with **eight collaborators**. `CancelUnfilledOrders` is the sweep twin with
**the money term** `PaymentStatus == Paid || (Cash && RecurringTemplateId == null)`, override per tenant

absorbs the Stripe fee, commit inside the loop. **The refund key is derived from the reason**

anything else → `"admin"`), so the admin cancel writes `refund:{OrderId}:cancel` and a
`ServiceNotRendered` refund writes `refund:{OrderId}:admin`. **A refund that Stripe refuses stays
`Pending`**, is resolved-to-existing **only when `Succeeded`**, and is re-driven **only by a second
`IssueRefundAsync` on the same key** — **there was no retry sweep**. The reason reaches the customer only
when `CancelledBy == System` (`OrderMappers`); `OrderCancellationReasons` held three keys.
`CancellationAssessor.BlockedReason` is the shared "cannot cancel at all" gate: Cancelled, Completed,
InProgress.

**Pausing a template, cancelling Plus, discharging credit, closing a period — each existed per row.**
`SetRecurringBookingActive` (`Pause()`/`Resume()`; the materialiser skips inactive templates).
`CancelMembershipSubscription` (`CancelSubscriptionAtPeriodEndAsync` then `MarkCancellationRequested()`;

seven-day TTL**. A consumer is a thin `[QueueTrigger]` shell over a `Functions.Core` handler with a
`-poison` twin over `PoisonHandlerBase`. `host.json`: `visibilityTimeout` 30 s, `batchSize` 1,
`maxDequeueCount` 5, no `functionTimeout`; the plan is **Dedicated**, where the default `functionTimeout`
is **30 minutes**.

**The write seam, and the one path that is not a consumer.** `CleansiaDbContext.CommitAsync` — stamps
`Created`/`Updated` and the tenant on every `Added` `ITenantEntity`, then `SaveChangesAsync`. Every
request commit goes through it (`UnitOfWorkPipelineBehavior`) and every job calls it directly. **Writers
that bypass it:** `ExecuteUpdateAsync` (the retention sweep's code clearing; the login throttle), the two
self-committing counter repositories (raw `INSERT … ON CONFLICT`, ADR-0046 D2.1),
`IRefreshTokenRepository.BulkRevokeIgnoringConcurrencyAsync`, the credit spend's conditional `UPDATE`, and
`OutOfBandAuditFailureSink`, which writes the failure row **from a fresh scope with `SaveChangesAsync`
directly**. A typed exception with keyed errors already had a host mapping: `RequestValidationException`
→ `RequestValidationExceptionFilterAttribute` → 400 ProblemDetails, applied to every controller by
`CleansiaApiController`. **The Stripe webhook is a synchronous request, not a consumer:**
`PaymentController.Webhook` on the Customer, Partner and Mobile.Customer hosts sends
`HandlePaymentNotification.Command : ICommand` through MediatR; a chargeback **adds a `Dispute`** under
the order's tenant override, a late settlement modifies the `Order`, a subscription event modifies the
`UserMembership`. Stripe treats any non-2xx as a delivery failure and retries with backoff for up to three
days against an endpoint every company shares. There was no poison twin on that path.

**Retention and erasure keep writing after everything else stops.** The nine per-company windows run
weekly under each company's override; the order-PII sweep **modifies tracked `Order` rows and commits**
(`Order.AnonymizeCustomerData()` blanks name, e-mail, phone, `UserId`, notes); consents, notifications,
devices are `RemoveRange` + commit; dispute text is blanked once `TextRetainedUntil` is past. Erasure
(`GdprDeletionService`) modifies the subject's rows and commits once through the UnitOfWork pipeline.
Receipts and payout invoices are never touched by any sweep — Czech and Slovak accounting law keeps them
ten years. `Employee` carries `PassportId`, `NationalityId`, `EmergencyContactName/Phone`,
`RejectionReason`, `ApprovalNotes`, an `IBAN` beside `LegalEntityName` and `RegistrationNumber`;
`EmployeePayoutDetails` **is the bank account**.

**Money.** One Stripe account for the holding (ADR-0061 §Rulings). Payout numbering is per company since
T-0757.

### What the rulings fix as fact

- The three letters are **scope levels, not a runtime sequence**: the scout's (b) was defined as *"(a)
  plus a wind-down"* and (c) as *"(b) plus archival"*, and *"build up to (c)"* chooses the level. The
  order in which an operator *uses* the three is this ADR's to lay down (D4), and it is not the
  alphabetical one — a company announces its closing date before it closes.
- "Deactivate" and "wind down" are two different acts because they answer two different questions — *may
  this company still take and serve work?* (a switch, instant, reversible) and *what happens to the work it
  already took?* (a sweep over money and people, long, irreversible per order). Folding them into one
  command would make the switch pay the sweep's cost and lose the reversibility.
- "Read-only" after archive means the **books** — the rows that record the company's transactions and
  obligations. It does not mean a customer of the company loses their account, their history, their right
  to export or erase, or their ability to sign in: those rows are the person's, stamped with the company
  that registered them (ADR-0061 D5), and the law's writes on them continue.
- Nothing is deleted. A wound-down company's rows are its accounting record for ten years; the bundle is a
  copy for the day the database is not the place to look, not a replacement.
- **A seal is only honest over settled books.** Money that can still move — a pending refund, a pay row
  not yet invoiced, a receipt not yet issued, a chargeback the card network still allows — is not a fact
  the freeze may refuse to record. The archive therefore waits for each of those, and refuses while any is
  outstanding (D3).

## Decision

> **Contract principle.** *A company's lifecycle is a small state machine on its own registry row, driven
> by its own administrators, whose every transition reuses a seam that already exists per row — the
> serviced-country predicate, the token mint, the admin cancellation, the refund key, the template pause,
> the Plus cancel, the credit discharge, the period close, the e-mail queue, the blob client, the commit —
> and adds exactly one new seam: the archived-company write guard at the one place every write crosses.*

### D1 — Deactivation: one switch on the row, one predicate at the repository, one sign-in gate

**The row.** `Tenant` moved from `BaseEntity` to **`Auditable`** — it is admin-edited from this ADR on,
which is the D7 sort (ADR-0061: *"keep `Auditable` if it is admin-edited"*) — and gained the lifecycle
columns. `Auditable` already carried `DeactivatedBy`/`DeactivatedOn` and `Deactivated(by, on)`; this ADR
added the inverse the base class never had, `Auditable.Reactivated()`, because a lifecycle needs both
directions and a private setter cannot be reached from the subclass.

```
Tenant : Auditable                                  // was BaseEntity; CreatedBy/On, UpdatedBy/On, DeactivatedBy/On, IsActive come with it
  Id                    varchar(26)   (unchanged)
  Name                  varchar(200)  (unchanged — the registry label; the notice prints CompanyInfo.LegalName, D2)
  WindDownFrom          date?         // the last day of service, market-local (D2)
  WindDownRequestedOn   timestamptz?  // the notice's idempotency instant (D2 step 1)
  WindDownRequestedBy   varchar(26)?  // the admin's user id — a JOB INPUT: the refund actor of every wind-down refund
  WindDownRunStartedOn  timestamptz?  // set by the sweep as its first commit, cleared by RecordWindDownRun (overlap guard, D2)
  WindDownLastRunOn     timestamptz?  // stamped by the sweep at the end of every completed run
  ArchiveRequestedOn    timestamptz?  // the freeze instant (D3); the bundle folder's stamp is derived from it
  ArchiveRequestedBy    varchar(26)?  // the admin's user id — shown on the page
  ArchivedOn            timestamptz?  // the bundle exists
  ArchiveManifestSha256 char(64)?     // the manifest's hash — the row's cross-check on the container (D3)

  IsDeactivated        => !IsActive                       // Auditable.Deactivated() keeps IsActive and DeactivatedOn in step
  IsWindDownRequested  => WindDownFrom is not null
  IsWindDownRunning(now) => WindDownRunStartedOn is not null && WindDownRunStartedOn > now - 1h
  IsFrozen             => ArchiveRequestedOn is not null  // the guard's predicate (D3)
  IsArchived           => ArchivedOn is not null
  State                => Archived > Frozen > Deactivated > WindingDown > Operating   // CompanyLifecycleState, the highest that applies

  Create(id, name)               // stamps CreatedBy = "seed"
  Deactivate(byUserId, now)      // Auditable.Deactivated; refused when IsFrozen or already deactivated
  Reactivate()                   // Auditable.Reactivated + clears the WindDown* stamps; refused when IsFrozen or not deactivated
  RequestWindDown(from, by, now) // refused when IsWindDownRequested or IsFrozen
  StartWindDownRun(now) / RecordWindDownRun(now)
  RequestArchive(by, now)        // refused unless IsDeactivated && IsWindDownRequested; refused when IsFrozen
  MarkArchived(manifestSha256, now)  // refused unless IsFrozen
```

The seed row and **both Postgres fixtures' raw inserts** carry `CreatedBy = 'seed'`, `CreatedOn = now()`
(`Auditable`'s two NOT NULL columns); every other new column is `NULL`. **One regenerated `Initial`** —
**`20260915232921`**, 87 tables, `Tenants` at eighteen columns (D13 of ADR-0061 governs: the DEV drop is
owed once, at deploy time). Why columns on the row and not a `TenantLifecycleEvent` table: the *state* is
one row's; the *events* are the admin audit trail (four labels below). Why no `ArchiveBlobPath`: it is
`<tenantId>/<ArchiveRequestedOn:yyyyMMddTHHmmssZ>/` by construction, and a column that restates a function
of another column is the proportionality rule's first target (C21). Why the two `By` columns stay:
`WindDownRequestedBy` is read by the sweep (the refund actor), and the page shows both beside their
instants; the audit trail is the record of the *act*, the row is the *state* — neither reads the other.
Why `IsActive` is kept and not `Ignore`d: `Auditable.Deactivated()` writes it in the same call as
`DeactivatedOn`, so it is one write with two reads, and S10's *"every query that should hide deactivated
rows filters `IsActive` itself"* stays true of this table too. **Readers use the predicates**, never the
columns — with one named exception: the repository's LINQ-to-SQL predicate below reads the column, because
a computed property does not translate.

**The market predicate — at the repository, so every reader inherits (C8).** `CountryRepository.GetServicedAsync`
and `IsServicedAsync` gained a third term beside `IsServiced && IsActive`:

```csharp
&& Context.CountryConfigurations.Any(cc => cc.CountryId == c.Id
                                        && cc.OperatorTenant != null
                                        && cc.OperatorTenant.IsActive)
```

`OperatorTenantResolver.ResolveAsync` stopped reading the two flags off the loaded entity and asks
`countryRepository.IsServicedAsync(countryId)` — one line, and the resolver, the scope behaviour and
`GetMarkets` now answer "is this a market?" from the same predicate the address resolver, the quotes, the
catalogue overviews, the plans, the wizard's country picker, the two membership writers and the employee
work-country rules already read. **Two paths the draft counted as readers were not**, and each gained its
own rule in review (`92c5d674`): `CreateRecurringBooking` refuses a saved address in a country nobody
operates, and `AddSavedAddress` refuses a new address in one — both `country.not_serviced`, the same answer
the one-off booking gets from `OrderAddressResolver`. **Nothing else in the read paths changed**: no
`OperatorTenant` include on `CountryConfigurationRepository`, no split in `CreateOrder`'s D6 rule, no
hand-edit of `CreateMembershipSubscription` or `CreateMembershipCheckoutSession`. The effect of
deactivation, inherited:

| Reader | Effect of deactivation |
|---|---|
| `GetMarkets` (the directory behind every customer surface and the partner picker) | the market is not listed; the clients' switcher drops it; a stored code falls to the default market (ADR-0058 D2) |
| `Country/GetServiced` (the order wizard's picker) | the country is not offered |
| `OperatorTenantScopeBehavior` | every anonymous write naming it — register, social provisioning, promo, referral, quote, guest order — is refused **`country.not_serviced`**, the user-input key, because a closed shop is not a configuration defect |
| `OrderAddressResolver`, `QuoteOrder`, `QuotePlusSavings`, `CreateRecurringBooking` (its own rule), `AddSavedAddress` (its own rule), `UpdateAddressInfo` | a signed-in customer's quote, booking, recurring booking or address in the deactivated company's country is refused `country.not_serviced` — the quote and the booking agree |
| the three catalogue overviews, `GetMembershipPlans` | the closed market's catalogue and plans are not served |
| `CreateMembershipSubscription`, `CreateMembershipCheckoutSession` | a customer cannot buy Plus in a market nobody operates |
| `ApproveEmployee`, `UpdateEmployee`, `UpdateAddressInfo` (work country) | a cleaner cannot be approved into, or moved into, a deactivated company's country |
| `OperatorTenantResolver.ResolveDefaultMarketAsync` | a deactivated default market is not listed, so it cannot be the default — **which is why the switch is refused while the company holds the default (below)** |
| `SetDefaultMarket` | a deactivated operator's market cannot be flagged the default — refused **`country.not_serviced`** by its own serviced rule (the operator term is inside it) |
| `SetCountryServiced(true)` | a country whose configuration's operator is deactivated cannot be switched on — `country.market_not_ready` (`OperatorIsActiveAsync`, one `ITenantRepository.GetByIdAsync`) |

`Country.IsServiced` the column is untouched; per-country delisting stays what it is. The `GetMarkets`
null-operator log became unreachable (a country with no operator is no longer serviced) and is left in
place as a belt. **Accepted consequence:** a country that is serviced but has no operator — a seed defect
logged at Error and omitted from the directory — is now "not serviced" everywhere, which is the more honest
answer. **Accepted residual:** an admin-app dropdown that reads `Country/GetServiced` to *configure* a
country shows nothing of a deactivated company; the lifecycle page needs no such dropdown, and the acts
that remain after deactivation go through none.

The materialiser is the one job that creates **new** business rows without a customer's act and reads no
country predicate, so it gained the fact directly: grouped by `template.TenantId` as it already was, it
reads the `Tenant` row once per group and **skips a deactivated company's templates** (the templates are
paused by D2 anyway; this is the belt for a customer who resumes one). `SetRecurringBookingActive(true)` is
**not** refused on a deactivated company — the resumed template materialises nothing, which is an unlikely
path failing quietly, and CLAUDE.md §4 says not to build a customer-facing key in three clients for it.

**The sign-in gate — cleaners on partner audiences.** *"Its staff sign-in refused"* is implemented for the
**Employee** profile on the **Partner and Mobile audiences**, and **not** for administrators, and **not**
for a cleaner signing in as a customer on a customer host. One small **scoped** service,
`ICompanySignInGate.RefusalForAsync(user, audience)` in `Core.AppServices.Tenancy`, returns
`auth.company_deactivated` or null; it reads `ITenantRepository.GetByIdAsync(user.TenantId)` **once per
request scope** — memoised, so the command and the mint share one read — and asks `tenant.IsDeactivated`.
Call sites, mirroring exactly the T-0754 profile gate they sit beside: `PartnerLogin`, `MobilePartnerLogin`,
`GoogleAuth`/`AppleAuth` (the existing-staff branch on a partner host), `RefreshToken.Handler` (after the
profile pin, when `RequiredAudience` is a partner audience). `TokenService.GenerateTokenAsync` enforces it
as the same class of invariant as `Admits` through the same gate instance: a partner-audience session is
never minted for a cleaner of a deactivated company; the issuing command refuses it first. Customers skip
the read entirely (profile check before the repository call), so the one PK read is paid by staff sign-ins
only. **An Administrator of the company is still admitted on the Partner and Mobile audiences** —
consistent with O-1, and the D4 table says so. The gate's profile set is one constant
(`CompanySignInGate.RefusedProfiles`).

*Why administrators are not refused.* They are the hands that settle the company: mark the last invoices
paid, answer the last dispute, run the wind-down again, request the archive, and — after archive — look
things up for a tax office. No holding role exists (ADR-0061 D5; T-0748); refusing them would leave the
company between (a) and (c) with nobody able to act on it from inside the product. When T-0748 lands, a
one-line ruling moves administrators into the constant. **Escalated as O-1 with this default applied.**
The accepted residual beside it: **any** administrator of the company can `ReactivateCompany`; the holding
cannot prevent it until T-0748.

*Why no bulk session revocation.* A cleaner already signed in keeps their access token for at most 30
minutes on the partner mobile host, 15 on the others; the next refresh is refused by the gate. Thirty
minutes of residual access to a company whose markets have just vanished and whose every act is audited
is the *"an unlikely path fails ugly"* CLAUDE.md §4 says not to build for.

*The surviving GDPR channel.* A cleaner of a deactivated company reaches Art. 15/17/20 on the **customer
host** (the customer audience admits every profile) — the partner `GdprController` becomes unreachable for
them, and the wind-down notice to cleaners names the customer app as where to export or erase.

**The commands.** Two, on the admin host, acting on **the admin's own company** — there is no tenant id on
the wire (S4):

```
DeactivateCompany.Command()  : ICommand<Response(State)>   [AuditAction("company.deactivate", ResourceType = "Tenant")]  Policy.CanDeactivateCompany
ReactivateCompany.Command()  : ICommand<Response(State)>   [AuditAction("company.reactivate", ResourceType = "Tenant")]  Policy.CanReactivateCompany
```

Validator refusals, in order: **`tenant.not_found`** (the ambient company is not in the registry — first,
on every lifecycle act, so a frozen check never masks a missing row), `company.archived` (a lifecycle
command on a frozen or archived company — reactivation from the archive is **not offered**, D5),
`company.already_deactivated` / `company.not_deactivated`, and — **the one refusal that protects the
platform, not the company (C1)** — **`company.operates_default_market`**: `DeactivateCompany` is refused
while the flagged default configuration's `OperatorTenantId`
(`ICountryConfigurationRepository.GetDefaultMarketAsync`) is the ambient tenant. Without it, one click
delists the default market, `GetMarkets` lists no default (unless another listed market shares the default
currency), `ResolveDefaultMarketAsync` answers `(true, null)`, and **every anonymous identity request on
every host — `AdminLogin` included, for every company's administrators — is refused `tenant.not_found`**
until somebody runs SQL. The owner moves the default with `SetDefaultMarket` first, then deactivates.
**Stated plainly: with one operating company in the registry (`cleansia-cz`, the tree today), that
company cannot be deactivated at all until a second operator exists and holds the default market.**
Closing the *platform* is not a company lifecycle event and is not this ADR. `DeactivateCompany`
additionally **re-enqueues the wind-down sweep** when `WindDownFrom` is set (D2 — the door closes and the
sweep catches anything booked since the announcement). Evidence: `CompanyLifecycleSnapshot(State,
WindDownFrom)` before and after, through `IAuditContext.RecordChange("Tenant", id, …)` — no PII. The
labels are frozen by `CompanyLifecycleAuditLabelTests`. Policies: `Policy.CanViewCompanyLifecycle`,
`CanDeactivateCompany`, `CanReactivateCompany`, `CanWindDownCompany`, `CanArchiveCompany` — all
`AdminOnly`, rostered in `FrozenPermissionMapTests`; the writes sit under the `auth` rate window.

**What deactivation is not.** It cancels nothing, refunds nothing, e-mails nobody, and closes no period —
D2 does those, and an operator who deactivates without a wind-down has simply closed the door first and
will run D2 afterwards (D4 says how). It does not touch `Country.IsServiced`, the filter, the claim, or any
DTO. It does not delete the `TenantConfiguration` rows, the `CompanyInfo` row or anything else.

### D2 — Wind-down: one request, one idempotent sweep that converges

**The request.**

```
WindDownCompany.Command(DateOnly? FromDate) : ICommand<Response(State, WindDownFrom)>   [AuditAction("company.wind_down", ResourceType = "Tenant")]  Policy.CanWindDownCompany
```

Validator: `tenant.not_found` first; not frozen/archived (`company.archived`); a re-run (no date) while
`IsWindDownRunning(now)` is refused `company.wind_down_in_progress` (the sweep's stamp, one-hour staleness
escape, C9); a date when one is already set is `company.wind_down_already_requested` — **the date is set
once**; an operator who needs an earlier date cancels the remaining orders by hand, because a cancelled
order cannot be un-cancelled and a rule for "moving the date" would have to say which direction is safe;
no date and none set is a plain `common.required` on `FromDate`; a date before today in the company's
**easternmost** market (`company.wind_down_date_in_past` — "today" is the latest local date among the
company's markets, so a date already past in one of them is refused while it is still today further
west; UTC when it has none; `WindDownCutoff.LocalDate`). Handler:
`tenant.RequestWindDown(from, adminId, now)` on the first request, and **one** message on a new queue
`company-wind-down`, key `wind-down:{tenantId}:{now:yyyyMMddHHmmss}`, envelope tenant = the company —
through `CompanyWindDownDispatch.EnqueueAsync`, which is shared with `DeactivateCompany`, drained after the
commit (`IPendingDispatch`) so a refused request enqueues nothing, and which **returns false when the
outbox already holds the key**: two acts within one second — a wind-down request and the deactivation
right behind it, a double-clicked re-run — ask for one run, and the unique `(queue, key)` index is the
concurrent backstop.

*Why a queue and not the request.* A wind-down with a hundred paid bookings after the date is a hundred
Stripe refund calls; `AdminCancelOrder` commits once at the end of the request, and a hundred-refund
transaction on an HTTP request is the shape ADR-0046's C1/C2 exists to forbid. The shipped answer to "an
admin click starts long work" is `SendSitewidePromo`: record one message, return, do the work in a
consumer.

**The sweep.** `CompanyWindDownService.RunAsync(tenantId, ct)` in `Core.AppServices.Services` (the
`DataRetentionBackgroundService` shape: a job service with one public method and private steps), called by
`CompanyWindDownHandler` in `Functions.Core` from a thin `CompanyWindDownFunction`
(`[QueueTrigger("company-wind-down")]`) with a `CompanyWindDownPoisonFunction` over `PoisonHandlerBase`.
The consumer sets `SetTenantOverride(envelope.TenantId)` (ADR-0061 D10 — the tenant comes from the
envelope, every read below is filtered to the company, every commit is inside a step), reads the `Tenant`
row, **returns as a permanent no-op when the company does not exist, has no `WindDownFrom`, or `IsFrozen`**
(C20 — a frozen company's books are sealed; there is nothing for the sweep to do and a stale memo must
never be given the chance; the handler logs and acks), stamps `StartWindDownRun(now)` and commits (the row
is tenantless — it is the one write that crosses no guard), then runs **six steps in this order**, each
idempotent by construction, each committing per unit of work so a crash resumes past what is done:

1. **Notices first — every customer and every cleaner of the company, by e-mail (C12).** They are enqueues
   only, so they cost seconds and reach the inbox *before* the cancellation pushes of step 2 — nobody is
   told "your booking is cancelled" by a push they cannot yet explain. Two e-mail kinds,
   `EmailType.CompanyWindDownCustomer = 8` and `CompanyWindDownCleaner = 9`, two templates
   `email-templates/company-wind-down-customer.html` and `company-wind-down-cleaner.html`, two
   `IEmailService` methods, in-code default copy in the **five locales** layered under
   `EmailTemplateTranslation` rows exactly as the promo e-mail does. The copy names the company by **the
   `CompanyInfo.LegalName` of each of its markets** (one in the tree; a two-market company lists two — the
   name the customer's receipt prints, not `Tenant.Name`, which the seed already spells differently) and
   the date; for the customer: bookings on or after the date are cancelled and refunded in full, Plus ends
   at the period end, unspent credit expires when the company closes, the account and its history stay,
   how to export or delete it; for the cleaner: the last day of work, that the last pay period is invoiced
   and paid as usual, that sign-in to the partner app ends at the close, and that the customer app is where
   to export or erase. The sweep pages the company's `Users` by keyset on `Id` (200 a page) with the
   predicate **`IsActive && IsEmailConfirmed && not anonymised`** (the `@anonymized.local` tombstone) —
   customers = `Profile == Customer`; cleaners = `Profile == Employee` with an `Employee` row whose
   `ContractStatus == Approved` (C11: no notice to a tombstone, an unconfirmed sign-up or a rejected
   applicant). No opt-in filter (a service notice under the contract, not marketing — `p.Promo` is exactly
   what this must *not* read). Each page is sent **straight to the `send-email` queue through
   `IQueueClient`** (the promo shape), never through the outbox — `SendEmailMessage` carries the address,
   and ten thousand addresses do not belong in `OutboxMessages`; the `CampaignProgress` cursor
   `wind-down-notice:{tenantId}:{WindDownRequestedOn:yyyyMMddHHmmss}` is committed per page so a re-run
   skips the pages it finished. Each message carries `Code = WindDownRequestedOn ("yyyyMMddHHmmss")`, so
   the shipped idempotency key `email:{type}:{userId}:{hash(code)}` makes a re-run of the *same* request
   send nothing twice, while a **later** wind-down after a reactivation — even on the same date — carries a
   new instant and is announced again (C23). `SendEmailHandler`'s `switch` gained two arms. SendGrid is
   the rate-limited hop; `batchSize: 1` on `send-email` is the throttle, as for every other e-mail.

2. **Orders — cancel, refund, and re-drive.** The cut-off instant per market is `WindDownFrom` at 00:00 in
   that market's `CountryConfiguration.TimeZoneId` (UTC when null or unknown; `WindDownCutoff.Utc`, the
   one place the settlement reader and the sweep both compute it) — once per country of the company.
   Select `Orders` with `CurrentStatus in {New, Confirmed, OnTheWay}` (**InProgress is left** —
   `CancellationAssessor.BlockedReason` refuses it and a clean under way finishes; if its cleaner can no
   longer sign in, the admin's status override closes it), `CleaningDateTime >= cutoff(the address's
   country)`, and **the money term adapted to a real booking: `PaymentStatus == Paid || PaymentType ==
   Cash`** — an unpaid card order is an abandoned checkout that `CleanupStalePendingOrders` owns and whose
   Session may still settle (C13); a cash order, recurring or not, is a real booking here. **When the
   company is deactivated the date floor is lifted**: every open order is in scope, because no cleaner can
   sign in to serve any of them — including an `OnTheWay` order whose cleaner is driving; there is no trip
   compensation rule in `business-rules.md`, and none is added (C23, accepted and named). For each, in
   `CleaningDateTime` order: **re-read `CurrentStatus` with a fresh untracked query immediately before the
   cancel** (C9 — the window for a racing run shrinks to the Stripe call), then **one call** to the
   cancellation service extracted from `AdminCancelOrder.Handler` (next paragraph) with
   `CancelledBy.System`, reason `OrderCancellationReasons.CompanyWindDown =
   "order.cancelled.company_wind_down"` (a fourth key — the customer sees why),
   `RefundReason.ServiceNotRendered` (the platform's fault, so the platform absorbs the fee), actor
   `WindDownRequestedBy`; then `CommitAsync` — **per order**, so a refund that succeeded is recorded before
   the next Stripe call (ADR-0046 C1 applied to money going out). The refund key the sweep writes is
   **`refund:{OrderId}:admin`** (the `ServiceNotRendered` segment of `BuildRefundKey`; not the admin
   cancel's `:cancel`, C3), and the test contract asserts that literal. A refund that fails is logged at
   *Error* and the order stays cancelled (`CancelUnfilledOrders`' reasoning holds: *"not a reason to leave
   the customer holding a booking nobody is coming to"*).

   **The second selection — the re-drive (C2).** A cancelled order is out of the first selection forever,
   and a `Pending` refund is re-driven only by a second `IssueRefundAsync` on the same key, so every run
   **also** selects the company's orders `Cancelled` with `CancelledBy == System`, `CancellationReason ==
   CompanyWindDown`, `PaymentType == Card`, `PaymentStatus == Paid` (not yet `Refunded`/`PartiallyRefunded`),
   **minus the orders this very run cancelled** — a refusal seconds old is not answered by asking again
   seconds later; the next run does — and calls the cancellation service's **refund leg alone** with the
   same `RefundRequest(orderId, TotalPrice, ServiceNotRendered, WindDownRequestedBy)` — the key resolves to
   the `Pending` row, the live ceiling re-clamps it, Stripe replays once. A card-paid order with no Stripe
   charge surface is logged at Error as one an admin must settle by hand. A run on a company whose refunds
   all succeeded finds the second selection empty. **The repeat suppressor is the cancel itself for the
   cancel, and the refund key for the money.** The archive refuses while any `Refund` of the company is
   `Pending` (D3), so an unsettled customer can never be sealed over.

   *The extraction.* `AdminCancelOrder.Handler`'s body became
   `IPlatformOrderCancellation.CancelAsync(order, actorId, cancelledBy, reasonKey, refundReason, ct)` plus a
   `RefundAsync(order, actorId, refundReason, ct)` leg the re-drive calls alone, in
   `Core.AppServices.Services`, and the handler became a status-gated caller of `CancelAsync` with
   `CancelledBy.Admin` and **`RefundReason.CustomerCancellation` — its key stays `:cancel`, byte-identical**
   (the `refundReason` parameter is why the extraction does not change the admin path). **The same
   collaborators, moved, not re-implemented**; the money path is not duplicated. Two callers (the admin
   command, the sweep) is the CLAUDE.md §4 bar for extracting. `CancelUnfilledOrders` is **not** moved onto
   it: it grants the apology credit and raises a different notification; a third arm on the service to
   serve one caller is what §4 forbids. The refactor is a money path and got the adversarial review the
   memory file asks for.

3. **Recurring templates.** Every `RecurringBookingTemplate` of the company with `IsActive` → `Pause()`;
   one commit for the batch. Already-paused templates are not selected. Occurrences already materialised
   are orders and were handled in step 2.

4. **Plus — every membership the company's customers hold, any currency (O-2 widened, C7).** Every
   `UserMembership` of the company (`TenantId` = the company — the account's operator, ADR-0061 D5) with
   `Status == Active` and no cancellation requested (`CancelledAt == null`):
   `CancelSubscriptionAtPeriodEndAsync` then `MarkCancellationRequested()`; commit per membership; a
   Stripe failure is logged and the row stays selected for the next run. *Why wide.* Under Q-TENANCY-01
   the customer can book nowhere else today, so a Plus in any currency is a benefit they pay for and cannot
   use; and a company that is sealed while its customers' subscriptions still renew is a company with live
   revenue after its books closed — the contradiction C7 named. The narrow default was the orchestrator's;
   the wide one is the panel's, and **O-2 asks the owner to confirm it**. The membership lifecycle job
   sends its own ending push as it does for any cancel-at-period-end; the notice (step 1) said Plus ends at
   the period end. The period-end status flip arrives by webhook as a write on the **person's** row
   (`UserMembership` stays on the account surface, D3), so it lands whether or not the company is frozen
   by then; the archive nevertheless **waits for every membership to end** (D3 — a sealed company has no
   customer still paying it).

5. **Credit — only once the company is deactivated (C6).** Every `CreditAccount` of the company with
   `Balance > 0` is discharged through the shipped admin-discharge statement —
   `ICreditAccountRepository.TryDebitAsync` for the full balance with `CreditTransactionReason.Expired`,
   the idempotency key `wind-down-credit:{accountId}:{runStartedOn:yyyyMMddHHmmss}` and the note
   `"company wind-down"` — one conditional `UPDATE` plus its ledger row per account. *Why.* Credit is
   non-cash and **expires rather than pays out** by the owner's ruling of 2026-09-05; once the door is
   closed the balance is spendable nowhere (Q-TENANCY-01), and a positive liability on sealed books is a
   book entry that lies. *Why not before deactivation.* Until the door closes the customer can still spend
   it on a booking before the date; the notice (step 1) says unspent credit expires with the closing. *Why
   not transfer it to the holding.* Cross-market spend is Batch 3; the day it lands, a discharge-with-note
   is exactly the row Batch 3 can read back to re-grant if the owner wants that. `CreditAccount` therefore
   is **books** (frozen by the guard, D3); the archive refuses while any balance is positive. **Escalated
   as O-5 with the discharge as the default.** `LoyaltyAccount` (points, a programme, no money) stays the
   person's and is left alone.

6. **The last pay period — only once the company is deactivated, has no open order, and no completed
   order awaiting its pay calculation (C5).** The company's Open period(s) are closed
   (`PayPeriod.Close("System", "Company wind-down")`) and the cleaners invoiced and e-mailed by the
   **existing** per-period body of `PayPeriodBackgroundService`, lifted into a public
   `ClosePeriodAsync(period, closeNote, openNext: false, ct)` that the rollover also calls with `openNext:
   true` — **no new invoicing code**; the reference→row→PDF→upload durability is reused verbatim. While an
   `OfferableStatuses` order remains, or a `Completed` order still has `EmployeePayCalculated == false`
   (its pay row is seconds away on the `calculate-order-pay` queue and a period closed before it would
   invoice the cleaner short), the step is skipped and logged. The step does **not** re-run invoicing on an
   already-`Closed` period: the body e-mails every active cleaner again, so a period with pay rows an
   allocation failure left uninvoiced is a fact the page shows (`UninvoicedPayRows`) and the admin settles
   with the pay-period tools, and the archive refuses until it is zero. The nightly rollover gained one
   predicate: **it opens no new period for a deactivated company** (it already groups per tenant; one
   `Tenant` read per group) — and, from `e1a1beb3`, it **excludes the period it is closing from the
   successor check** rather than committing the close early (ADR-0046 declines a commit before the
   e-mails). `EnsureOpenPeriodAsync` is left as is: an `InProgress` order completing after deactivation
   opens a period that the next wind-down run closes — the sweep converges.

**The end of a run.** `tenant.RecordWindDownRun(now)` (clears `WindDownRunStartedOn`); a summary log at
*Error* level with the counts (notices enqueued, cancelled, refunded, refunds re-driven, refund failures,
templates paused, memberships cancelled, credit discharged, periods closed or the step skipped and why) —
*Error* because the Functions host's Sentry integration drops *Warning* to a breadcrumb. No counts on the
row: the **live facts** are the report (D4's query), and they are what the archive validator checks.

**The bound, redelivery, overlap (C9, C10).** The plan is Dedicated and `functionTimeout` defaults to
**30 minutes**; at roughly one second per order (one Stripe call, one commit) a delivery handles ~1,800
orders, and `maxDequeueCount 5` gives ~9,000 before the message poisons — every attempt makes durable
progress, because every order commits alone. A company with more open future bookings than that is not a
cleaning company this platform has seen; **poison-then-"Run wind-down again" is the designed recovery**,
and a continuation-message shape is one ticket on top of this ADR if a company ever needs it. A run that
dies mid-way is redelivered up to five times and resumes past everything committed; after five it is
poisoned into a `DeadLetter` row with an alert. **Re-run:** `DeactivateCompany` re-enqueues the sweep, and
for a company already deactivated the admin page offers **"Run wind-down again"** — the same
`WindDownCompany` endpoint with no date, which the validator admits only when `WindDownFrom` is already set
and no run is in flight (the one-hour escape for a run that died). `DeactivateCompany`'s enqueue is **not**
gated on a running sweep — deactivation must never wait — so two runs *can* overlap on scale-out. What
overlap produces, stated: the fresh status re-read before each cancel bounds the race to the Stripe call;
the refund collapses on its key; a template pause, a membership cancel and a credit discharge are each
idempotent on state; the e-mail key collapses; the residual is **at worst a duplicate `Cancelled` history
row and a duplicate cleaner notification on one order, never a duplicate refund** — a dirty trail line,
named here rather than claimed away.

**What the wind-down is not.** It does not delist markets or refuse sign-in (D1 does, at deactivation). It
does not touch loyalty, does not deactivate cleaners' accounts, does not e-mail guests (an order with no
account is refunded and gets the standard cancellation push path — the cancellation e-mail is the notice),
and does not run on a timer: it runs when an admin asks, and again when the door closes.

### D3 — Archive: settle, freeze at the request, bundle the books, guard the commit

**The request.**

```
ArchiveCompany.Command() : ICommand<Response(State, ArchiveRequestedOn)>   [AuditAction("company.archive", ResourceType = "Tenant")]  Policy.CanArchiveCompany
```

Validator — **live facts, not stamps**, read through one `ICompanySettlementReader.ReadAsync(ct)` (in
`Core.Domain.Tenancy`, implemented by `Infra.Database.Repositories.CompanySettlementReader` — every count a
filtered read) that the lifecycle query (D4) shares, refused in this order with one key each, after
`tenant.not_found`:

| Fact (the reader's member) | Refusal | Why it is a precondition |
|---|---|---|
| not `IsDeactivated` | `company.not_deactivated` | the door must be closed |
| not `IsWindDownRequested` | `company.wind_down_not_requested` | the people must have been told |
| `OpenOrders` > 0 (`OrderAvailability.OfferableStatuses`: New, Confirmed, OnTheWay, InProgress) | `company.has_open_orders` | work still owed |
| `OrdersAwaitingPay` > 0 (`Completed && !EmployeePayCalculated`) | `company.has_orders_awaiting_pay` | a pay row still to be written (C5) |
| `OrdersAwaitingReceipt` > 0 (paid, or cash, **not cancelled**, and no receipt row — the reconciliation predicate) | `company.has_orders_awaiting_receipt` | a fiscal document still to be issued (C5) |
| `ReceiptsAwaitingFiscalRegistration` > 0 (`FiscalNextRetryAt != null`) | `company.has_receipts_awaiting_fiscal_registration` | a legal-obligation write still due (C5) — a precondition, not a third gate caller |
| `PendingRefunds` > 0 (`Refunds.Status == Pending`, any purpose) | `company.has_pending_refunds` | money still owed to a customer (C2) |
| `ActiveMemberships` > 0 (`Status == Active`, cancellation requested or not) | `company.has_active_memberships` | a customer still paying the company (C7) |
| `CreditBalances` > 0 (accounts with `Balance > 0`) | `company.has_credit_balances` | a liability on the books (C6) |
| `OpenPayPeriods` > 0 | `company.has_open_pay_period` | pay not yet closed |
| `UnpaidInvoices` > 0 (a `Closed` period holding an invoice not `Paid`/`Cancelled`) | `company.has_unpaid_invoices` | a cleaner not yet paid |
| `UninvoicedPayRows` > 0 (`OrderEmployeePay.EmployeeInvoiceId == null` in a `Closed` period) | `company.has_uninvoiced_pay` | a cleaner never invoiced (C5) |
| `OpenDisputes` > 0 (`Pending`, `UnderReview`, `WaitingForResponse`, `Escalated`) | `company.has_open_disputes` | a claim still open |
| `ChargebackHorizonEndsOn` in the future | `company.within_chargeback_horizon` | a bank can still move money (C4) |
| `IsFrozen` and not `IsArchived` | *(admitted — the re-run)* | |
| `IsArchived` | `company.archived` | |

**The chargeback horizon.** Card networks let a cardholder dispute a charge for 120 days and, on some
reason codes, longer; a chargeback on a sealed company is a books event the freeze would refuse. So the
archive waits: `ChargebackHorizonEndsOn` = the latest `CleaningDateTime` among the company's card-paid
orders (`PaymentType == Card && PaymentStatus in {Paid, PartiallyRefunded, Refunded}` — the service date is
after the charge, which errs on the safe side) plus **`lifecycle.chargeback_horizon_days`**, a
`TenantSettingCatalog` key in a new `lifecycle` category, **default 180, min 0 (no horizon), max 730**,
rendered and editable on the Company settings page T-0759 shipped with no frontend work. Null when the
company never took a card payment. The page shows the date the archive becomes admissible.

Handler: `tenant.RequestArchive(adminId, now)` — **this is the freeze** — and one outbox message on
`company-archive`, key `archive:{tenantId}:{now:yyyyMMddHHmmss}` (deduplicated on the outbox like the
wind-down's), carrying the row's `ArchiveRequestedOn`. A re-run on a frozen company stamps nothing a second
time — the folder is named by the first request — and enqueues a new message.

*Why the freeze is at the request and not at the bundle.* The bundle must be a snapshot; a write landing
while it is built makes the bundle and the books disagree by one row nobody will find. Freezing first
turns the bundle into a pure function of frozen input, which is also what makes a failed build
**restartable by re-running the same request** rather than resumable with a cursor.

**The bundle.** `CompanyArchiveService.RunAsync(tenantId, requestedOn, ct)` in `Core.AppServices.Services`,
consumed by `CompanyArchiveHandler` / `CompanyArchiveFunction` / `CompanyArchivePoisonFunction`, under
`SetTenantOverride(tenantId)`. It no-ops (logged, acked) when the company is missing, already archived,
**not frozen**, or when the message's request instant names a folder the row's freeze does not. Container
**`company-archives`** (`Constants.BlobContainers.CompanyArchives`, the eighth name, mirrored in
`storage.bicep`; created on first write like every container). Layout, container-relative:

  manifest.json                     // written LAST: tenant id + name, the ADR id, the schema version (the Initial id, ISchemaVersionReader),

**What a bundle row may carry — the rule, and the guard that enforces it.** *A bundle row carries what the
platform's own retention regime would leave on the live row after every window has run, plus the ids the
books need to cross-reference each other; identity lives in the receipt and invoice PDFs, which are the
lawful ten-year documents and are in the bundle already.* Concretely: `orders.jsonl` carries **none of the
members `Order.AnonymizeCustomerData()` assigns** (`CustomerName`, `CustomerEmail`, `CustomerPhone`,
`UserId`, the instructions and notes) and no street — the country and city of service, the prices, VAT,
currency, payment type and status, the cash collection, the discounts, the cancellation facts, the receipt
number; an order the sweep already anonymised is exported as it is. The rows are projected into `record`
types in `Features/CompanyLifecycle/Archive/CompanyArchiveRecords.cs` (one per file), and a build-time
guard (`CompanyArchiveRecordGuardTests`, the `CustomerAuditPayloadGuard` shape) walks them and **fails on
a member whose name contains, case-insensitively, any of:** `Email, Phone, Passport, Nationality,
EmergencyContact, AccessInstructions, SpecialInstructions, Notes, Description, ResolutionNotes, Secret,
Password, Ip, Device, Token, Message, Evidence, Review, CustomerName, FirstName, LastName, HolderName,
Street, HouseNumber, PostalCode, BirthDate, Iban, AccountNumber, BankCode, Swift` (C14, extended on
provenance with the bank fields; *contains*, not *equals*, since `44d30a28`). **Out of the bundle,
deliberately:** `Users` (identity lives on under the account), `UserConsents` (a three-year window after
withdrawal — a sealed copy would outlive it), `CustomerActionAudits` (three years per row),
**`EmployeePayoutDetails` (a bank account — the invoice PDF prints the payment reference, which is all the
books need)**, `UserNotifications`, `Devices`, `RefreshTokens`, `OrderNotes/Issues/Photos/Reviews`, dispute
messages and evidence, `RecurringBookingTemplates` (no money), loyalty (points, a programme),
`UserMemberships` (the money is in Stripe's ledger on the holding's account; the row is the person's),
`SavedAddresses`, `Carts`. The bundle is the **books**; the personal-data estate stays in the database
under the retention and erasure regime that keeps running (below). This departs from the orchestrator's
default (*"consents, audit rows"*) on the retention argument, and is overrulable (O-3). **One member the
review left for a ruling:** `credit-accounts.jsonl` and `promo-code-redemptions.jsonl` carry the `UserId`
that ties a ledger and a redemption to a person — an id, not a name, but an id erasure keeps; whether it
belongs in a ten-year copy is filed as an open question.

*Why JSON Lines, not CSV or one JSON document.* A file per table with one object per line streams through
`CreateFileForWritingAsync` without holding a company's ten years of orders in memory, is `jq`- and
Excel-importable, and a partial file from a died run is overwritten whole on the re-run. CSV loses nested
money (`OrderEmployeePays` per order, lines per dispute) and types; one document cannot stream. Tenantless
children (`OrderExtra`, `DisputeLine`, `CreditTransaction`) are reached through their parent's filtered
query and nested or written beside it.

**The end of a build, and the seal's anchor (C15).** `manifest.json` last (its presence *is* completion),
**uploaded if absent** — two builds of one frozen company can overlap (a redelivery beside a *Build
again*); their files are the same bytes but their manifests differ by the build instant, so the first
manifest to land is the seal and a build that finds one there stamps the row with **that** one's hash,
never its own — then `tenant.MarkArchived(sha256(manifest.json), now)` and commit; the row is tenantless,
so the guard below does not see it. The hash on the row is the one fact about the bundle that lives outside
the container that holds it, and the lifecycle DTO shows it, so an operator with the bundle in hand can
check it against the database. A **time-based immutability policy** on `company-archives` would make the
seal a storage fact rather than a hash to compare; it is irreversible for its term and is **escalated as
O-6** — the ticket did not set it. Redelivery of a finished archive is a no-op (the `Tenant` row says
`IsArchived`). Redelivery of an unfinished one rebuilds into the same folder (the stamp is the request
instant, not the build instant) — every file is rewritten from the same frozen input, so the outcome is
the same bundle, hash for hash. After five failures the message poisons and the admin page offers **"Build
archive again"** (the same endpoint; the validator admits it when frozen and not archived; a new message).
Retrieval of the bundle is an operations step in the storage account until T-0748 gives a role something
to read it with; the admin page shows `ArchivedOn` and the hash and says so. No blob path is on any DTO
(S4).

**The write guard — central, at `CommitAsync`.** After the stamp loop, before `SaveChangesAsync`:

```
touched = ArchivedCompanyWriteGuard.TouchedBooksTenantIds(ChangeTracker)
            // every Added/Modified/Deleted ITenantEntity with a TenantId whose CLR type is NOT on the account surface
if touched is empty                         → save
if archiveWriteGate?.IsOpen                 → save            // the law's writes (retention, erasure) — below
frozen = Tenants.AsNoTracking().Where(t => unknown.Contains(t.Id)).Select(t => new { t.Id, Frozen = t.ArchiveRequestedOn != null })
                                            // memoised per context instance — one Tenants read per company per scope
if any touched id is frozen                 → throw new CompanyArchivedException(thatId)
```

- **`CompanyArchivedException : Exception`** lives in `Core.Domain.Tenancy` (so `Infra.Database` can throw
  it and `Config` can map it) and carries the tenant id. On the request path
  `RequestValidationExceptionFilterAttribute` gained a second arm: **409 Conflict** with the same
  ProblemDetails body the 400 arm builds, one error `TenantId → tenant.archived`; the customer audit
  failure row is written as for any refused act.
- **The Stripe webhook is acknowledged, never refused (C4).** `ArchivedCompanyWebhookAcknowledgeFilterAttribute`
  in `Cleansia.Config/Filters`, applied to the three `PaymentController.Webhook` actions, buffers the
  body, catches `CompanyArchivedException` at the commit, hands it to `ArchivedCompanyDeadLetter` — a
  **`DeadLetter` row from a fresh scope** (the `OutOfBandAuditFailureSink` shape: `IServiceScopeFactory` →
  `SetTenantOverride(frozen id)` → `IDeadLetterStore.RecordAsync("stripe-webhook", body,
  "tenant.archived:{tenantId}")` → commit; a `DeadLetter` is an envelope and passes the guard), logs at
  *Error* (the alert), and answers **200** — Stripe must always see 2xx, or it retries for days and flags
  an endpoint every company shares. The horizon precondition above makes this the rare case; the
  dead-letter row is the operations fact when it happens, and the bundle stays a true snapshot of the
  freeze.
- **On a queue consumer it is permanent.** The two books-writing consumers that can legitimately arrive
  late — `calculate-order-pay`, `generate-receipt` — classify it as business-rejected through the same
  `ArchivedCompanyDeadLetter` (log, `DeadLetter` row under the consumer's queue name, ack) to skip the
  retries; a consumer that does not classify it reaches **the same `DeadLetter` row through its poison
  twin after five deliveries**. Either way it is an operations fact, not a lost message; the preconditions
  above make both rare.
- **The account surface** — the `ITenantEntity` types the guard lets through even when the company is
  frozen, because they are the *person's*, not the *company's*: `User`, `RefreshToken`, `Device`,
  `LiveActivityToken`, `Cart`, `SavedAddress`, `UserConsent`, `UserNotificationPreferences`,
  `UserNotification`, `GdprRequest`, `UserStripeCustomer`, `UserMembership`, `MembershipBenefitUsage`,
  `LoyaltyAccount`, `LoyaltyTransaction`, `ReferralCode`, `Referral`, and the three audit tables
  `AdminActionAudit`, `CustomerActionAudit`, `EmployeeActionAudit` (the pipeline's happy-path audit row
  commits beside the request's rows and must pass; the failure sink writes with `SaveChangesAsync` from a
  fresh context and never meets the guard), plus the two envelopes `OutboxMessage`, `DeadLetter`.
  **`CreditAccount` is books** (C6). **Everything else is books and is guarded by default** — a new stamped
  table fails closed until it is sorted, and `ArchivedCompanyWriteGuardRosterTests` (roster-free over
  `ctx.Model`, the `TenantIdRequiredModelTests` shape) fails on an `ITenantEntity` type that is in neither
  the account surface nor the test's own books roster (`Address`, `CompanyInfo`, `CreditAccount`,
  `Dispute`, `DocumentDeletionRequest`, `Employee`, `EmployeeDocument`, `EmployeeInvoice`,
  `EmployeePayConfig`, `EmployeePayoutDetails`, `FiscalCounter`, `Order`, `OrderEmployeePay`, `OrderIssue`,
  `OrderNote`, `OrderPhoto`, `OrderReceipt`, `OrderReview`, `OrderStatusTrack`, `PayPeriod`,
  `PayoutReferenceCounter`, `PromoCode`, `PromoCodeRedemption`, `RecurringBookingTemplate`, `Refund`,
  `TenantConfiguration`), so sorting is a deliberate act with a one-line reason.
- **Tenantless children — the residual, named (C16).** **Nine** types carry no tenant and a foreign key
  into a books type, so a commit that adds only such a row passes the guard: the four the draft named —
  `DisputeLine`, `CreditTransaction`, `OrderReviewLine`, `OrderExtra` — and the five the model also holds
  — `DisputeMessage`, `DisputeEvidence`, `OrderEmployee`, `OrderService`, `OrderPackage`. Why that is
  bounded: each is written beside a parent write the guard sees (a line with its dispute, an extra or a
  service with its order, a review line with its review) or through a path the freeze has already closed
  (a credit movement needs a booking, a grant, or a discharge — the first two are refused upstream, the
  third finds every balance at zero; a dispute message needs an open dispute; a seat needs an open
  order). The roster test **enumerates the nine by name**, fails on a tenth, and fails when a named one
  leaves the model; the guard does **not** walk foreign keys to a parent's tenant (a query per child type
  on every commit for a residual this narrow is the machinery §4 forbids).
- **The gate for the law's writes.** `IArchiveWriteGate` (scoped; `bool IsOpen`; `IDisposable
  OpenForLegalObligation(string reason)`) in `Core.Domain.Tenancy`, registered in `Cleansia.Config`,
  injected into the context beside `ITenantProvider` (null at design time, like the provider). **Two
  callers, both today:** `DataRetentionBackgroundService` wraps the per-company loop
  (`OpenForLegalObligation("data retention")`) — the order-PII sweep modifies books rows and the
  dispute-text sweep too, and both are the company's GDPR obligations, which do not end with its trading;
  and `GdprDeletionService` wraps the walk (`OpenForLegalObligation("erasure")`) — erasure pseudonymises
  orders and disputes of the archived company, and Art. 17 does not care that the books are frozen.
  Fiscal retry is **not** a third caller: the archive waits for `FiscalNextRetryAt` to be null (above).
  **Enforced by a build-time test, not a grep (C22):** `LegalObligationGateCallSiteTests` reads the
  `Core.AppServices` sources and asserts that exactly two files contain `OpenForLegalObligation(` and
  names them.
- **Cost.** One `Tenants` read per commit that touches a books row, memoised per context instance (one per
  request scope, one per job iteration), on a table with a handful of rows and a PK predicate. Commits
  that touch only the account surface, or nothing stamped, pay nothing.
- **Known bypasses, enumerated.** `ExecuteUpdateAsync`/`ExecuteDeleteAsync` (the retention code sweeps —
  account surface anyway; the login throttle — `Users`), the two counter repositories (allocation only
  happens on a receipt or an invoice, which need an order completion or a pay period, which the
  preconditions exclude), `BulkRevokeIgnoringConcurrencyAsync` (`RefreshTokens` — account surface), the
  credit spend's conditional `UPDATE` (needs a booking — refused upstream). A reviewer re-derives the list
  with `grep -rn "ExecuteUpdateAsync\|ExecuteDeleteAsync\|ExecuteSqlRawAsync\|ON CONFLICT"
  src/Cleansia.Infra.Database src/Cleansia.Core.AppServices` and checks that none names a books table
  reachable after the preconditions.

**What stays readable, and by whom.** The company's **administrators** sign in and read everything they
could before (the filter is untouched; the guard is on writes); every write they attempt answers 409
`tenant.archived`, and the admin app shows the state banner (D4). **Customers** of the company sign in, see
their history, download their receipts (`DownloadOrderReceipt` reads the receipt blob that still exists —
the archive *copies*, it does not move), export (reads only) and erase (the gate). **Cleaners** cannot
sign in to the partner apps (D1), hold the invoice PDFs they were e-mailed, and export or erase on the
customer host. **The public** sees no market. **The bundle** is read by operations in the storage account
until T-0748, and checked against the hash on the row.

**The ten years.** No row of the company is deleted by this ADR, ever — not at deactivation, not at
wind-down, not at archive. The retention sweep keeps blanking PII on its windows (through the gate);
receipts, invoices, orders, refunds, pay rows and the bundle stay. A rule for what happens after ten years
is a future ADR with an accountant in the room (O-4).

### D4 — The order of operations, and what each state permits

**The intended sequence is announce → close → seal**, and it is not alphabetical:

1. **`WindDownCompany(fromDate)`** with the last day of service, weeks out: customers and cleaners are told
   now; anything already booked on or after the date is cancelled and refunded now; templates pause; every
   Plus ends at period end; **bookings before the date still happen and cleaners still work them, and
   credit can still be spent on them.**
2. **`DeactivateCompany`** on the date (or earlier if the owner says so — and only once the company no
   longer holds the default market): the markets vanish from every predicate at once, cleaners are out,
   and the wind-down sweep runs again with the floor lifted — anything booked after the announcement is
   cancelled and refunded, unspent credit is discharged, and, once no order is open and no pay is pending,
   the last pay period closes and the cleaners are invoiced.
3. **`ArchiveCompany`** once the live facts are clean and the chargeback horizon has passed (the page shows
   every count and the date): the books freeze at the click, the bundle is built, the hash lands on the
   row.

An emergency closure runs 2 then 1 (`WindDownCompany(today)`) then 3, and every guarantee holds — the sweep
does not care which came first. Reactivation reopens from **Deactivated** only.

| State (predicates) | Markets listed / serviced | Customer sign-in + history + GDPR | Cleaner sign-in (partner) | Admin sign-in (admin, and partner audiences) | New bookings / Plus / registrations in its markets | Open orders served | Books writable | Retention + erasure run | Commands admitted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Operating** (none) | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | Deactivate (unless it holds the default market), WindDown(date) |
| **Winding down** (`IsWindDownRequested`, active) | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes — and anything on/after the date is cancelled on the next run | yes, before the date | yes | yes | Deactivate, WindDown (run again) |
| **Deactivated** (`IsDeactivated`) | **no** | yes | **no** (`auth.company_deactivated`) | yes | **no** (`country.not_serviced`) | no — the sweep cancels them; `InProgress` finishes | yes | yes | Reactivate, WindDown(date) if none / (run again) if set, Archive when settled and past the horizon |
| **Frozen** (`IsFrozen`, not `IsArchived`) | no | yes | no | yes, **read-only** (409 on write) | no | — | **no** (`tenant.archived`) | yes, through the gate | Archive (build again) |
| **Archived** (`IsArchived`) | no | yes | no | yes, read-only | no | — | no | yes, through the gate | none |

**The read.** `GetCompanyLifecycle.Query : IQuery<CompanyLifecycleDto>` under `Policy.CanViewCompanyLifecycle`

**The page (T-0764).** `libs/cleansia-admin-features/company-lifecycle`, route `/company-lifecycle`

a run is in progress or a fact is unsettled. The facade holds the DTO, the act in flight and the dialog in
signals, runs one act at a time and re-reads after every act whether admitted or refused, so the shared
interceptor's sentence lands on a page that agrees with the server.

### D5 — What stays out, by name

- **No holding role, no cross-company read or write.** Every command acts on the ambient tenant; there is
  no way to deactivate, wind down or archive a company one is not an administrator of. T-0748 is where a
  holding role arrives; this ADR forecloses nothing there and builds none of it.
- **No per-company Stripe.** Refunds and subscription cancels go through the one account
  (`StripeClientFactory.CreateClient()`, ADR-0061 §Rulings).
- **No data deletion.** Not a row, not a blob. Not after archive, not after ten years — that is a later ADR.
- **No un-archive.** `Reactivate` is admitted from `Deactivated` only. A frozen company whose bundle failed
  is re-run, not unfrozen; unfreezing is a deliberate SQL step in `sql-scripts/` with the owner's hand on
  it, because a bundle that exists and books that changed after it is the one state this ADR is written to
  make impossible.
- **No registry writes.** Creating a second company is still ADR-0061 D12's seed step; nothing here
  creates, renames or deletes a `Tenants` row.
- **No platform shutdown.** The company that holds the default market cannot be deactivated (D1); closing
  the last company is a different decision with a different blast radius.
- **No customer lock-out, no account move.** A customer of a closed company keeps an account that, today,
  can book nowhere (Q-TENANCY-01 refuses cross-market booking until Batch 3). That is the orchestrator's
  default and the honest state of the tree; Batch 3 changes it without touching this ADR.
- **No credit transfer.** Unspent credit is discharged (D2 step 5); moving it to the holding is Batch 3's
  to decide, and the discharge row with its note is what Batch 3 would read.
- **No retention change.** `GetAllIdsAsync` keeps returning deactivated and archived companies; the sweep
  keeps its windows; nothing shortens or lengthens them.
- **No new timer.** Both consumers are queue-triggered by an admin's act.
- **No horizon gate in the booking path.** "Refuse bookings after the last day while still taking bookings
  before it" was considered (Alternatives (f)) and not built: the sweep's re-run at deactivation cancels
  and refunds anything booked in the window, and the notice says so.
- **No storage immutability policy.** Escalated (O-6); the hash on the row is the anchor until then.

## Alternatives considered

- **(a) One switch on `Tenant.IsActive` and nothing else** — the scout's option (a) alone. Rejected by the
  ruling (*"build up to (c)"*), and on its merits: a switch that leaves paid bookings nobody will serve and
  cleaners who cannot sign in to serve them is a defect with a date on it.
- **(b) `SetCountryServiced(false)` per country instead of a company switch.** Rejected: a company serving
  two countries is two switches with nothing tying them, no staff refusal, and the state of the *company*
  lives nowhere — the archive has nothing to check. `Country.IsServiced` stays for what it is.
- **(c) Refuse administrators' sign-in at deactivation too.** Rejected for now (D1, O-1): with no holding
  role, nobody could then settle the company from inside the product. The gate's profile set is one
  constant; the day T-0748 lands the ruling is one line.
- **(d) One `SetCompanyActive(bool)` command** (the `SetRecurringBookingActive` shape). Rejected: two audit
  labels read better in a trail where "reactivated" is the rare, notable event, and the two validators
  refuse different things.
- **(e) Wind down inside the request, order by order, one transaction.** Rejected (D2): a hundred Stripe
  refunds on one HTTP request with one commit at the end is the failure ADR-0046 C1 names; the queue is
  the shipped answer to admin-started long work.
- **(f) A booking horizon gate** (`CleaningDateTime < WindDownFrom` in quote/create/recurring/materialise)
  so nothing after the last day can be booked while bookings before it continue. Considered seriously —
  it is the humane shape — and not built: four predicates in the hottest path of the platform, a new
  customer-facing key in three clients, all paid on every booking forever, to avoid a refund that the
  sweep's re-run at deactivation already issues in full, on a day the notice already announced. If the
  owner wants it, it is one ticket on top of this ADR, not a change to it.
- **(g) A wind-down that cancels only the Plus in the company's currencies** — the orchestrator's default
  and the author's draft. **Reversed by the panel (C7):** under Q-TENANCY-01 the benefit is unusable in
  any currency, and a subscription that keeps renewing into a sealed company is revenue after the books
  closed. The wide rule is D2 step 4; O-2 asks the owner to confirm.
- **(h) Send the notices as push through the promo fan-out.** Rejected: the fan-out is opt-in (`p.Promo`)
  and a service notice is not marketing; e-mail is the record the customer keeps, and the `send-email`
  queue already has the idempotency key that makes a re-run free.
- **(i) A pipeline behaviour instead of the `CommitAsync` guard** (refuse any command whose ambient tenant
  is frozen). Rejected: it sees requests only — jobs, webhooks and consumers call `CommitAsync` directly
  and would write into frozen books; and it would need its own answer to "which tenant" that the commit
  already has on every row. The commit is the one seam every write crosses; the guard goes there.
- **(j) A guard keyed on a "books" roster with everything else passing** (the inverse of D3's
  account-surface list). Rejected on failure direction: a new stamped table would pass by default. The
  account surface is the closed set; books is what is left.
- **(k) No legal-obligation gate — exempt `Modified`/`Deleted` and guard `Added` only.** Rejected: an
  admin's edit of a receipt row is `Modified`, a late review is `Added`; state alone does not separate the
  law's writes from a business write. The gate is two callers and one scoped flag.
- **(l) Freeze at `ArchivedOn` (after the bundle), not at the request.** Rejected (D3): a write during the
  build makes the bundle lie by one row; and a cursor-resumable build is more machinery than a
  restartable one over frozen input.
- **(m) Bundle everything the company's tenant filter can see, users and consents included.** The
  orchestrator's default; departed from (D3) on retention: a sealed copy of a consent row or a customer
  audit row outlives the three-year window the sweep enforces on the live row, and a copy of `Users`
  outlives every erasure. The bundle is the books; the estate stays where the law can reach it. The same
  argument, applied by the challenger to the order rows (C14), is why `orders.jsonl` is the anonymised
  projection.
- **(n) A `TenantLifecycleEvent` table for the record.** Rejected (D1): the admin audit trail with four
  labels and typed evidence is that table, already written by the pipeline.
- **(o) Extract the sweep's cancellation from `CancelUnfilledOrders` instead of `AdminCancelOrder`.**
  Rejected: the sweep grants an apology credit and raises a different push; the admin cancel is the shape
  the wind-down is (platform-initiated, full refund, cleaners told, loyalty revoked, waiver released). One
  extraction, two callers.
- **(p) Move `WindDownFrom` earlier by a second request.** Rejected (D2): a cancelled order cannot be
  un-cancelled, so only one direction is safe, and a rule that says which is a rule nobody will remember;
  the date is set once and the admin cancels stragglers by hand.
- **(q) The operator term in `GetMarkets.ResolveMarketAsync` with hand-moved writers** — the author's
  draft: an `OperatorTenant` include, a split refusal in `CreateOrder`, two membership writers moved onto
  the resolver, a `company.deactivated` key for template resume. **Reversed by the panel (C8):** it left
  thirteen of fifteen `IsServiced` readers inconsistent — the wizard's picker offering the closed market, a
  signed-in quote succeeding and the booking refusing afterwards — and wrote that inconsistency into an
  acceptance criterion. The repository predicate (D1) is fewer edits and every reader inherits; the two
  paths that read nothing (the recurring booking, the saved address) were found in review and given a
  rule each.
- **(r) Let a chargeback through the guard as a legal-obligation write.** Rejected (C4): the money moved
  at the bank, but recording it after the seal is exactly the "books changed after the bundle" state D3
  exists to forbid; the horizon precondition makes the case rare, and a dead-letter row with an alert is
  the honest record when it happens.
- **(s) Cancel every Plus immediately with a prorated refund instead of at period end.** Rejected: a new
  Stripe client member and a proration rule for a benefit the customer keeps until the period ends
  anyway; the archive waits for the period, and the chargeback horizon usually waits longer.
- **(t) The guard walks foreign keys to find a tenantless child's company.** Rejected (C16): one query per
  child type on every commit for a residual that is bounded by the parent write and the closed paths; the
  roster test names the nine instead.
- **(u) A continuation-message sweep** (K orders per delivery, the promo fan-out's shape). Not built
  (C10): the bound is ~9,000 orders before poison, every order commits alone, and "Run again" is the
  recovery; one ticket if a company ever needs it.
- **(v) Transfer unspent credit to the holding at wind-down.** Deferred to Batch 3 (C6, O-5): there is no
  holding-level account to move it to, and the discharge row with its note is what a later transfer would
  read.
- **(w) Re-drive a refund the same run refused.** Not done (reviewer #4): the re-drive selection excludes
  the orders the current run cancelled; a refusal seconds old is re-driven by the next run, which is a
  click away.

## Consequences

**Cheaper / safer:**
- Closing a company is three admin clicks in an order the page explains, each reusing a money or people
  path that already has its tests — the refund key, the template pause, the Plus cancel, the credit
  discharge, the invoicing body, the e-mail queue — with no second implementation of any of them.
- "Is this country open for business?" has one answer in one place, and a deactivated company's markets
  vanish from every directory, quote, booking, catalogue, plan and work-country rule at once.
- The archived-company guard closes a class at the root: after the freeze, no writer that carries a
  tenant — request, job, webhook, consumer, or the next one written — can change a frozen company's books
  without saying so through the gate, a new stamped table is guarded until sorted, and the nine tenantless
  children are named.
- The archive cannot seal over a pending refund, an uninvoiced cleaner, an unissued receipt, a live
  subscription, a positive credit balance or a chargeback the network still allows.
- The customer's rights survive the company: sign-in, history, receipts, export and erasure all keep
  working, and retention keeps running, because the person's rows were never the company's books.
- `Tenant.IsActive` got its reader; the CRC card's watch-list sentence retired; ADR-0061 O-3 closed.

**More expensive (accepted):**
- One regenerated `Initial` (`20260915232921` — `Tenants` gained fifteen columns and the `Auditable`
  mapping; both fixtures' raw inserts gained two columns) and the DEV drop at deploy time. The
  `StampedTables = 48` pin is unchanged — `Tenant` is tenantless.

  `storage.bicep` / `queueAlerts.bicep`), two e-mail kinds with five locales of default copy each, and
  templates; one catalogue key.
- A company that holds the default market cannot be deactivated; with one company in the registry, none
  can — until a second exists.
- The archive waits for the chargeback horizon (180 days by default from the last card-paid clean) and for
  the last Plus period to end; a company is Deactivated, not Archived, for months. That is the price of a

- **Keys and locales (CLAUDE.md landmine 3):** admin app — `company.already_deactivated`,
