# ADR-0069 — The customer states how dirty the home is; that one statement raises the price, the booked time and the cleaner pay by the same rate, and the job's pay is split across its seats

> **One decision:** *the customer picks one of three levels — Normal, Increased, Heavy — and the level's
> rate (0, 30 or 60 %) is applied to the whole basket, to the booked minutes and to the cleaner's pay,
> so a dirtier home costs more, is given the time it needs, gets the crew that time implies, and pays the
> cleaners for it; and a job's pay, which a rate describes, is shared across the seats that do it.*

## Context

The business summary of 2026-09-27 (page 4) introduced three dirtiness levels with a +30 % and a +60 %
surcharge and a promise that the cleaner then has *enough time*. The tree had none of it, and three
facts made a surcharge alone dishonest:

- **Duration was fixed per service.** A heavy home was booked for the time of a normal one, and the
  crew (`ceil(minutes / 120)`) and cash eligibility followed that time — so the promise of enough time
  would have been false.
- **Size did not scale time.** An 8-room and a 1-room flat with the same services got the same minutes
  and the same crew; only the price scaled with rooms.
- **Every assigned cleaner was paid the whole job.** Pay was one row per cleaner with no crew-size
  term, so a two-seat job paid its rates twice against one customer price. Lengthening heavy jobs would
  create more two-seat jobs, and each would double the labour cost.

## Decision

### D1 — The level is the customer's statement, stored on the order (decisions 30, 31)

`DirtinessLevel` is an integer wire enum — `Normal = 0`, `Increased = 1`, `Heavy = 2`, append-only.
Normal is zero so a client that omits the field books at Normal: the server default is for old clients
and API callers. **Every shipped booking flow makes the customer choose** — a required step of its own
after the services on the web, Android and iOS, with the three descriptions and the hint to pick the
higher level when unsure. An unknown value is `common.invalid_enum_value`. The order stores the level
(`Orders.DirtinessLevel`) and nothing changes it after booking.

**The descriptions are client strings in five locales**, not a served catalogue; the order stores the
level, not the words the customer read (decision 30 (a)). If the on-site top-up comes to depend on the
exact wording, the text moves to a server catalogue snapshotted on the order (30 (b)).

### D2 — The price: the whole basket, inside the subtotal (decisions 28, 29)

```
dirtiness = round(Σ lines × rate, 2)       # packages, services and extras; BookingPolicy.DirtinessSurchargeFor
raw       = Σ lines + dirtiness            # discounts come off this; the tier floor and the 12 % cap judge it
express   = raw × 0.20 on an express slot  # compounds: Heavy + express = × 1.92
```

It is the base express already used, so refunds needed no change. The quote and the Plus preview price
at the level they are asked about, and the quote echoes it with the surcharge (`dirtinessLevel`,
`dirtinessSurchargeAmount`); the booking re-prices at its level, so the quote is the charge. `OrderFactory` stores the surcharge in
cents (`Orders.DirtinessSurchargeAmount`), computed from the lines it stores, and the order's terms
still add up:
`Σ lines + dirtiness + express − discounts = TotalPrice`. The receipt prints the surcharge as a line of
its own, labelled with the level in the receipt's language, and the fiscal request carries it as a line.

**The rates are constants** — `BookingPolicy.IncreasedDirtinessSurchargeRate = 0.30`,
`HeavyDirtinessSurchargeRate = 0.60` — the same in every market, like express, until a market needs
others (decision 29 (a)). `check-booking-policy-parity.mjs` reads both and pins every copy that states
them: the web mirror constants, the web chip that renders `{{rate}}`, and each Android and iOS level
chip and surcharge line, which must state its own level's rate and no other.

### D3 — The time and the crew follow the home (decisions 32, 39)

```
EstimatedTime     = ceil( Σ (service.EstimatedTime + service.MinutesPerRoom × (rooms + bathrooms))
                          × (1 + rate) )
RequiredEmployees = ceil(EstimatedTime / 120)
```

A packaged service is counted the same way. `Service.MinutesPerRoom` is new, edited on the admin
catalogue's service form, and **seeded 0 on every service** — the real durations are a launch value
the owner supplies (decision 77), so until they are typed in, size scaling is inert and only the level
lengthens a job. `OrderDuration.EstimateMinutes` stays the one definition; its SQL twins sum the
catalogue in the database and scale through the same `OrderDuration.ScaleForDirtiness`. Every reader
asks it: the order, the quote (`estimatedDurationMinutes`, `requiredEmployees`), the Plus preview, the
24 h span cap on all three, the preferred-cleaner picker (`GetMyServingCleaners` now takes rooms,
bathrooms and the level with the slot), recurring cash eligibility, and everything that later reads the
order's stored `EstimatedTime`. A booking with a negative room or bathroom count is refused
(`validation.must_be_positive`), because a negative count would now shorten the job and shrink the crew.

**The crew decides cash, so the level can take cash away.** A 120-minute job is one cleaner; at
Increased it is 156 minutes and at Heavy 192, two cleaners, and a two-cleaner job pays by card. The
quote says so before the customer submits, and every client re-reads cash from it.

### D4 — The pay: split per seat, raised after the clamp (decisions 33, 40)

A rate describes the **job**. `PayCalculatorExtensions.CalculateSeatPay` sums the job's base, extras
and clamp bounds from the rates as before, then gives one seat of `RequiredEmployees` an equal share of
each, in whole cents, with every term's residue on the first seat (the crew member with the lowest
`SeatOrdinal` when the pay is calculated), so a full crew's rows add up to the job. The seat's base +
extras are clamped to its share of the bounds. The dirtiness term is the job's clamped pay × the
level's rate, split the same way and **added after the clamp**, so a maximum cannot swallow it; it is
stored as `OrderEmployeePays.DirtinessPay` and kept outside the clamp when a bonus or deduction
recomputes the total. The board, the job detail, the dashboard and the available-jobs preview quote the
same per-seat figure (`OrderPayEstimator`), and My Pay carries the term (`dirtinessPay`,
`totalDirtinessPay`).

### D5 — A recurring schedule carries its own level (decision 34)

`RecurringBookingTemplate.DirtinessLevel` — Normal when a client sends none, editable on update, an
unknown value refused. The materialiser prices, times, crews and stores every occurrence at it; the cash
check, the list's `requiresPaymentMethodChange` flag and the schedule's audit facts read it. The web,
Android and iOS schedule forms ask for it on a new schedule and keep the stored one on an edit.

### D6 — The catalogue does not charge twice (decision 35)

The **pet-hair extra is seeded inactive**: *Increased* covers a home with pets, and keeping both would
charge a pet owner twice for the same effort. The deep-cleaning services stay as scope, and the level
applies on top of them.

### D7 — What is not built

- **The on-site top-up** (decision 36, approved in principle): the cleaner finds a dirtier home than
  booked and the present customer approves and pays the difference — a 15-minute answer window, e-mail
  and push only, pay raised by D4's rule, a separate document, and never a re-crew, so a one-cleaner
  cash job stays one cleaner. It waits for the lawyer's answer to PR-10 and ships after launch (plan
  phase 5). Until then the level is fixed at booking.
- **Extra work on site** (decision 37): not at launch. The cleaner does the booked scope; anything else
  is a new order.
- **Supplies** (decision 38) needed no code here: the cleaner always brings them, included in the
  price, and the customer copy was reworded in phase 1.

## Consequences

- **A single 120-minute service at a raised level needs two cleaners and is card-only.** With the seeded
  catalogue that is a lone *General Cleaning*. This is the trade decision 32 accepted for honest time.
- **Size scaling waits for data, not code.** When the real per-room minutes are entered, quotes, crews
  and cash eligibility change for new bookings. An order stores its `EstimatedTime` and
  `RequiredEmployees` at booking, so a catalogue edit never re-crews one already made.
- **An empty seat's share is nobody's.** The divisor is `RequiredEmployees`, not the size of the crew
  that turned up, so a cleaner who works a two-seat job alone is paid one seat.
- **A rate now pays the job, not each cleaner.** A seat on a two-cleaner job earns half of what the
  same rate paid each cleaner before; the neutral rate templates (0.5 / 0.6 / 0.7 of list price)
  describe the job like every other rate.
- **A package and a service it includes still count twice** — for the per-room minutes as for the
  service minutes and the price; the shipped rule is preserved, not fixed.
- **The schema and the wire moved.** Two `Orders` columns, `Services.MinutesPerRoom`,
  `RecurringBookingTemplates.DirtinessLevel` and `OrderEmployeePays.DirtinessPay`, folded into the
  regenerated `Initial` migration; the three web clients were regenerated and both mobile specs
  re-dumped.

## Alternatives considered

Each is the option the owner did not take, with the reason the plan gave for it where it gave one.

- **Apply the rate to services and packages only, or after the discounts** (28 (b)): every price
  display would need a second composition rule.
- **A per-market admin setting snapshotted on the order** (29 (b)): not until a market needs other
  rates.
- **A served catalogue of the descriptions, snapshotted on the order** (30 (b)): the move to make if
  PR-10 makes the top-up depend on the exact wording.
- **Leave the time alone** (32 (a)): crew and cash would stay as they were, but the page-4 promise of
  enough time would be false.
- **Keep the pay, or pay a fixed bonus** (33 (b), (c)): under (b) cleaners skip dirty jobs.
- **A recurring schedule always at Normal** (34 (b)), with every mismatch going through the top-up.
- **Keep the pet-hair extra** (35 (a)): a pet owner pays twice. **Exempt deep services from the
  level** (35 (c)).
- **No top-up; the level is fixed at booking for good** (36 (b)).
- **Extra work through the top-up** (37 (b)).
- **Area, effort or hourly pricing** (39 (b)).
- **Keep paying every cleaner the whole job** (40 (a)), which pays a two-seat job twice against one
  price; **or raise the customer price with the crew** (40 (c)).

## Amended 2026-10-03 — the rates are +15 % and +30 % {#amended-2026-10-03}

> **Recorded, not rewritten.** No line of the decision above is edited; where this ruling departs from
> one, the table below says which and how.

**The ruling (owner, 2026-10-03).** *Increased* adds **15 %** and *Heavy* **30 %**, not 30 % and 60 %.
The one-rate rule stands: the level's rate still moves the price, the booked minutes and the cleaner's
pay together.

| Where | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| D2 — `BookingPolicy.IncreasedDirtinessSurchargeRate` / `HeavyDirtinessSurchargeRate` | `0.30` / `0.60` | `0.15` / `0.30` |
| D2 — Heavy + express | × 1.92 | × 1.56 |
| D3 — booked minutes | × 1.3 / × 1.6 | × 1.15 / × 1.3 |
| D4 — the dirtiness term on pay | 30 % / 60 % of the clamped job pay | 15 % / 30 % |
| The copy that states the rate | +30 % / +60 % | +15 % / +30 % — the web mirror in `booking-window.models.ts`, the Android and iOS level chips and surcharge lines in five locales; `check-booking-policy-parity.mjs` pins each to the constants |
| The customer terms | the 2026-09-30 version states +30 % / +60 % | a new version, effective **2026-10-03**, states +15 % / +30 %. A published version is immutable once in force, so the wording change is a new date; 2026-09-30 stays as accepted |

**What a booked order keeps.** The order stores its surcharge, its minutes and its crew when it is
booked, so an order booked before the change keeps all three. Its **pay does not**: `CalculateOrderPay`
reads the rate from `BookingPolicy` when the job is paid, not from the order, so a job booked at the old
rate and paid after the change pays the dirtiness term at the new one. No production order exists, so
nothing is affected today; if a rate ever changes with live orders, the rate belongs on the order.
