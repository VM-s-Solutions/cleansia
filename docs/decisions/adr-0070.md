# ADR-0070 — A cash booking is guaranteed by a card saved at the first one; a fee or debt a booking leaves behind is a receivable, paid through the customer's pay link or — once the terms allow it — charged to that card; and the crew shares a fee only once it is collected

> **One decision:** *a customer who pays cash has first saved a card, under a versioned consent, that
> the fees a cash booking can leave behind may be charged to; each such fee is a receivable that refuses
> them cash until it is settled; it is settled through a pay link the customer opens — and, once the
> lawyer's consent wording is in the terms, by a charge to that card; and the cleaners who lost the job
> are paid half of a fee only when the company has it.*

## Context

A card booking is paid before anyone moves, so a late cancellation keeps its fee out of the refund.
A cash booking was paid at the door or not at all. Its late-cancellation fee was recorded on the order
and collected by nothing; a customer could cancel cash bookings late without limit, and a customer who
did not open the door owed nothing a system could claim. The meeting of 2026-09-27 introduced a 100 %
lockout fee and a cleaner's share of fees, both of which assume the fee is collected — which, for cash,
nothing could do.

## Decision

### D1 — A saved card is the guarantee for cash, captured once (decision 16 (a))

A signed-in customer books cash only while they hold a **usable card saved in the booking's currency**
— captured, not removed, not past the end of its expiry month (`SavedCard.IsUsableOn`). It is captured
**at the first cash booking**, never at registration, and **nothing is charged**: a setup-mode Stripe
Checkout Session on the web, a card-only SetupIntent in PaymentSheet on the apps, on the customer's
Stripe Customer for the market's currency — the one Cleansia Plus also bills. The card lands when Stripe
reports the setup; a later capture retires the earlier card in the currency.

**The consent is evidence on the card's row.** The customer ticks that a late-cancellation fee, a
lockout fee, unpaid cash and an approved top-up may be charged to the card (decision 17); the row
records the wording's version, the IP and the device before the capture starts. The version is a
constant, `SavedCard.ConsentTextVersionInForce = "card-guarantee-draft-2026-09-28"`, a draft that is
bumped with every change to the wording, so each card records the text it was saved under.

**Removal deactivates the row and leaves the card at Stripe**, because the same Stripe Customer renews
Plus; the platform charges only the card its own active row names. Erasure deletes the rows with the
Stripe Customers they hang on.

### D2 — Cash is refused on the customer's standing, in a fixed order (decisions 18 (a), 24)

After the one-cleaner rule, `CreateOrder`, `CreateRecurringBooking`, `UpdateRecurringBooking` (in their
validators) and `ConfirmRecurringOrder` (in its handler) refuse cash, in this order, when the customer:

1. owes any operating company an open receivable — `order.cash_unpaid_receivable`;
2. holds `BookingPolicy.MaxOpenUnpaidCashBookings` (**2**) open unpaid cash bookings, counted across
   companies, a recurring occurrence from its confirmation — `order.cash_open_bookings_limit_reached`;
3. holds no usable card in the booking's currency — `order.cash_requires_saved_card`.

The server asks about the debt and the limit before the card, and the web and Android ask `CreateOrder`
first, so there a customer who could not book cash anyway is not asked to save a card for it. iOS reads
the saved cards before it books, so a customer with a debt or at the limit and no usable card is taken
through the capture and then refused (see the Consequences). **There is no amount ceiling**: the
one-cleaner rule already bounds the price. Card bookings need none of it — they are prepaid — and
guests were already refused cash.

### D3 — What a customer owes is a receivable (decision 17)

`Receivables`: order, customer, currency, kind, amount, status (`Open`, `Paid`, `WrittenOff`) and the
charges tried; the company's books, kept pseudonymous by an erasure. Two writers open one today:

| Kind | Writer |
|---|---|
| `CashCancellationFee` | a signed-in customer's own late cancellation of a cash booking that took no payment — the assessed fee |
| `Lockout` | an administrator's lockout confirmation on a signed-in customer's cash booking that took no payment — the whole price (D6) |

`UnpaidCash` and `TopUp` are declared for decision 17 and **opened by nothing yet**; the top-up waits for
the on-site top-up (ADR-0069 D7). A receivable never touches the order's payment status, charge surface
or refunds. Administrators list the company's receivables (any administrator) and write an open one off
with a note (Manager and above, audited, sensitive).

### D4 — A receivable is paid through a pay link now, and charged to the card only when the terms allow it (decisions 16, 18 (a))

**The pay link works always.** The customer lists what they owe across companies and opens a
payment-mode Checkout Session for one (`Receivable/GetMine`, `Receivable/CreatePayLink/{id}`), which
returns to the order's page. The session names the receivable and never an order, so the webhook's
order path cannot mistake the fee for the booking's sale. The link is recorded on the receivable and
handed back while Stripe has it open.

**The off-session charge is built and dark.** `ChargeOpenReceivables` (every 15 minutes) charges each
open receivable once to the usable card in its currency — attempt committed before the call, company by
company, a frozen company left out, a held pay link closed first and one already paid not charged — and
**does nothing unless `Payments:OffSessionChargesEnabled` is true**. The switch is false in code and
absent from the Functions `appsettings.json`, where a committed value would beat the Azure setting. A
declined or unauthenticated charge e-mails a pay link. **It is switched on when the phase-4 terms carry
the lawyer's consent wording, and not before** — the plan's own item advised building nothing until
then; the owner took the recommendation to build early and keep it dark.

**A receivable is paid once.** The webhook settles it on the pay link's completion or the charge's
success, under the receivable's company; a second payment is refunded in full under a deterministic
key; a written-off receivable paid anyway is paid.

### D5 — A paid fee gets a receipt of its own

An order holds one **sale** receipt and one **fee** receipt per receivable paid on it, numbered from
the operator's same counter, stating the fee alone on the document and on the fiscal request, and not
e-mailed. The download, the reconciliation sweep, the archive's count of orders awaiting a receipt and
every refund path read the sale only; the archive's count of receipts awaiting fiscal registration reads
every receipt, so a fee receipt still to be registered holds the archive like a sale receipt. A fee is a
sale in its own right; folding it into the booking's receipt would restate a document already issued,
which [the cash-receipt ruling](/flows/payment-and-fiscal#cash-receipt-restated) removed.

### D6 — A lockout is the customer's cancellation at the whole price, confirmed by a person (decisions 11, 13)

The assigned cleaner reports *cannot get in* from `BookingPolicy.LockoutWaitMinutes` (**15**) past the
start, with an entrance photo and a note of the calls made; the administrators are told
(`admin.order.lockout_reported`) and nothing moves. An administrator confirms it
(`AdminOrder/cancel-lockout`): cancelled by the administrator at `BookingPolicy.LockoutFeeRate`
(**100 %**), no refund, reason `order.cancelled.customer_lockout`. A paid card booking keeps its payment
and the credit applied; a signed-in customer's unpaid cash booking returns the credit and owes the whole
price as a `Lockout` receivable; **a guest keeps nothing back and is never charged beyond the
prepayment** (13 (a)). No timer confirms a lockout for the same reason none confirms a cleaner's
no-show: a missing tap is not evidence.

### D7 — The crew is paid half of a fee, only once it is collected (decision 12 (b))

`BookingPolicy.CleanerFeeShareRate = 0.50` of the fee the company **collected** — what a card payment
kept, never more than the company still holds after refunds, or the order's paid cash-cancellation and
lockout receivables — split across `RequiredEmployees` with the residue on the first seat, as job pay is
(ADR-0069 D4). The row is its own pay-line type (`PayLineType`: `Job`, `CancellationFeeShare`,
`LockoutFeeShare`, append-only) and its invoice line says so. A fee still owed pays nothing; a cancelled
order that collected nothing is refused (`payroll.no_collected_fee`). Decision 12 (b) is *subject to
PR-1 Q6*: the lawyer may still move the figure, which is one constant.

### D8 — What is not built

- **No receivable for unpaid cash or a top-up.** The kinds exist for the consent and the receipt
  labels; nothing writes them.
- **No collection beyond the pay link and the card.** No agency, no automatic write-off, no refusal of
  card bookings (18 (b)–(d)).
- **The consent is not yet a stored legal document.** It is a version string on the card's row; the
  lawyer's wording arrives with the phase-4 terms, and the switch in D4 waits for it.
- **One of two cleaners missing, and a cleaner who never came,** stay the dispute and the no-show paths
  of phase 1; a lockout is only the customer's side.

## Consequences

- **The first cash booking is one step longer.** The customer saves a card before cash is accepted.
  The web and Android ask `CreateOrder` first and capture only on its card refusal; iOS reads the saved
  cards before it books and captures when none is usable, and also offers *Save a card* under Payments.
- **Until the terms change, a receivable is paid only if the customer pays it.** The block on cash is
  the whole of the pressure; a customer who never books cash again is never asked for more than the
  pay link and its e-mail. An administrator's write-off is the release valve.
- **A customer who owes one company cannot book cash with another.** The debt and the limit are
  counted across the holding, as the account is one identity across it.
- **The fee receipt is issued to a cancelled order.** A cancelled cash booking never gets a sale
  receipt; its paid fee gets a fee receipt. A cancelled order is not counted as awaiting a receipt; a
  fee receipt awaiting fiscal registration holds the archive like any other.
- **The customer apps do not render the lockout reason yet.** The parity checker carries
  `order.cancelled.customer_lockout` as declared and not rendered; the cancellation e-mail states it.
- **The schema and the wire moved.** `SavedCards`, `Receivables`, `OrderReceipts.ReceivableId`, three
  `Orders` lockout columns and `OrderEmployeePays.LineType` were folded into the regenerated `Initial`
  migration; the three web clients were regenerated and both mobile specs re-dumped.

## Alternatives considered

Each is the option the owner did not take, with the reason the plan gave where it gave one.

- **Cash only for customers who already hold a card from a mobile payment or Plus** (16 (b)), **a card
  only after a first lockout or non-payment** (16 (c)), or **no card, and cash fees stay uncollectable**
  (16 (d)).
- **Build nothing before the lawyer's consent wording** — the verified item's own advice. Taken in part:
  the charge is built, and switched off.
- **Refuse every new booking on a failed charge** (18 (b)), **write the fee off** (18 (c)), or **send
  it to a collection agency above an amount** (18 (d)).
- **An amount cap per customer** (24): the one-cleaner rule already bounds the price.
- **An automatic lockout when the customer does not answer** (11 (b)).
- **The cleaner gets nothing** (12 (a)), or **full job pay even when nothing is collected** (12 (c)).
- **Refund a guest's prepayment on a lockout** (13 (b)).

## Amended 2026-10-01 — a card may be saved while paying by card, and only a ticked one is kept {#amended-2026-10-01}

> **Recorded, not rewritten.** No line of the decision above is edited; where this ruling departs from
> one, the table below says which and how.

**The ruling (owner decisions 2026-10-01).**

- **The saved cards stay.** The list and *Remove* stay on the customer web, Android and iOS.
- **A signed-in customer paying by card may tick *Save this card for my next bookings*, off by
  default.** Ticked, Stripe keeps the card for off-session use (`setup_future_usage=off_session`), the
  consent is recorded on a `SavedCards` row as D1's capture records it, and once the payment succeeds the
  card is listed and is the guarantee for cash bookings in that currency.
- **Unticked, no card is kept — on the mobile apps too.** Until this ruling every PaymentIntent the
  apps opened, a booking's and a recurring occurrence's alike, asked Stripe for
  `setup_future_usage=off_session` unconditionally, so every card paid in the apps was kept on the
  Stripe Customer silently, with no `SavedCards` row and no consent. Now only a ticked intent asks for
  it. An unticked app intent is still opened on the account's own Stripe Customer, created when the
  account has none, and Stripe records the payment against it without attaching the card. The booking's
  sheet is opened without that Customer and its ephemeral key, because a sheet given them draws
  Stripe's own save box on an intent that keeps nothing, and a card saved through that box would stay
  on the Customer with no row and no consent.
- **A guest never sees the tick.** A guest's `saveCard: true` is refused `saved_card.requires_account`.

**What it changes here:**

| Text | Since 2026-10-01 |
|---|---|
| D1: *captured **at the first cash booking**, never at registration* | **Departed from.** A card is also saved at a card booking whose payer ticks the box. Still never at registration, and a cash booking still asks for a card whenever the customer holds no usable one in its currency — which a ticked card, while usable, answers. |
| D1: *nothing is charged* | **Holds for the cash capture.** A card saved while paying is the card the booking was charged to; saving it charges nothing more. |
| D1: *a setup-mode Stripe Checkout Session on the web, a card-only SetupIntent in PaymentSheet on the apps* | **Holds for the cash capture.** The tick rides the booking's own charge surface: the web's Checkout Session (`CreateOrder`'s `saveCard`) and the apps' PaymentIntent (`CreatePaymentIntent`'s `saveCard`) are opened on the customer's Stripe Customer for the booking's currency and carry `SavedCardId`; the webhook lands the card from whichever of `checkout.session.completed` and `payment_intent.succeeded` arrives first. An app order is then settled by its intent's event; a web order only by the session's, because the PaymentIntent behind a web session carries `SavedCardId` but no `OrderId`. |
| D1: *the consent is evidence on the card's row* | **Holds.** The tick is printed with the same card-guarantee sentence, `card-guarantee-draft-2026-09-28`, and the row records the version, the IP and the device before the redirect or the sheet. There is no second box: ticking is the assent. |
| D1: *removal deactivates the row and leaves the card at Stripe* | **Holds**, whichever way the card was saved. |
| Consequences: *the first cash booking is one step longer* | **Holds for any cash booking made while the customer holds no usable card in its currency** — the first, or one after the card was removed or ran past its expiry month. A customer who ticked the box on an earlier card booking in that currency and still holds that card usable is asked for nothing. |

**What stands:** D2–D8 — the cash standing and its order of refusals, receivables, the pay link, the
off-session charge (still dark; once on, it charges a ticked card exactly as a captured one), the fee
receipt, the lockout and the crew's share. The saved-cards copy on the three clients was not changed
with the ruling: it still introduces the card as the cash guarantee and, with none, says a cash booking
asks for one, which stays true of a ticked card.

**Built with it:** a card ticked while booking another company's market is recorded, with its Stripe
Customer, in the account's company, while the booking commits in the market's; and re-opening
PaymentSheet hands back the order's open intent for the same amount, Stripe Customer and tick, and
otherwise opens a new one and cancels the old with Stripe's reason `duplicate`, on which the webhook
leaves the order as it is.

**A recurring occurrence confirmed by card — as built, not ruled.** The ruling names a booking paid by
card and says nothing of a recurring occurrence. What follows is the current implementation, not an
owner decision. `ConfirmRecurringOrder` takes no `saveCard`, and the payment it opens keeps no card.
Android and iOS offer the booking's tick above *Confirm and pay*, off by default, and after the confirm
take the sheet's intent from `CreatePaymentIntent` with it: unticked, that hands back the confirm's own
intent and the sheet opens without the Customer; ticked, it records the row with the consent, replaces
the intent with a card-saving one, cancels the old one as `duplicate`, and the sheet opens on the
Customer — so a ticked occurrence keeps its card as a ticked booking does. The web offers no tick
there: the occurrence is paid through the Checkout Session `ConfirmRecurringOrder` opens on no Stripe
Customer, and no card is kept.

**Open item — the web's occurrence confirmation.** Whether it should offer the tick, as both apps now
do, is not ruled. Until it is, a web customer cannot keep the card they pay an occurrence with.
