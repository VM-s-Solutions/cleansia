> **Three decisions:** *the set read that answers "which of these cleaners is busy in this window" reads
> every company's orders, because a commitment booked under any operator occupies the same cleaner — so it is

> method of its form whose every caller needs every company's rows is one method whose name still says so, and
> names `IsFirstBookingAsync` as an open instance (D2); and ADR-0039's compliance step 2 is restated to match
> (D3).*

## Context (citations at `aef59372a`, with this change's own lines where it moved them)

### The read and who reaches it

`OrderRepository.GetBusyEmployeeIdsInWindowAsync` read `LiveCommitmentsInWindow(GetQueryableIgnoringTenant(),
…)` (`OrderRepository.cs:416`). It has since `7751c5fa9` (2026-09-16), when customer bookings across market
operators shipped: a booking carries the operator of its address's market, so a read scoped to the caller's
claim would call a cleaner free who is booked under another company. Its contract says so, and states what the
callers owe: every employee id must be server-derived (`IOrderRepository.cs:166-174`).

Request paths and background jobs both reach it:

- **Request paths:** the preferred-cleaner picker (`GetMyServingCleaners.cs:158`), and the hold resolver
  (`PreferredCleanerHoldResolver.cs:108`) through `OrderFactory.cs:316` from `CreateOrder` (`CreateOrder.cs:1109`),
  through `ChoosePreferredCleaner.cs:148`, and through `PreferredOfferNotifier.cs:72` from
  `ConfirmRecurringOrder.cs:278-279`.
- **Jobs:** the Stripe payment webhook, `HandlePaymentNotification.cs:426-427` → `PreferredOfferNotifier.cs:72`,
  after the webhook's tenant override at `:270`; and the recurring materializer,
  `MaterializeRecurringBookingTemplate.cs:335` → `OrderFactory.cs:316`.

### What the name said, and what ADR-0039 says

The name sounded scoped. S8's third form says that a repository method reachable from both a request path and a
background job must not pick its own tenancy: name the two variants and let the call site say which world it is
in (`security-rules.md:712-714`). This method picked its own — the right one, for every caller — under a name
that did not say so. That is the autocomplete trap T-0815 deleted a method to close: a tenant-escaping read one
completion entry away from a customer-facing handler.

ADR-0039 ruled the set read tenant-scoped (D3.2's end state, `adr-0039.md:476-480`), and its compliance step 2
says the picker and the resolver call scoped methods and that there is no ignoring variant of the set method
(`adr-0039.md:1276-1281`). The 2026-09-16 departure was recorded only as a parenthesis in a living doc
(`agents/architecture/decisions/preferred-cleaner-dispatch.md:277-283`). Once the name says `IgnoringTenant`, step
2 applied as written flags both callers. The sanctioned way to retire a step of an accepted record is a record
that supersedes it.

### The roster is not an option

The ticket offered a second route: add the method to the roster that S8's repository-wide tier names
(`security-rules.md:652`). That roster does not exist — S8 says writing it is the ticket (`:652-655`) — and the
ticket it names was never filed in the current backlog (`agents/backlog/README.md:26-28`). Built, it would still
be a list someone has to read; S8's third form closes the trap at the call site, by the name.

## Decision

### D1 — One method, and its name says it reads every company

`GetBusyEmployeeIdsInWindowAsync` is renamed `GetBusyEmployeeIdsInWindowIgnoringTenantAsync`: the interface
(`IOrderRepository.cs:176`), the implementation (`OrderRepository.cs:400`) and both callers
(`GetMyServingCleaners.cs:158`, `PreferredCleanerHoldResolver.cs:108`). Behaviour does not change, and the
contract text at `IOrderRepository.cs:166-174` stands word for word.

- **Exactly one method, no scoped sibling.** Every caller needs every company's calendar, because a commitment
  under any operator occupies the same cleaner. A scoped variant would have no correct caller, and ADR-0039
  D3.2's rule against building a method with zero callers stands.
- **What the callers owe is unchanged:** every id must be server-derived — the caller's own completed orders
  (the picker) or a preferred cleaner the booking validator has already proven (the resolver).

### D2 — S8 says what a method of the third form is when every caller needs every company's rows

S8's third form gains one paragraph after its rule: when every caller of a method of this form needs every
company's rows, there is one method and no scoped variant, and its name still carries `IgnoringTenant`, so
each call site says which world it reads. Its reference is the method of D1.

- **It reaches only the third form's own subject:** a method reached from both a request path and a background
  job. A read that one world alone reaches is governed by the matrix and its re-pin, not by its name, so the
  re-pinned bypass names S8 already allows (`security-rules.md:641-643`, `:671-673`) and `GetQueryableForOwner`
  (`OrderRepository.cs:98-102`) are untouched.
- **Its tier is `T3-HUMAN`.** Whether every caller needs every company's rows is a reading of meaning that no
  machine decides. The named item is S8's own caller test (*for every repository method, list its callers*),
  which the security review walks on every `security_touching` ticket (`.claude/agents/security.md`, item 8);
  that test gains one clause, so that it asks the question the paragraph states. The paragraph cites no gate
  ticket.
- **One open instance is named in S8, not fixed here.** `IOrderRepository.IsFirstBookingAsync` reads every
  company by contract (`IOrderRepository.cs:10-18`, `OrderRepository.cs:20`) under a name that does not say so,
  and is reached from request paths through `CancellationPolicyResolver.cs:43` and from a Function through
  `SendEmailHandler.cs:290`. Read literally, S8's existing rule (`security-rules.md:712-714`) already makes it a
  violation; read by its header's purpose (*opposite tenancy requirements*, `:705`), the new paragraph newly
  covers it. Either way the action is the same: S8 carries a ✗ line naming it, and T-0837 renames it and sweeps
  the remaining repository files for the same shape; T-0837's acceptance criteria remove the ✗ line.

### D3 — ADR-0039's compliance step 2, restated

Step 2 now reads:

> **Every `*IgnoringTenant*` call site is on a sweep or is a call of
> `GetBusyEmployeeIdsInWindowIgnoringTenantAsync`** (the picker, the hold resolver, and through it the payment
> webhook and the recurring materializer); `TakeOrder` and `AdminReassignOrder` call the scoped
> `HasOverlappingOrderAsync`; the digest calls `GetOverlappedCandidateIdsIgnoringTenantAsync`; there is exactly
> one set method, and a scoped sibling with no caller is a finding. *Retired once ADR-0039's P4 architecture
> test lands, whose allowlist then carries `GetMyServingCleaners.cs`.*

## Challenge

The author's draft renamed the method, added one sentence to S8 (*"a method used by only one world still names
that world"*), and superseded only D3.2's tenancy and the scoped-call clause of step 2. The challenger
sustained the rename and raised four objections:

- **2a** — The drafted sentence reached far beyond S8's third form. Read broadly, it contradicted the
  re-pinned bypass names S8 itself allows (`security-rules.md:641-643`, `:671-673`) and `GetQueryableForOwner`
  (`OrderRepository.cs:98-102`), and it covered `IsFirstBookingAsync`, which reads every company and is reached
  from request paths and from a Function.
- **2b** — The sentence carves an exception out of *"name the two variants"*, so it needs an enforcement tier,
  and the repository-wide roster's pending-gate ticket is not available: it was never filed.
- **2c** — The supersession was scoped too narrowly. After the rename, step 2's headline (*"every
  `*IgnoringTenant*` call site is on a sweep"*) and *"there is no ignoring variant of the SET method"* also
  become false for the picker and the resolver.
- **2d** — The verification grep contradicted the edit list: it would still find the dated note in the
  hold-resolver card and the historical A1 plan row, and the dispatch notes' `:269` carries no method name (the
  hits are `:225`, `:283` and `:807`).

**After acceptance and before merge, the implementation review raised item 8 (2026-10-13).** The supersession was
still too narrow, the way 2c found it: D6's caller table (`adr-0039.md:816-818`) still says the picker and the hold
resolver call the scoped variant, and its materializer row forbids the ignoring one, which D1 contradicts. Name these
rows too before this record merges, or say why not.

## Defense

- **2a conceded.** The paragraph is narrowed to the third form's subject — a method reached from both a request
  path and a background job — and says that a read reached from one world alone is governed by the matrix and
  its re-pin, not by its name. Both readings of whether S8's existing rule already governs `IsFirstBookingAsync`
  are recorded (D2); either way it is named in S8 as an open instance and ticketed as T-0837, not renamed here.
  The sweep covered all eleven bypass sites in `OrderRepository.cs`: one instance (`IsFirstBookingAsync`);
  `GetQueryableForOwner` names its pin; the named `IgnoringTenant` reads comply;
  `GetEmployeeIdsCurrentlyOnAJobAsync` and `GetReceiptReconciliationCandidatesAsync` are one-world sweeps,
  outside the subject; `UserRepository` and `EmployeeRepository` are confined by their roster tests; the other
  34 repository files with a bypass are T-0837's first task.
- **2b conceded, and resolved with neither option offered.** The paragraph is a law a machine cannot check, so
  its honest tier is `T3-HUMAN` with a named checklist item: S8's own caller test, which gains one clause. The
  new text cites no gate ticket.
- **2c conceded.** This record supersedes the whole of step 2 and D3.2's end-state tenancy, and restates step 2
  (D3). D3.2's rule against a method with zero callers is kept.
- **2d conceded.** The grep lists its two expected hits, the edit list drops `:269`, and the historical row
  `:807` is left as written.
- **Provenance, checked by the lead:** the draft cited `HandlePaymentNotification.cs:132` →
  `PreferredOfferNotifier.cs:72`. Line `:132` is the constructor injection; the call is at `:426-427`, after the
  tenant override at `:270`.
- **Item 8:** not defended; §Verdict.

## Verdict

**Consensus.** The rename is sustained, and S8's sentence, the supersession and the verification grep are amended
per 2a–2d. Accepted on 2026-10-12, in the commit that ships S8's text.

**Item 8 — the challenge stands, and the supersession is completed (2026-10-13).** Nothing defends the rows: they
rule the variant D1 replaced, and the materializer row's *"Do not reach for the ignoring variant here"*, applied as
written, flags the resolver's call now that the name says `IgnoringTenant`. That is 2c's finding about step 2, in
another section. So that the list is closed rather than grown one review at a time, the lead read every passage of
ADR-0039 that states the set read's tenancy. Three more rule the same thing, and §Supersedes now lists them: D3's
specified doc comment (`adr-0039.md:294-296`), D9's note on the materializer (`:1032-1033`), and D6's rule read
literally, whose *"name the two variants"* asks for the scoped sibling that D3 above makes a finding. The rest is
outside this record's subject or stays as the record of its time:

- the one-employee reads: the Context's Defect 1 (`:135-147`), D3.2's `TakeOrder` and digest lines (`:476-477`),
  and D6's digest row (`:819`), whose ruling that the digest reads every company still holds;
- D6's architecture-test ruling (`:847-854`) stands, and its allow-list takes `GetMyServingCleaners.cs`
  (§Residuals);
- D10's struck A0 (`:1043`) and CH-D7 (`:1604-1617`) are the panel's reasoning on the record.

D1, D2 and D3 are unchanged: this completes the list of what they replaced. ADR-0039 is not edited; its dated
pointer names D3.2's end state and step 2 and links here (`agents/knowledge/consistency.md:758-762`).
