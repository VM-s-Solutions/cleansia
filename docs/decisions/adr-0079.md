# ADR-0079 — Pre-take location: a Prague district from the postcode, and a distance band from home measured to a snapped cell

- **Status:** `accepted` — both decisions were built on 2026-10-31. Mike acknowledged the pre-take
  location ceiling stated under [Consequences](#owner-acknowledgement) on 2026-11-06.
- **Date:** 2026-10-31
- **Decided by:** Mike's rulings of 2026-10-31 — a Prague job names its district, read from the
  postcode, with an approximate district accepted (ruling 1); the Available tab shows a distance band
  from the cleaner's home (ruling 5c). His words, 2026-10-31, answering "show districts, or keep
  what the API sends?": *"Prague 4 is nice"*; and, of the reported findings that included the
  distance band from the cleaner's home: *"Also fix the findings"*. The band edges and the cell size
  are the build's defaults for the questions put to him with them; his acknowledgement of the
  ceiling below was given on 2026-11-06: *"Approve the current approximate-location behavior"*.
- **Supersedes:** —
- **Amends by reference:** [ADR-0068](./adr-0068) — a contract for work accepted from 2026-10-31
  freezes the district label in its facts ([§Amended 2026-10-31](./adr-0068#amended-2026-10-31)).
- **Applies to:** `OrderMappers.BuildApproximateAddress`, `JobProximity`, `HomeDistanceBand`,
  `OrderListItem`, `GetPagedOrders`; the Available tab of the partner Android and iOS apps.
- **Ticket:** T-0903.

## Context

Before a take, a cleaner is told where a job is by one string, `customerAddressApproximate`, which one
server function builds for every pre-take surface — the board, the job detail, the pending offers, the
dashboard's preview — and for the contract-for-work facts, which freeze it. It read the city and the
postcode's first three digits (`Praha · 140 xx`). Its own remarks call it the single pre-acceptance
ceiling, and say that a second spelling is how the ceiling stops being one decision. No DTO carried a
distance. Both partner apps measured one from the phone's location to the job's coordinates, which are
withheld before the take, so on the Available tab it never rendered.

Mike ruled that a Prague job names its district, `Praha 4`, with an approximate district accepted, and
that the Available tab shows how far a job is from home. Two facts shape how:

- **Every Prague postcode is five digits from 100 00 to 199 99, and no other Czech postcode starts with
  1** (owner decision 2026-10-25, [Serviced cities](/product/business-rules#serviced-city)). The
  second digit is the historic postal district. That mapping is not checked against an authoritative
  source — Háje, `149 00`, is administratively Praha 11 and reads Praha 4 — which is what "approximate"
  accepts.
- **A cleaner's home is not server truth about where they live.** The mobile `UpdateAddressInfo` stores
  the coordinates the client sends, as often as the `auth` rate-limit window allows. A cleaner can move
  their stored home at will and read the board after each move. A crisp band boundary measured to the
  job's exact point answers *"is this job within 3 km of P?"*, and a binary search along three lines
  locates a customer to tens of metres in a few dozen probes — minutes at that rate. Coarse bands alone
  do not help, because the boundary is what leaks.

## Decision

**D1 — The district is computed on the server, inside `BuildApproximateAddress`, with no DTO change.**
The postcode with every whitespace character removed is five ASCII digits starting with `1`, and the
city matches `Praha` or `Prague` by the serviced-city name rule (`CityNameMatch`): the label is
`Praha N`, where N is the second digit and `0` reads `10`. Anything else keeps the earlier body
unchanged. The label is coarser than the three-digit prefix (`Praha 4` covers `140`–`149`), so it
discloses strictly less.

- **D1a.** A contract accepted from 2026-10-31 freezes `Praha N` in its facts and its PDF copy; a stored
  acceptance keeps what it stored.
- **D1b.** An exonym (`Прага`, `Prag`) is not matched by name and keeps the earlier shape; a district
  typed into the city loses to the postcode (`Praha 11` at `149 00` reads `Praha 4`).

**D2 — A distance band from home on `OrderListItem`.** A nullable `HomeDistanceBand` enum, the record's
last positional member, `[SwaggerEnumAsInt]`: `Under3Km = 1`, `From3To6Km = 2`, `From6To10Km = 3`,
`From10To20Km = 4`, `Over20Km = 5`, with edges `< 3`, `3–< 6`, `6–< 10`, `10–< 20`, `≥ 20` km of the
snapped straight-line distance. `GetPagedOrders` reads the caller's home once per request and sets the
band on each row before the browsing redaction. **The set is frozen:** changing a band or adding one
means a new field, never a new value.

- **D2a — Snap, then measure from the stored home.** The job's point moves to the centre of its cell on
  a grid of 0.02° latitude × 0.03° longitude (`JobProximity.SnapToBandCell`), and the haversine distance
  from `Employee.Address` to that centre is banded. Never the phone's location, never a request
  parameter (S1).
- **D2b — Null** for an administrator, for a cleaner with no home coordinates, and for an order with no
  coordinates yet. Clients render nothing for null. The band is computed for every cleaner row and shown
  only on Available, and is kept out of `WorkContractFacts` and out of every log (S6).
- **D2c — Honest wording.** *under 3 km from home*, *3–6 km from home* … *over 20 km from home*, and one
  footnote under the list: the distances are straight-line from the home address, in rough bands. No
  point figure, ever.
- **D2d — The phone's location leaves Orders.** Neither app reads it there any more; the address picker
  keeps its own use, and the iOS location purpose string names only that.

## Consequences

- **D1 needs no regeneration and no phone code.** The string is opaque to every client, so the phones,
  the partner web's contract dialog and the PDF follow at once. The docs' and code comments' examples
  move to `Praha 2`.
- **D2 regenerates the contract.** The three TypeScript clients, both committed mobile specs and the
  iOS models gain the field. `OrderListItem` is one record that the customer hosts serve too, so the
  customer spec gains it as well, always null there; the Android customer app's wire test lists it.
- **The snap closes the probe.** Two job points in one cell give the same band from every origin, so no
  number of moves of the home tells a cleaner more than which cell the job is in. The snap moves the
  point by at most half the cell's diagonal, about 1.54 km, which is why the narrowest band is 3 km and
  the wording says *rough*. No rate limit on address changes is added: more probes gain nothing.
- **The band set is frozen.** Both generated mobile clients reject an unknown enum value and fail the
  whole response — a plain `enum: Int, Codable` on iOS, kotlinx `Json` without `coerceInputValues` on
  Android — so a new value would blank the Available tab on every installed build. The enum's remark
  says so, and adding a field stays the S9-safe change.

### The pre-take location ceiling {#owner-acknowledgement}

**Once the band ships, the effective pre-take location ceiling is the snapped band cell, narrowed by
the label's area: at most about 2.2 × 2.1 km (about 4.8 km²) in Prague, and only part of a cell where
the cell crosses a district or prefix boundary — not the label.** The band and the label are read on
the same row, and the grid is aligned to neither the postal districts nor the three-digit prefix areas,
so the snap bounds what the band tells a cleaner, not what they learn in total: that is the part of the
cell inside the label's area (the postal district for `Praha N`, the prefix area elsewhere). It is
finer than `Praha N`, and it can be finer than the old three-digit postal area wherever that area is
larger than a cell. The claim that the label
and the band together disclose no more than the board did before is **not made**: it would rest on a
size comparison nobody measured. [S4](/architecture/security-rules#pre-take-location) and the
[business rules](/product/business-rules#distance-band) state this ceiling, and the builder's remarks
point to both pre-take rules.

**Owner acknowledgement: accepted, 2026-11-06.** Mike answered *"Approve the current
approximate-location behavior"* after being told that the district plus rough distance bands can
narrow the location to part of an area about 2.2 × 2.1 km in Prague while the exact pin stays hidden.

**Residuals, reported and not fixed here.** The seat-open push still tests a cleaner's exact-coordinate
radius (at least 1 km) against the job's exact point, a weak oracle that fires only when a seat happens
to open. The partner web's Available table shows no location at all, because it reads the redacted
street field. The older builder strips only ASCII spaces and admits letters in the three-character
prefix, which no served market reaches today.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Parse the label on the phones.** Two parsers of a display string, in Kotlin and Swift, while the
  partner web, the PDF and every new contract's facts kept `Praha · 140 xx` — the card would disagree
  with the contract it opens.
- **Project the address's country into every surface** so an ISO gate replaces the city test. About
  five projections and their includes, to fix only the exonym fallback.
- **The distance to the exact point.** The probe above locates a customer in minutes.
- **The phone's location.** A spoofed fix makes probing free, and the job's coordinates are withheld
  before the take anyway.
- **A 1 km cell.** More accurate bands, but each cell narrows to about 1.2 km², finer again than any
  ceiling the platform has had.
- **Keep the field off the customer contract** with a per-host schema filter or a second DTO, for a
  field that is always null there. Not proportionate.

## Verification

`ApproximateAddressTests` pins one case per row of the
[business rules' table](/product/business-rules#approximate-location), with `Praha-západ`, a blank city
and Berlin's `10115` among them; the four integration and unit expectations that read
`Praha · 120 xx` read `Praha 2`. `JobProximityTests` pins the band edges at 2.999 / 3.0 … 20.0, each
null arm, the cell's idempotence, a snapped distance within 1.6 km of the exact one, and the
anti-triangulation property — two points in one cell give the same band from every origin on a 1 km
sweep across ±30 km. `OrderRedactionSurfaceTests` lists the band as kept, with its reason.
`AvailableOrdersHomeDistanceBandTests` checks against real Postgres that a browsing row whose
coordinates are null on the wire carries the expected band, and that no home, no order coordinates and
an administrator each give null. `MobileSpecEnumGuardTests` covers the enum once the specs are re-dumped.
