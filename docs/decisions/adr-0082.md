> **One decision:** *an open pay period lasts at most its configured length, 14 days in production, and closes
> by itself the night after its end date. An administrator may end it today (Close now) or move its end
> (Change end date); nobody reopens a closed period. Pay that arrives after a period was invoiced goes into the
> next open period. A closed period is invoiced once, by the platform. The company pays the invoice by bank
> transfer and an administrator marks it paid; Stripe takes customers' card payments and never pays a cleaner.
> No cleaner app shows a payout date.*

---

## Context

**Three defects shared one cause.** On DEV a period created by hand for a future date captured current pay;
a period closed early was invoiced by the next night run or by nothing; and a cleaner's invoice disappeared
from an iPhone. The investigation found that the platform let an invoice be issued before its period was
closed, reopened a period after its invoices existed, and left a cancelled invoice's jobs stranded. Each was
reachable because a closed period could become open again and because "open" did not mean "one".

**Both cleaner agreements promise an invoice after each pay period of 14 days.** The framework contract
(§ 10) and the self-billing agreement (§ 3) say a pay period is 14 days. The invoice is due 14 days after it is
issued. Neither agreement promises a payout date, and neither names Stripe.

**The platform never moved the money.** An admin keys the transfer in the company's bank and marks the invoice
paid. Decision 48 (2026-09-27) planned to replace that with Stripe Connect Express after the lawyers' answer;
nothing of it was built.

---

## Decision

### D1 — R1: a period closes by itself; early close is an exception; no period is reopened

1. **A period lasts at most the configured number of days**, start and end inclusive. The setting is
   `PayPeriodClosing:LengthInDays`, default 14 (`PayPeriod.LengthInDays`, read through
   `IPayPeriodClosingConfig`). **Only the DEV template sets it, to 3**, so testers see a whole pay cycle within
   days (Mike's ruling of 2026-11-09, D39). Production keeps 14 because both agreements promise it. The invoice
   stays due 14 days after issue everywhere.
2. **The night run closes a period the night after its end date.** `CloseExpiredPayPeriods` (02:00 UTC) closes
   every open period whose end date is before today, commits the close and the successor period first, and
   then invoices each cleaner who has pay in it. A cleaner it cannot invoice is picked up by the safety net
   (the reconciliation sweep of [ADR-0002](./adr-0002) D3.4), about 15 to 20 minutes later.
3. **Close now** ends the open period today (UTC) unless it already ended earlier, requires a reason of up to
   500 characters that is kept in the audit log with the old and new end, records the administrator as the
   closer, and opens the next period from the day after the new end in the same commit. The invoices follow from
   the safety net. A period that has not started cannot be closed.
4. **Change end date** moves only the end, to any day from tomorrow to the period's last allowed day: its start
   plus the configured length minus one. The start stays fixed, because pay joins the open period whatever its
   dates, so moving the start would change only the dates printed on the invoice. To end a period today, use
   Close now.
5. **A closed period is never reopened.** Invoices are issued once a period closes, so reopening one could only
   strand pay again. A mistaken close loses nothing: its jobs are invoiced sooner.
6. **There is one open period per company, and it never starts in the future.** Create works only when no period
   is open and refuses a start after today. The one exception is the period Close now opens for tomorrow. The
   database holds the rule as a unique index over the open status, `NULLS NOT DISTINCT`.
7. **Mark period as paid stays a button, guarded**: the period is closed, every pay row in it is invoiced and
   every invoice in it is paid or cancelled.
8. Both actions keep the Accountant-and-above permission the old Close and Edit had. A Delete button stays out
   of the admin.

### D2 — R2: late pay goes to the next period

Pay that arrives after its period was invoiced (a job finished or a fee collected later) lands in the next open
period and is invoiced with it. A job finished between midnight and the 02:00 UTC run still lands in the period
that has just ended: booking pay by the job's date for that window is not built. Cancelling an invoice frees its
pay rows, and the safety net issues a corrected invoice with a new number within minutes.

### D3 — R3: payouts are manual first, then the bank, and never Stripe

1. **At launch an administrator transfers the money from the company's bank account and marks the invoice
   paid**, with a note. This is the ruling of 2026-08-08 and it is unchanged. The platform never observes the
   payment.
2. **Stripe takes customers' card payments and nothing else.** No Connect account, no transfer and no payout
   webhook is built. Decision 48's Stripe route is withdrawn.
3. **The later step is the bank, not Stripe**: statement matching first, which removes the manual mark and shows
   bounced payments, then a batch upload; if the company does not bank with Fio, a payment-file export replaces
   the upload. Which bank Cleansia CZ uses is open. Nothing of it is built, and it needs a migration of its own
   once production exists.
4. **The ruling holds on the own-name basis.** Cleansia CZ sells the cleaning in its own name and buys each job
   from the cleaner, so paying an invoice is settling the company's own debt and needs no Stripe Connect. The
   lawyers have not confirmed this. If they choose the intermediary model, D3 reopens.

### D4 — R4: "next payout" leaves the cleaner apps

No cleaner app, and no endpoint a cleaner reads, shows a payout date. `NextPayoutDate` and its five-day offset
are removed from the dashboard contract. The pay-period card reads *This period, not invoiced yet* and says
when the period closes, because that is the only date the platform knows. The date returns with automation.

### D5 — the permission map

`CanOpenPayPeriod` and `CanReopenPayPeriod` are deleted from `Policy`, from the physical map and from the admin
host, together with their endpoints. The two rows leave [ADR-0066](./adr-0066) D3's `AccountantOrAbove` line
and [ADR-0001](./adr-0001) D2's table. `CanClosePayPeriod` now guards Close now and `CanUpdatePayPeriod`
guards Change end date.

## Consequences

- **A closed period is final.** An administrator who wants to correct an invoice cancels it, which frees its
  jobs for a corrected invoice, or adjusts its amounts while it is pending or disputed.
- **Nothing in the platform knows a cleaner was paid** except the administrator's mark. "Which cleaners have I
  not paid yet" is answerable from the invoices; "did the money arrive" is not.
- **A shorter DEV period is a DEV fact.** It exercises the whole cycle quickly and must never be read as
  production behaviour. A local run keeps 14 unless a developer sets the variable.
- **A job finished in the 02:00 window is invoiced in the period that just ended.** Mike accepted this.
- **The self-billing wording** *your rewards in that period* does not fit pay that lands in the next period, and
  both agreements say 14 days where an administrator may now end a period early. The lawyers are asked whether
  the texts need *up to 14 days*.
- **The schema moved with the work, not with this record.** The one-open-period index and the other indexes the
  work added are described on [the database page](/architecture/database).

## Alternatives considered

- **Stripe Connect Express (decision 48).** About twice the work of automating the bank (26 to 39 developer-days
  against 12 to 18), reaching a second webhook, all three partner apps, two contracts every cleaner accepts
  again and Stripe's identity checks for every cleaner. It costs about 2,300 to 2,900 CZK a month at 50 payouts
  and 23,000 to 29,000 CZK at 500, where the bank costs nothing, and its payouts come only from the Stripe
  balance, which credit, discounts, refunds and chargebacks shrink. Under own-name sale the ČNB does not force
  it. Rejected.
- **Reopen a closed period.** It is what the old Reopen did, and it is how invoices were issued for a period
  that then took more pay. Rejected: Close now and Change end date cover every reason found for it.
- **A future-dated period.** It was the defect. Rejected: Create refuses it.
- **Book pay by the job's date, not the day it is calculated.** It would close the 02:00 window. Not worth
  building; D2 accepts the window.
- **Keep a Reject action on the invoice.** It was a Cancel that kept the cleaner's cash set-off, and the company
  issues these invoices itself. Removed with this work; the status stays in the enum for rows written before.
- **Show "next payout" as an estimate.** Rejected: the platform cannot know a date it does not control.
